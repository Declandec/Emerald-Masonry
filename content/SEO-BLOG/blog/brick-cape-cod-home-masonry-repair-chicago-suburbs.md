> A Cape Cod's masonry problems almost all start with water: off a short roof edge, around a tall chimney, or into the gap behind a front stoop.

This guide walks through how a brick Cape Cod is built, what fails and why, how it should be repaired, and how to keep it from coming back.

## How a brick Cape Cod is built

The post-war Cape Cod is a simple house: a rectangle, one and a half storeys, a steep gable roof, often with one or two dormers on the front. In the Chicago area, many were built of brick or with a brick front and siding on the dormers and gable ends.

### What matters for masonry

- **Steep roof, short eaves.** The roof sheds water fast, but the overhangs are short, so water off the roof and from overflowing gutters lands close to or on the top of the walls.
- **The chimney.** Many Cape Cods have a chimney on an outside end wall, exposed on three sides and rising high above the roof ridge. Others have one through the middle of the roof.
- **Front stoop.** A small concrete or brick stoop with a few steps up to the front door, often on its own shallower footing.
- **Brick veneer or block backup.** Many were built as brick veneer over a wood frame; some have concrete block behind the brick. This changes how water should drain out of the wall.
- **Mixed materials.** Brick walls meet siding on the dormers and gables, and that's where flashing matters.
- **Simple openings.** Steel angle lintels over most windows and doors, often with limestone or brick sills.

### Cape Cod vs Chicago bungalow

| Feature | Brick Cape Cod | Chicago bungalow |
|---|---|---|
| Era | Mostly 1940s–1950s | Mostly 1910s–1930s |
| Roof | Steep gable, short eaves, dormers | Low hipped roof, deeper eaves |

| Chimney | Often an exterior end-wall stack | Usually interior or side, shorter above roof |

## Causes and warning signs of masonry failure on Cape Cods

### The exterior chimney

The end-wall chimney is the most exposed masonry on the house. Look for:
- **Crumbling mortar** and missing joints, especially near the top.
- A **cracked or missing crown** or no chimney cap.
- **Spalling brick** with faces flaking off.
- **White staining (efflorescence)** down the sides.
- A chimney **pulling away from the house** or leaning.
- **Water stains** on the ceiling or walls next to the chimney inside, often from failed **flashing** where it passes the roof.

Many Cape Cod chimneys also vent a modern furnace or water heater. Flue gases from newer equipment can produce condensation that attacks mortar and old clay liners from the inside, so a chimney inspection should check the liner, not just the outside.

### Wet, worn brick under the eaves

Because the eaves are short, the top few feet of wall under the roof edge get wet in every storm. Signs:
- **Recessed or crumbling mortar joints** in a band under the eaves.
- **Dark staining** or algae on that brick.
- **Spalled brick** in the top courses, especially under gutter seams and downspout outlets.

### Front stoops and steps

- A stoop **tipping** away from or toward the house.
- A **gap** between the stoop and the brick wall.
- **Cracked or crumbling** concrete, or loose brick on brick stoops.
- **Loose railings** where the posts are set in the masonry.
- Brick **damaged at the base of the wall** next to the stoop from salt and splash.

### Rusted lintels over windows and doors

Steel angle lintels on Cape Cods are 70-plus years old. Watch for **rust staining**, **horizontal cracks** along the top of openings, and **stair-step cracks** from the corners. Garage lintels on homes with attached or detached brick garages are often the worst.

### Where dormers meet brick

Dormers and gable ends are often sided. Where a sided wall meets the brick, or where the roof meets the side of the brick wall or chimney, **flashing** keeps water out. Old or missing flashing lets water behind the brick or into the wall, and shows up as stains on upstairs ceilings or damp brick below.

### Foundation and basement

Cape Cods usually have full basements with block or poured foundations. Common issues are **stair-step cracks in block**, leaks at **basement windows and window wells**, failed **parging**, and rusted lintels over basement windows.

## How a professional repairs Cape Cod masonry: step by step

1. **Full walk-around inspection**, including the chimney top, the eave line, every lintel, the stoop and the foundation. We check from the basement too, to see whether the walls are veneer or have block backup.
2. **Prioritize by risk.** A loose chimney top or a stoop pulling away comes before cosmetic mortar work.
3. **Chimney work**: repoint, rebuild the top courses or the full stack above the roof as needed, pour a new **crown** with a drip edge, add a **cap**, and replace **flashing** with the roofer where needed.
4. **Tuckpointing**: grind out the mortar joints to a sufficient depth for the new mortar to bond properly, then repoint with mortar matched in color, strength and joint profile.
5. **Brick replacement** for spalled or cracked units, using matched brick.
6. **Lintel replacement** over windows and doors with new steel, flashing and weep holes.
7. **Stoop repair or rebuild**, with the joint to the house properly detailed and sealed.
8. **Foundation repairs**: repoint block, repair cracks, rebuild basement window openings, and repair parging.
9. **Clean up and walk through the work** with you, with notes on what to watch.

## Materials and techniques for Cape Cod masonry

**Mortar.** Most Cape Cod repointing uses **Type N** mortar, colored to match. Mortar should never be harder than the brick; hard Type S or straight Portland cement mortar on older brick pushes freeze-thaw damage into the brick faces. Chimney tops and stoops, which are more exposed, may call for a different mix, and we choose it for the location.

**Brick matching.** Post-war Chicago-area brick came in a wide range of reds, browns and blends. Replacement brick is matched from current production or salvaged stock, and we show you samples against the wall.

**Crowns and caps.** A proper chimney crown slopes away from the flue and overhangs the brick with a drip edge, so water falls clear. A cap keeps rain, snow and animals out of the flue.

**Flashing.** Step and counter flashing at the chimney and at wall-to-roof joints, and through-wall flashing over lintels, are what keep water out of the wall.

**Weep holes.** On brick veneer, weep holes at the base of the wall and over openings let water out. They should stay open; never caulk or mortar them shut.

**Sealers.** A breathable, penetrating water repellent can help on very exposed brick after repointing. Film-forming sealers and paint trap moisture and cause spalling, so we don't use them on Cape Cod brick.

### Tuckpointing vs repointing on a Cape Cod

People use the words interchangeably. Strictly, **repointing** is removing and replacing worn mortar, and **tuckpointing** is a repointing method with a contrasting fine line, common on older Chicago brick. On most Cape Cods the job is repointing with mortar matched to the original joint.

## DIY vs hiring a pro on a Cape Cod

Small jobs, like keeping gutters clean, extending downspouts and keeping weep holes clear, are homeowner work. Masonry repair on a Cape Cod mostly isn't. The chimney is high above a steep roof, which is dangerous without proper equipment. Bagged mortar from a home center is usually too hard for older brick and the wrong color. A stoop patched with concrete over a settling footing cracks again. And caulk smeared over mortar joints traps water and makes a proper repair harder later. A professional repair costs more up front and avoids paying twice.

## The Chicago context: where Cape Cods are and why they age this way

Brick Cape Cods were built in large numbers after World War II, when returning veterans and growing families needed affordable homes. In the Chicago area they're common on the city's southwest side, in neighborhoods like Mount Greenwood and parts of Beverly, and in the inner suburbs such as Oak Lawn, Evergreen Park, Burbank and Chicago Ridge, along with many north and west suburbs.

Chicago's climate is hard on them: lots of freeze-thaw cycles every winter, wind-driven rain, and heavy snow that sits on steep roofs and melts off the short eaves onto the walls. Add 70-plus years of salt on stoops and sidewalks, and the damage pattern above is what you get. ## Maintenance and prevention for Cape Cod masonry

- **Clean gutters** every fall and spring, and fix leaking seams over brick.
- **Extend downspouts** well away from the foundation and the stoop.
- **Look at the chimney** from the ground each year with binoculars: crown, cap, mortar near the top, and any lean.
- **Check the stoop** for gaps against the house and reseal as needed.
- **Watch for rust** above windows and doors.
- **Keep weep holes clear** of mulch, soil and caulk.
- **Avoid paint and film-forming sealers** on brick.
- **Have a masonry walk-around** every few years, and after major storms.
