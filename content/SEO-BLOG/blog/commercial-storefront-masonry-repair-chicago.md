When masonry fails on a house, the stakes are a homeowner's comfort and repair bill. When it fails on a commercial storefront, the stakes include tenants, foot traffic, a public sidewalk below the parapet, an insurance file, and — for taller buildings — a facade ordinance. A loose brick over a busy Chicago retail entrance is not the same problem as a spalled bungalow face, and it shouldn't be managed the same way.

## What Commercial Storefront Masonry Repair Covers

A commercial facade is a system, and a proper repair scopes the whole system rather than chasing one visible crack. On Chicago storefronts and commercial buildings, that typically includes:

- **Facade brick repair and tuckpointing** — replacing spalled or cracked brick and repointing failed mortar joints across the elevation.
- **Parapet wall repair** — the most weather-exposed masonry on a flat-roofed building, and a frequent source of leaks and falling brick.
- **Lintel repair and replacement** — the steel beams spanning storefront windows, doors, and openings, which rust and lift the brick above.
- **Cornice and coping repair** — the projecting and capping elements that shed water off the top of the facade.
- **Sill repair** — stone or masonry sills that channel water away from openings.
- **Sealing and waterproofing** — breathable protection where appropriate, after the masonry is sound.

The through-line on nearly every commercial job is **water and steel**: water getting into the wall through failed joints, parapets, and coping, and embedded **steel lintels and shelf angles** rusting and expanding as a result.

## Why Commercial Storefronts Deteriorate

Commercial masonry fails faster than residential for structural and human reasons both.

### Freeze-thaw and exposure

Like every Chicago masonry surface, commercial brick drinks water and endures the **freeze-thaw cycle** all winter. But a storefront's **parapet** is exposed on both faces and takes concentrated runoff from the roof edge, so it weathers faster than the wall below.

### Rusting steel — lintels and shelf angles

Most commercial storefronts span wide window and door openings with **steel lintels**, and multi-story facades often hang on **shelf angles**. As that hidden steel corrodes, it expands — a phenomenon called rust-jacking — and lifts and cracks the brick above. This is both the most common and the most **safety-critical** commercial repair, because the failure mode is brick dropping onto a sidewalk.

### Failed flashing and coping

Water that gets past **coping stones** and **through-wall flashing** at the parapet and roof edge runs straight into the wall, washing out mortar and rusting steel from the inside.

### Deferred maintenance

The quiet killer. On an owner-occupied house, small problems get noticed. On a leased commercial building, open joints and a hairline parapet crack are invisible to tenants and easy to defer — until they become water in a tenant's ceiling or a chunk of parapet on the pavement.

### Signs a property manager should act on

- **Cracking above storefront windows and doors**, often with orange rust staining.
- **White efflorescence** or damp patches on interior tenant walls.
- **Loose, bulging, or displaced brick**, especially at the parapet.
- **Open, receding, or sandy mortar joints** across the facade.
- **Water stains at the top-floor ceiling** or roof line.

## The Process on an Occupied Commercial Building

The defining challenge of commercial work isn't the masonry — it's doing the masonry while the business runs. Our process is built around that.

1. **Inspection and assessment.** We evaluate the full facade — brick, joints, lintels, parapet, coping, sills — and document conditions with the detail a manager, board, or insurer needs.
2. **Scope and phasing.** We build a scope that can be **phased** to minimize disruption: sequencing the facade, scheduling loud or access-blocking work around business hours, and keeping the storefront usable throughout.
3. **Access and safety.** We set up **scaffolding or lift access**, and — critically on a storefront — **sidewalk and entrance protection** so customers and the public stay safe below the work.
4. **Repair.** We tuckpoint and replace brick, repair or replace **lintels** and **shelf angles**, rebuild **parapets**, and address **coping and flashing** so water stops getting in.
5. **Seal and finish.** Where appropriate, we finish with breathable [masonry sealing and waterproofing](/services/masonry-sealing) once the wall is sound.

## Materials and Technique

Commercial repairs use **Type N and Type S mortar** matched to the existing wall, **matched face brick** for replacements, **galvanized or coated steel** for lintel and shelf-angle work, and proper **flashing and coping** to keep water out for the long term. On a commercial building especially, the goal is a repair that lasts through many winters with minimal re-entry — every return trip to a multi-story facade means scaffolding again, so getting the water management right the first time is where the real savings live.

## What Drives the Cost

Commercial pricing has a wide range, and the biggest drivers are:

- **Height and access** — scaffolding or lifts on a multi-story facade is often the largest single factor.
- **Scope** — isolated lintel and spot repointing versus a full-facade restoration.
- **Occupied-site logistics** — phasing, off-hours scheduling, and public protection.
- **Extent of hidden steel** — how many lintels and shelf angles are involved.
- **Parapet and coping condition** — often more deteriorated than the visible wall.

Taller buildings in Chicago fall under a **facade-inspection ordinance** that requires periodic assessment of exterior walls and prompt correction of unsafe conditions. What applies depends on your building's height and location, and this isn't legal advice — but the practical point for an owner is that a deteriorating parapet or a rust-jacked lintel over a public way isn't only a maintenance issue, it's a liability and compliance one. ## Maintenance and Prevention

- **Schedule routine inspections** — every few years and after severe weather, plus any required facade-ordinance cycle.
- **Keep water managed** — sound coping, flashing, and roof-edge details.
- **Repoint failing joints early**, before water reaches the steel.
- **Address rust staining promptly** — it's the visible warning of a lintel or shelf angle beginning to fail.
- **Budget proactively** — phased maintenance is far cheaper than emergency repair and the liability of falling masonry.
