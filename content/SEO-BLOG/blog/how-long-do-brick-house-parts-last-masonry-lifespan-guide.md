# How Long Do the Parts of a Brick House Last?

**A brick house can last well over a century**, but not every part of it does. The **brick** itself can last 100 years or more. The **mortar joints** typically need repointing every 20 to 30-plus years. **Chimney crowns**, **caulk** and **sealers** wear out sooner, and **steel lintels** can rust within a few decades if they were poorly flashed. Knowing those lifespans lets you plan repairs instead of reacting to them. The ranges below are typical, not guarantees. A well-built, well-maintained wall on a sheltered side of a house will beat them. A wet chimney top on the north side of an exposed house will fall short. The point is to know which parts to watch, and when.

> Brick rarely fails on its own. It fails when the parts that are supposed to protect it wear out and nobody notices.

## The masonry lifespan chart

| Component | Typical lifespan | What shortens it | Signs it is wearing out |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Brick** | 100+ years | Saturation, hard mortar, sealers, salt | Spalling, flaking faces |
| **Mortar joints** | 20–30+ years | Exposure, weak or hard mix, poor original work | Recessed, sandy or cracked joints |
| **Chimney crown (cement wash)** | Often under 10–15 years | Thin mix, no overhang | Cracks, pieces on the roof |
| **Chimney crown (formed, with drip)** | Often 20+ years | No cap, poor mix | Hairline cracks that open up |
| **Chimney cap (metal)** | 10–25+ years depending on metal | Cheap galvanized metal, wind damage | Rust, loose fastening |
| **Chimney flashing** | 20–30+ years | Poor install, failed counterflashing | Leaks at the chimney, loose metal |
| **Steel lintels** | Decades if dry; 20–40 years if wet | No flashing, blocked weeps | Rust, cracks at opening corners |
| **Limestone and cast stone sills** | Many decades | Open joints, back pitch, salt | Cracks, spalling, tipped sills |
| **Sealant joints (caulk)** | About 5–15 years | Sun, movement, poor prep | Splits, hardening, pulling away |
| **Water repellents** | Several years per application | Product, exposure | Brick darkens readily when wet |
| **Parging on foundations** | 10–20+ years | Water behind it, wrong mix | Cracks, hollow spots, flaking |

These are general ranges we see on Chicago-area homes. Product, workmanship and exposure change them a lot.

## Why each part ages differently

Masonry is a **system**. Brick is the durable part; everything else is there to keep water away from it or to manage movement.

### Brick

Fired clay is extremely durable as long as it can dry out. Problems start when it stays wet: each winter, water inside the brick freezes and expands, and the face eventually **spalls**. Soft older brick, such as **Chicago common brick**, is more sensitive than modern face brick.

### Mortar

Mortar is meant to be the **sacrificial** part of the wall. It is softer than the brick, so movement and moisture wear the joints rather than the brick. That is a feature, not a flaw: repointing joints every few decades is far cheaper than replacing brick. Mortar that is harder than the brick reverses this and damages the brick.

### Chimney crowns and caps

The crown sits flat on top of the chimney and takes standing water, snow and every freeze-thaw cycle. Thin **cement wash** crowns crack early. A properly formed crown that overhangs the brick with a drip edge, under a cap, lasts far longer.

### Lintels

**Steel lintels** last as long as they stay dry. Without flashing to turn water out, moisture running down inside the wall sits on the steel. Rust swells and cracks the brick above, and the lintel then needs replacement.

### Sills

Stone sills last a long time, but their joints do not. Open joints and a sill that tips back toward the wall let water into the wall under the window.

### Sealants

Caulk at window frames, sills and lintel ends is the shortest-lived part of the system. It is also cheap to maintain, which is why it is worth checking regularly.

## Warning signs by area

### On the roof

- Crumbling mortar or flaking brick at the chimney top.
- A cracked crown, missing cap or loose flashing.

### On the walls

- Joints recessed more than about a quarter inch.
- Flaking brick near downspouts or at the base.
- White efflorescence or dark wet patches.

### At openings

- Rust and cracks above windows and the garage.
- Cracked or tipped sills and split caulk.

### At the base

- Washed-out bottom joints, cracked parging, damp basement walls.

## How a professional extends the life of a brick house

1. **Inspect the whole system**, roof to foundation, once every few years or after major storms.
2. **Find the water**: gutters, downspouts, flashing and grading.
3. **Repoint early** in the areas that wear first, with mortar compatible with the brick.
4. **Upgrade the chimney top** with a formed crown and a cap.
5. **Flash lintels** when they are replaced, with end dams and weep holes.
6. **Reset and seal sills** so they shed water.
7. **Renew caulk** on a schedule.
8. **Use water repellents selectively**, only where they help and only breathable ones.

## Materials and techniques that last longer

| Choice | Longer-lasting option | Shorter-lasting option |
|---|---|---|
| Repointing mortar | Matched to brick, Type N or lime-rich on soft brick | Hard, high-cement mix |
| Repointing depth | Joints cut back to a proper depth | Thin skim over old mortar |
| Chimney crown | Formed, overhanging, with drip edge | Thin cement wash |
| Chimney cap | Stainless steel | Light galvanized steel |
| Lintel | Primed or galvanized steel with flashing and end dams | Bare steel, no flashing |
| Sealant | Quality masonry-rated sealant on a proper backer | General-purpose caulk smeared over gaps |
| Water repellent | Breathable silane/siloxane where needed | Film-forming sealers and paint |

## What to expect at each age of a brick house

Lifespans make more sense when you line them up against the age of the house. This is the pattern we typically see on Chicago-area brick homes. Your house may run ahead of or behind it, depending on exposure and past repairs.

### Around 10 to 20 years

- **Caulk** at windows, doors and sills starts to split and needs its first replacement.
- **Thin chimney crowns** may show their first cracks.
- **Weep holes** may be clogged by mulch, dirt or insects.
- Brick and mortar are usually in good shape.

### Around 20 to 40 years

- **Lintels** without good flashing begin to rust; the first cracks appear at garage and window corners.
- **Mortar** on the chimney top and the most exposed walls starts to recess.
- **Sills** may need joint work, and some begin to tip.
- **Chimney crowns** often need replacement, ideally upgraded to a formed crown with a cap.

### Around 40 to 70 years

- **Repointing** is commonly due on larger areas, starting with the chimney, parapets and weather side.
- **Chimney tops** may need partial rebuilds above the roof.
- **Steps, stoops and low walls** near salt and splash often need rebuilding.
- **Foundation parging** and window well masonry need attention.

### 70 to 100-plus years

- A second or third round of **repointing**, ideally with compatible mortar this time if earlier repairs used hard cement.
- **Brick replacement** where past water problems or hard mortar have caused spalling.
- **Lintels and sills** that were never replaced are likely due.
- Careful work on soft older brick and lime mortar becomes the main concern.

If your house is approaching one of these stages, a whole-house look is usually worth more than repairing one item at a time. A single visit can catch the next two or three items on the list while the ladders and scaffolding are already up, and it gives you a realistic picture of what the next decade of upkeep will involve.

## DIY vs hiring a pro

Homeowners can do a lot to extend masonry life: keep gutters and downspouts clear, check the chimney top with binoculars, look for rust and cracks, and replace small sections of caulk. Repointing, crown work, lintel replacement and sill resetting are skilled jobs, and the wrong materials, especially hard mortar and film-forming sealers, can shorten the life of the brick itself.

## A simple maintenance plan

- **Every year**: clear gutters and downspouts; check caulk; look at the chimney top with binoculars.
- **Every 3 to 5 years**: have a mason look over the whole house, including the roof-level chimney.
- **When caulk fails**: replace it promptly.
- **When joints recess**: repoint the worst areas before they spread.
- **When lintels rust**: plan replacement before the brick cracks.
