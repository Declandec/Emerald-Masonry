Most homeowners ask what it costs to do the house. That is the wrong unit.

**Your house does not weather as one object. It weathers as four walls in four different climates.**

The south elevation takes the most sun and the sharpest daily temperature swings. The west elevation takes the driving rain, because most Chicagoland storms arrive from that direction. The north elevation stays shaded and damp and grows algae, but often deteriorates more slowly. The east elevation usually does best of all.

The practical consequence: **on a typical Elmwood Park brick home, the south and west walls are frequently several years ahead of the north and east in deterioration.** A single number for "the house" either overpays for two walls that were fine, or underscopes two walls that were not.

## What Restoration Actually Includes

Restoration is not one service. On a residential envelope it usually means some combination of:

- **Repointing** deteriorated mortar joints, cut back to sound material and refilled.
- **Brick repair and replacement** for units that have spalled, cracked or come loose.
- **Lintel work** where the steel over an opening has corroded and lifted the brick.
- **Sill repair** on limestone or cast stone that has cracked or eroded.
- **Chimney restoration** — crown, joints, flashing and cap.
- **Sealant renewal** at window perimeters and material transitions.
- **Water management** — the gutter, downspout, sill and grade issues feeding the damage.

## Signs an Elmwood Park Home Needs Restoration

- **Mortar joints receded** behind the brick face, or raking out as sand under a key.
- **Spalling brick** — faces flaking, shelling or popping off.
- **Step cracks** running diagonally through joints, especially at corners and openings.
- **Rust staining** below window and door openings — the steel lintel corroding.
- **Cracked or eroded limestone sills**, or sills that have dropped at one end.
- **Efflorescence** — white powder or crust — on the exterior, or on the inside of a basement wall.
- **Loose or missing brick**, particularly at the chimney and the top courses.
- **Damage concentrated at the bottom three courses**, where splash, snow and salt collect.
- **Hard gray mortar standing proud** of the brick — a prior repair with the wrong mix.

## Risks of Waiting

Masonry deterioration is not linear. **Open joints admit water, water accelerates the damage, and the damage opens more joints.** A wall that needed repointing this year needs repointing plus a dozen replacement brick in three years, and repointing plus fifty replacement brick in eight.

The threshold to watch for is **spalling**. Once faces start coming off the brick, you have crossed from a mortar problem into a unit problem, and unit replacement is several times the cost of repointing the same area. Everything before that point is comparatively cheap.

## How We Scope It by Elevation

1. **Walk all four sides** and the chimney, and photograph conditions on each.
2. **Test the mortar** for hardness relative to the brick, which determines the repair mix.
3. **Grade each elevation** — urgent, this season, monitor, or fine.
4. **Identify the water sources** feeding the worst areas: gutters, downspouts, sills, grade, flashing.
5. **Write the scope elevation by elevation** with quantities, so phasing is a real option rather than a guess.
6. **Sequence by water path** — the top and the openings before the field walls, because that is where water enters.
7. **Document the mortar mix, sand and joint profile** so a later phase matches this one.

## Our Repair Process

1. **Cut joints back to sound mortar** — generally two to two and a half times the joint width — using hand-raking on softer brick and controlled grinding only where the masonry can take it.
2. **Mortar matched** to the existing in hardness, color, sand and texture, judged on cured samples rather than wet.
3. **Repoint in lifts**, packed and compacted rather than skimmed.
4. **Tool to the original joint profile** so the repair disappears.
5. **Replace failed units** with salvaged or matched brick, cut out through the joints so neighboring brick are not damaged.
6. **Lintel, sill and chimney work** as scoped, with flashing where it belongs.
7. **Correct the water** so the repair is not on a countdown.
8. **Clean down** as we go, before mortar cures onto the faces.

## What Drives Cost

- **How many elevations** are in scope.
- **The repointing-to-replacement ratio** — replacement is the expensive part.
- **Height and access** — two-story and dormer work needs staging.
- **Chimney condition**, which is often the single largest line on an older home.
- **Lintels and sills** involved, each of which adds shoring, removal and rebuild.
- **Mortar matching complexity** on older or unusual brick.
- **Prior bad repairs** that have to be removed before anything correct can go in.
- **Water-source correction** at gutters, grade and flashing.
