## What Brick Repair Actually Involves

There is an important distinction homeowners rarely get explained.

**Mortar failure is repointing. Brick failure is replacement.** Mortar is the sacrificial layer in a masonry wall — designed to be softer than the brick and to wear out first, because replacing mortar is cheap. When only the joints have gone, [tuckpointing](/services/tuckpointing) restores the wall.

But once water has been getting in long enough to saturate and freeze inside the brick units themselves, the faces **spall** — they flake, shell and pop off — and a spalled brick cannot be restored. It has to be cut out and replaced with a matched unit. Most real jobs in St. Charles involve both operations, plus fixing whatever was letting the water in.

## Signs Your St. Charles Brick Needs Repair

- **Spalling faces** — brick flaking, shelling or crumbling, worst near grade, below sills, and on shaded north elevations.
- **Cracked brick**, especially cracks running through the units rather than the joints.
- **Loose brick** you can move by hand.
- **Step cracks** climbing diagonally through the mortar joints.
- **Rust staining** running down from above a window or door — a corroding steel lintel expanding inside the wall.
- **Receded or sandy mortar joints** that rake out under a screwdriver.
- **Efflorescence**, the white chalky bloom that proves water is moving through the wall.
- **A bulge or bow** in the wall plane — a structural condition needing evaluation, not a patch.
- **Brick debris** collecting at the base of the wall.

## Why Brick Fails Here Specifically

**Freeze-thaw is the engine.** It is not extreme cold that destroys masonry but repeated crossings of the freezing point with water present — dozens of them per Kane County winter, plus more in the shoulder seasons. Each cycle expands trapped water roughly nine percent inside the brick.

**The river raises the moisture load.** Elevated humidity along the Fox corridor and shaded riverside lots mean masonry that stays wetter, longer, going into each of those cycles.

**Age varies the vulnerability.** Downtown's 19th-century soft brick absorbs far more water than modern face brick and needs soft lime mortar to match. Post-war brick is denser but its Portland-based mortar is now sixty to seventy years old. Modern veneer fails differently again, usually at ties, flashing and weep holes rather than in the units.

**Water management is usually the real culprit.** Overflowing gutters, downspouts discharging at the wall, grade sloping toward the house, failed sills and splash-back at grade concentrate water exactly where the damage shows up.

## Our Brick Repair Process

1. **Free on-site assessment.** We read the damage pattern, determine whether it is unit failure, joint failure, movement or steel corrosion, and find the water source.
2. **Fix the cause first.** Repairing brick while leaving a failed sill, rusting lintel or overflowing gutter in place guarantees a repeat.
3. **Careful removal.** Damaged units cut out without disturbing sound surrounding brick — the part where inexperienced crews cause more damage than they repair.
4. **Matching.** Replacement brick selected for size, color, texture and absorption; mortar matched for hardness, color and sand.
5. **Setting and repointing.** New units bedded properly and joints packed in lifts, then tooled to the original profile.
6. **Related repairs.** [Lintel repair](/services/lintel-repair), [limestone and sill repair](/services/limestone-sill-repair) or [foundation masonry repair](/services/foundation-masonry-repair) where the assessment calls for it.
7. **Cleanup and protection.** Gentle cleaning only — never sandblasting or high-pressure washing, both of which strip the fired face off brick and accelerate the next round of spalling.

## What Waiting Costs

Spalling is progressive. Once a brick loses its fired face, the soft porous body underneath absorbs water far faster, so the unit deteriorates more quickly and starts wetting its neighbors. A dozen brick becomes fifty; fifty becomes a section rebuild. And because spalling means water is already inside the wall, the steel lintels and ties in there are corroding at the same time.
