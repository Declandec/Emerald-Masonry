## Why brick fails where it meets concrete

Brick and concrete behave differently. The house wall sits on a foundation that goes below the frost line. Stoops, porch slabs, patios and driveways usually do not. In a Chicagoland winter, wet soil under those slabs freezes and lifts them slightly, then they settle back in spring. Over decades they also sink.

When a slab or stoop is built tight against the brick with no separation, that movement pushes, lifts and drags on the lowest courses. Add water that pools where the slab slopes toward the house, and road salt carried in on tyres and boots, and those few courses take more abuse than the rest of the wall combined.

## Signs your Hickory Hills home needs brick repair

- **Cracked or broken bricks** in the bottom two or three courses, especially beside a stoop or slab.
- **Horizontal cracks** just above a porch slab, or bricks pushed outward at the slab line.
- **Loose, rocking bricks** on step treads, stoop sides and garage door piers.
- **Spalling**: brick faces flaking off near the driveway and garage door.
- **A gap opening** between the stoop and the house, or steps leaning away from the wall.
- **Planter walls** bulging, cracking or stained white with efflorescence.

## Risks of waiting

A cracked brick at the slab line lets water straight into the wall, and each freeze makes the crack longer. A loose step tread becomes a trip hazard. A stoop pulling away from the house opens a direct path for water into the foundation. Small brick repairs done early cost far less than a stoop rebuild or replacing a whole run of lower brick.

## How we repair brick in Hickory Hills

1. **Diagnose the movement.** We check whether the slab, stoop or wall is moving, and how water reaches the damaged brick.
2. **Remove damaged units** carefully, cutting out mortar around each brick so neighbouring brick is not disturbed.
3. **Match the brick** for size, colour and texture, using salvaged brick where it gives the best match.
4. **Set new brick** in matched mortar, fully bedded, with joints tooled to the original profile.
5. **Separate brick from concrete** with a proper isolation joint and a durable sealant, so slab movement no longer drags on the wall.
6. **Reset or rebuild** steps, stoop facing, piers and planters on a sound base where needed.
7. **Correct the water**, sloping slabs and planters away from the wall where we can and keeping weep holes clear.

## Materials we use

Matched solid or cored face brick, or salvaged brick; **Type N** mortar for above-grade wall repairs and **Type S** for steps, stoops and planters where the masonry sits in contact with soil and slush; flexible joint sealant at brick-to-concrete joints; and drainage and liners in rebuilt planters. Where a slab itself has failed, we tell you so and can coordinate the concrete side.
