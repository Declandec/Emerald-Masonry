## What Foundation Masonry Repair Covers

Foundation masonry repair addresses the **masonry** of a foundation wall — the brick or concrete block (CMU) and the mortar holding it together. That includes:

- **Repointing deteriorated joints** in block and brick foundation walls.
- **Rebuilding spalled or crumbled sections** of block or brick, common near grade and at corners.
- **Crack repair** in block walls, matched to the cause — sealing and monitoring a stable hairline, or rebuilding around a displaced one.
- **Parging** — the protective mortar coating on the exterior of a foundation — where it has failed and exposed the block.
- **Managing the water source** — grading, splashback, downspouts, and joints that let water reach and weaken the wall.

It is distinct from structural underpinning or piering, which lifts and stabilizes a settling footing. When a wall needs that engineered work, we say so plainly.

## Signs Your Minooka Foundation Needs Masonry Repair

- **Stair-step cracks** following the mortar joints up a block wall.
- **Horizontal cracks** in a block wall — a more serious sign of soil pressure that needs prompt evaluation.
- **Crumbling, spalling, or flaking block**, especially the exposed courses near grade.
- **Failed or missing parging** exposing bare block.
- **White efflorescence** on interior foundation walls — water moving through the masonry.
- **Damp crawl space or basement walls**, musty smell, or visible moisture tracking.
- **Loose, sandy mortar joints** you can rake out by hand.

## Why Foundations Fail Around Minooka

Two forces do most of the damage here. The first is **soil movement** — the clay-heavy soils common across Will, Grundy, and Kendall County swell when wet and shrink when dry, and that seasonal push-pull stresses foundation walls, especially under the newer subdivisions that filled in around Minooka over the last few decades. The second is **water** — poor grading, short downspouts, and splashback keep the lower courses wet, and freeze-thaw cycling then breaks down the block and mortar from the outside in. Older homes near the original downtown add a third factor: foundations old enough that the original mortar has simply worn out.

## The Risk of Waiting

Foundation masonry damage is progressive and it compounds. Open joints and failed parging let in more water; more water means more freeze-thaw breakdown and more efflorescence; a hairline crack that is left to move becomes a displaced one. Water in the foundation also does not stay in the foundation — it shows up as a damp basement, mold, and ruined finishes. Catching it while it is still a masonry repair keeps it from becoming a structural project.
