## What HOA masonry repair in Homer Glen actually covers

For a board, "the masonry" usually means four separate groups of work. Separating them is what makes the scope something you can budget.

| Group | What it includes | What typically fails first |
|---|---|---|
| Building fronts | Brick veneer front elevations, garage returns, front porch piers | Open joints at corners and garage returns, rusted lintels over garage doors |
| Openings and transitions | Window and door lintels, stone or precast sills, brick-to-siding corners | Rust staining and step cracks at lintels, failed sealant at sills and corner returns |
| Site masonry | Entrance monuments, pier walls, brick mailbox banks, planters | Loose caps, cracked piers, salt-damaged lower courses |
| Walls and grade | Masonry retaining walls, pond edge walls, brick steps | Bulging and leaning sections, washed-out joints |

### Building fronts

A brick veneer front is a drained wall: water is expected to pass the brick and leave through weep holes over flashing. After twenty-plus Will County winters, the mortar joints at outside corners, garage returns and porch piers are open, and many builders' weep holes are blocked with mortar droppings. Tuckpointing those areas — not necessarily every joint on every front — is usually the first building-side line item.

### Openings and transitions

The steel angle lintels over garage doors and wide front windows on this era of townhome were rarely galvanized. When they rust they expand, crack the brick above at a stair-step angle and push the end bricks out. The second weak point is where the brick front stops and the siding starts: at that corner return, the window heads and the top of the brick ledge, failed flashing and sealant let water behind the veneer.

### Site masonry

Monuments and mailbox banks are freestanding, so every face gets wet and the stone or precast cap is the only roof they have. Once the cap joints open, water freezes in the core and splits the piers. Add sprinkler overspray and plow salt, and the bottom courses go next.

## Signs your association needs HOA masonry repair

- **Rust streaks and stair-step cracks** above garage doors and front windows.
- **Open or sandy mortar joints** at outside corners and garage returns.
- **Loose, tipped or cracked caps** on entrance monuments and pier walls.
- **Cracked or leaning brick around mailbox banks.**
- **White efflorescence** on low courses near driveways and walks.
- **Bulging or leaning retaining walls** and washed-out joints near pond edges.
- **Interior water stains** at the corner where a brick front meets siding.

## Risks of waiting

Freestanding site masonry fails fastest and fails publicly: a monument cap that falls, a pier that leans toward a sidewalk, a mailbox bank residents have to reach around. Lintel rust that is cheap to address while the crack is hairline becomes a brick rebuild above every garage door. And a board that waits until owners complain usually ends up funding the whole community at once instead of in planned phases.
