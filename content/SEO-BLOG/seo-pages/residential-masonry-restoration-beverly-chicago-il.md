## What Whole-Home Masonry Restoration Covers

Residential masonry restoration is broader than any single repair. On an older Beverly home it ties several trades together into one coordinated plan:

- **Tuckpointing and repointing** — grinding out failed mortar joints and refilling them with matched mortar, the backbone of most restorations.
- **Brick repair and replacement** — resetting loose brick, patching spalled faces, and cutting out and replacing individual failed units with matched brick.
- **Greystone and limestone restoration** — repointing and repairing spalled or delaminated Bedford limestone on elevations, porches, water tables, and decorative banding.
- **Chimney repair** — rebuilding deteriorated stacks above the roofline, crowns, caps, and flashing.
- **Lintel and sill repair** — addressing rusted steel lintels and failed limestone sills that funnel water into the walls.
- **Breathable sealing** — a water-repellent finish step on sound masonry, applied only after the actual defects are fixed.

The reason to treat these together on a historic home is simple: water doesn't respect trade boundaries. A failing sill, an open joint, a cracked crown, and a clogged downspout can all be feeding the same damp wall. Restoring the whole envelope at once — walls, chimney, sills, lintels, foundation, and water management — is what actually stops the deterioration.

## Why Older Beverly Homes Need a Coordinated Approach

The single most important fact about restoring a pre-1930 Beverly home is this: **it was built with soft lime mortar and softer brick, and repointing it with hard modern Portland-heavy mortar will spall the historic brick.** Rigid, high-strength mortar won't flex the way the original does, so seasonal movement and freeze-thaw stress get taken out on the brick face instead of the joint — and the brick loses. That's how a well-meant "repair" ends up destroying the very masonry it was supposed to save.

Emerald matches mortar to the original by composition and compressive strength, by color, and by tooled joint profile, so the repointing behaves like the mortar around it and disappears into the wall. On a landmark-quality Beverly home, matching the look matters as much as matching the performance — the restoration should be invisible from the sidewalk.

> Residential masonry restoration works because it treats the home as one system: the mortar is matched to the brick, the brick and stone are repaired to shed water, and the chimney, sills, and lintels are brought back in line — so the whole envelope protects itself the way it did when it was built.

## Signs Your Beverly Home Is Due for Restoration

- Widespread **receding, sandy, or crumbling mortar joints** across multiple elevations
- **Spalling or flaking brick** — faces popping or crumbling off, especially on north and weather walls
- **Cracked, delaminating, or spalled greystone** and failing limestone sills
- **Rust stains and horizontal cracks** above windows, doors, or the garage (failing steel lintels)
- A **deteriorating chimney** — crumbling joints, a cracked crown, missing cap, or a lean
- **Efflorescence** (white chalky bloom) signaling water moving through the walls
- **Prior bad repairs** — hard gray mortar smeared over soft historic brick, or mismatched patches

### The Risk of Waiting

Masonry deterioration compounds. Once water gets past failed joints and spalled brick into the wall core, it accelerates freeze-thaw damage, rusts embedded steel, rots window frames, and stains interior plaster. Spot repointing that would have solved the problem a few years ago becomes brick replacement and, eventually, section rebuilding. On an irreplaceable historic home, catching it earlier also means saving more of the original material.

## Our Restoration Process

1. **Assessment.** We walk the whole home and document the masonry envelope — walls, chimney, sills, lintels, foundation, and how water is being managed — so nothing is missed.
2. **Mortar matching.** We match the original mortar's composition/strength, color, and joint profile, and identify matching brick and stone for any units that need replacing.
3. **Repointing.** We grind or rake failed joints to sound depth, pack them with matched mortar, tool them to the original profile, and keep them damp to cure properly.
4. **Brick and stone repair.** We reset loose units, patch or replace spalled brick, and address greystone spalling and delamination.
5. **Chimney, sill, and lintel work.** We rebuild or repair the chimney as needed, perform Dutchman repairs or replacements on failed limestone sills, and treat rusted lintels.
6. **Sealing.** Where it helps, we finish sound masonry with a breathable, penetrating water repellent — never a film-forming coating that traps moisture.

## Materials We Use

We work in **matched mortar** — lime-rich, softer mixes for pre-1930 masonry, and the appropriate Type N or Type S mix where the wall calls for it — because matching the mortar to the brick is what makes a restoration last. We source close-matching brick and **Indiana/Bedford limestone**, use color-matched jointing, and finish with breathable silane/siloxane-type water repellents rather than paint or film-forming sealers.
