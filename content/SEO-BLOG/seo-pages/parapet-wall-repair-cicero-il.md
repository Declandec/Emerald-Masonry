## What a Parapet Wall Is — and Why It Fails First

A parapet is the section of exterior wall that continues **above the roofline** on a flat-roofed building — the low brick wall you see running along the top edge of Cicero's two- and three-story storefronts and apartment blocks. Here's the insight most owners never hear: the rest of your building's wall is exposed to weather on one face, but the **parapet is exposed on both faces and across the top**. Rain, snow, and freeze-thaw attack it from every direction at once.

That's why the parapet is the most abused masonry on the entire building. Water gets into it faster, freezes and expands inside the brick and mortar more often, and dries out more slowly. On Cicero's older commercial masonry — the brick blocks along **Cermak Road, Roosevelt Road, and Cicero Avenue**, the mixed-use buildings with apartments over ground-floor retail, and the century-old industrial stock around the former Hawthorne Works — parapets take decades of this punishment and eventually let go.

## Signs Your Cicero Parapet Wall Needs Repair

Parapet failure is usually visible from the street or the roof if you know what to look for:

- **Leaning, bowing, or bulging** — the parapet is visibly out of plumb or belly-ing outward.
- **Open, crumbling, or missing mortar joints** along the parapet brick.
- **Cracked, shifted, or missing coping stones or caps** on top of the wall.
- **Failed sealant** at the coping joints — open gaps where water pours straight in.
- **Efflorescence** — white chalky staining — and **spalled, flaking brick** on the parapet.
- **Water stains on the top-floor ceiling or wall** inside the building.
- **Loose brick or debris** landing on the roof or the sidewalk below.

That last one is the emergency. On a busy Cicero block, a brick or a piece of coping falling from a parapet onto a public sidewalk is a serious life-safety and liability problem. If your parapet is dropping material, treat it as urgent.

## Why Waiting Costs More

A parapet that's only lost its mortar joints can often be repointed and saved. Left alone, that same water keeps working down into the wall core, freezes, and pushes the brick apart until the wall leans — and now it's a rebuild, not a repointing. Meanwhile the water that's entering at the top is running down inside the wall and showing up as stained ceilings, damaged roofing at the wall-to-roof joint, and rotted interior finishes on the top floor. Acting while the problem is still a repointing job is dramatically cheaper than waiting until it's a rebuild plus interior repairs.

## The Real Root Cause: Coping and Flashing at the Top

Most parapet leaks and most parapet failures start at the **top of the wall**, not the face. Two things protect that top:

1. **Coping** — the caps (stone, precast, or metal) that sit on top of the parapet and shed water off the wall.
2. **Through-wall flashing** — a concealed waterproof membrane built into the wall just below the coping that catches any water that gets past the caps and directs it back out.

When the coping cracks or shifts and its joint sealant fails, and when the flashing underneath is old, damaged, or was never there, water pours straight into the core of the parapet from above. This is exactly why some owners repoint the face of a parapet and are baffled when it leaks again the next spring — **the face was never the problem.**

A parapet repair that ignores the coping and flashing is not a repair; it's a delay. When we rebuild or repair a Cicero parapet, we reset or replace the coping, repair or install the through-wall flashing, seal the coping joints, and tie the roof membrane back into the wall so the whole top of the parapet sheds and drains the way it should.

1. **Inspection** — we assess the parapet from the roof and the street, checking plumb, mortar condition, coping, flashing, and where water is tracking inside.

## What Drives the Cost of Parapet Wall Repair in Cicero

Every building is different, so we never quote a parapet without seeing it. The factors that move the number:

- **Linear footage** of parapet that needs work.
- **Height and access** — a three-story building on a tight commercial block needs staging or a lift before a single brick is touched.
- **Repoint vs. rebuild** — stabilizing sound brick costs far less than dismantling and rebuilding a leaning run.
- **Coping scope** — resetting existing coping vs. sourcing and installing new caps.
- **Flashing** — installing through-wall flashing is more involved than resealing joints.
- **Roof tie-in** — whether the roofing membrane has to be reworked at the wall.
