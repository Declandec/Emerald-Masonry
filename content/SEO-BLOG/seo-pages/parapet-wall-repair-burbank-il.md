## Why parapet wall repair in Burbank should come before the roof

A parapet and a roof share one detail: the point where the membrane turns up the inside face of the wall and the masonry above laps over it. The roofer owns the membrane and base flashing. The mason owns the coping, the through-wall flashing and the reglet or joint that receives the counterflashing.

When those two jobs happen in the wrong order, three things go wrong:

1. **The roofer terminates onto failing masonry.** Termination bars and sealant fastened into soft, cracked brick or open joints do not hold.
2. **Water keeps coming in from above.** A new roof does nothing for an open coping joint, so the "roof leak" returns and the roofer gets called back.
3. **The masonry repair destroys the new work.** Rebuilding the top courses or installing through-wall flashing later means cutting back the base flashing on a roof that may still be under warranty.

If you are pricing a roof replacement in Burbank, have the parapet assessed in the same month. If the parapet needs work, the masonry goes first, and the roofer arrives to a wall that is ready.

## What a parapet is, and how it fails

A parapet is the part of the exterior wall that continues above the roof line. It is wet from both faces, capped horizontally, exposed to every freeze-thaw cycle, and almost never inspected. On Burbank's 1950s and 1960s commercial stock it is typically brick on the street face with common brick or concrete block behind, capped with clay tile, limestone, precast or sheet-metal coping.

### Signs you need parapet wall repair

- **Open or cracked coping joints**, or coping stones that rock when pushed.
- **Spalled, flaking or missing brick** on the roof-side face — usually worse than the street face.
- **The parapet leaning outward** or bulging, visible when you sight along the roof edge.
- **Water stains on the top-floor ceiling or walls** near the perimeter, even after roof repairs.
- **White efflorescence or rust streaks** running down the street face from the top.
- **Old sign brackets, conduit straps, satellite mounts and guy-wire anchors** drilled into the wall.
- **Base flashing pulling away** from the inside face of the wall.

## Rooftop equipment, signs and two generations of tenants

Owner-occupied buildings change use. A Burbank building that housed a print shop in the 1980s may now hold a restaurant with a rooftop exhaust fan, a new HVAC unit, a sign on the parapet face and a satellite dish strapped to the coping. Every one of those installations was fastened into the parapet, and almost none of the abandoned ones were ever patched.

Those holes are the most overlooked water entry on small commercial roofs. Our scope lists every penetration we find — active and abandoned — and assigns each a fix: reseal, patch, replace the unit, or relocate the bracket onto a curb rather than the wall.

## Risks of waiting on parapet repair in Burbank

- **Interior damage** — ceilings, insulation, inventory and equipment under the perimeter.
- **Falling masonry** — a loose coping stone or brick above a sidewalk, parking lot or entrance is a liability, not a maintenance item.
- **Steel corrosion** — water in the wall reaches lintels and roof framing.
- **A shorter roof life** — a parapet leak that saturates the insulation can shorten the life of a membrane that is otherwise sound.
- **A bigger scope** — a parapet that needs repointing this year can need rebuilding after two or three more winters.

Where a parapet will be rebuilt, we also look at whether a metal coping cap over the masonry is the better long-term choice for that building.

## What drives the cost of parapet wall repair

- **Linear footage** of the parapet and its height above the roof.
- **Coping** — reset versus full replacement, and the material.
- **Repoint versus rebuild** — rebuilding the top courses is the largest cost driver.
- **Access** — roof hatch, ladder, lift and material hoisting.
- **Rooftop equipment and signage** that has to be worked around or removed.
- **Roof timing** — work done alongside a roof replacement avoids cutting and re-flashing later.
