## Commercial Tuckpointing in Bridgeview Starts With Four Different Walls

Drive the industrial streets between 71st and 79th, or along Harlem toward 87th, and the same building type repeats: a **brick office front** facing the street, with **concrete block warehouse walls** on the other three sides, a dock wall at the back, and a flat roof behind a short parapet.

Those walls do not age at the same rate, and they should not be bought as one number.

- **The brick office face** usually has the best brick and the most visible mortar. It is the wall a tenant or buyer judges the building by, and the one where colour matching actually matters.
- **The block side walls** carry wide, often flush or struck joints in a cement-heavy mortar. They fail at the head joints and along the top courses under the parapet.
- **The dock wall** takes forklift impact, de-icing salt, standing water at grade and truck exhaust. Its lower four feet routinely look a decade older than the wall above.
- **The weather elevation** — typically west or south-west in this part of Cook County — takes driven rain and freeze-thaw on every storm, and shows open joints first.

A single lump sum hides all of that. A scope by elevation shows it.

## What a Bridgeview Commercial Tuckpointing Scope Should Look Like

The deliverable from an Emerald assessment is a written scope, not a phone quote. For each elevation it lists:

1. **Measured area** of wall and the estimated percentage of joints needing cut-out, rather than "tuckpoint as needed."
2. **Condition rating** — sound, spot repointing, or full repointing.
3. **Minimum cut-out depth** — at least two and a half times the joint width, so new mortar locks in rather than sitting on the surface.
4. **Mortar specification** — type and colour-match method, softer than the units it sits between.
5. **Access method** — lift, pipe staging or swing stage — and what it assumes about the lot, the docks and the sidewalk.
6. **Unit prices** for anything found during cutting: cracked or spalled units, loose brick at the parapet, rusted lintel steel.

That last line is what keeps a commercial tuckpointing job honest. A [unit-price versus lump-sum contract](/blog/unit-price-vs-lump-sum-masonry-contract) explains the trade-off in more detail.

## Signs Your Bridgeview Building Needs Commercial Tuckpointing

- Joints recessed more than a quarter inch, or sandy enough to rake out with a key.
- Open head joints on block walls, especially in the top three courses.
- White efflorescence or dark wet staining below the parapet after rain.
- Interior staining on the inside face of exterior walls in the warehouse.
- Daylight or drafts at the top of block walls in unheated areas.
- Cracked mortar stepping up from the corners of dock and overhead-door openings.

Our guide to [signs a commercial building needs tuckpointing](/blog/signs-commercial-building-needs-tuckpointing) covers these with photos.

## The Risk of Waiting

Open joints let water into the wall. On brick, that becomes spalled faces and loose units. On block, water fills the hollow cores and freezes. Under the parapet, it rusts the steel at the roof edge and works its way down to the office ceiling. Repointing costs a fraction of replacing brick, rebuilding block or replacing interior finishes — and it is the line on a capital plan that protects every other line.

## Our Process on an Operating Building

1. **Walk every elevation** and the roof edge, with the property manager or owner where possible.
2. **Written scope and pricing by elevation**, with phasing options.
3. **Staging plan** agreed around dock schedules, tenant entrances and fire lanes.
4. **Mock-up panel** for mortar colour and joint profile on the office face before production starts.
5. **Cut out** to specified depth with dust control; hand-raking around soft or damaged units.
6. **Repoint** in lifts, tooled to match the original profile.
7. **Cure and protect**, then clean the wall without damaging the brick face.
8. **Walkthrough, photos and closeout documents**, including lien waivers.

## Materials

Type N mortar on most brick office faces; Type S where the block wall and below-grade exposure call for it; lime-rich mixes on older soft brick. Mortar colour is matched to the weathered original, not to a fresh sample. Where joints meet window frames, storefront systems or expansion joints, those get sealant — not mortar.

## What Drives the Cost

- Total joint area actually cut out, and joint width.
- Wall height and access method.
- Working around live docks, storefronts and parking.
- Colour and profile match on the public face.
- Damaged units found during cutting (priced per unit, not guessed).
- Season — cold-weather work needs heated enclosures.
