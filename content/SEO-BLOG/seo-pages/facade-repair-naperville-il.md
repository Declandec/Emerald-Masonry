## Why facade repair in Naperville, IL is usually a veneer problem

Most of these façades are **brick veneer**: a single four-inch wythe of brick, an air space, and a backup wall of **steel stud** and sheathing or concrete block. The brick is not holding anything up. It hangs off the building on **wall ties** and sits, floor by floor, on steel **shelf angles** (also called relieving angles) bolted to the structure.

That arrangement only works if four things are right:

- **Soft joints** under every shelf angle — a sealant joint, not mortar, so the frame and the brick can move independently
- **Through-wall flashing** with end dams at every shelf angle, lintel and sill, draining out through **weep holes**
- **Corrosion-resistant ties** at the right spacing, fixed into studs rather than just sheathing
- **Sealant** at windows, expansion joints and dissimilar materials, replaced on schedule

When a Naperville façade fails, it is almost always one of those four — not the brick. [Brick veneer ties, shelf angles and hidden steel](/blog/brick-veneer-ties-shelf-angles-hidden-steel) and [solid masonry versus brick veneer repair](/blog/solid-masonry-vs-brick-veneer-repair) explain the anatomy.

## Signs your façade needs attention

- **Horizontal cracks or a bulge at floor lines** — the brick is bearing down on a shelf angle with no soft joint
- **Vertical cracks near corners and wall ends** — the veneer has no expansion joint where it needs one
- **Rust staining or a displaced course** along a shelf angle line
- **Interior leaks at window heads** or water stains at the top of a wall
- **Split, hardened or debonded sealant** at window perimeters, expansion joints and coping
- **Efflorescence** concentrated below windows and shelf angles — water is in the cavity and not draining
- **Cracked limestone** trim, coping or sills, or failed joints between stone pieces

## What waiting costs on a veneer wall

With solid masonry, water that gets in slowly works its way through. With veneer, water that gets past the brick lands in a **cavity full of steel** — studs, ties, screws and shelf angles. Corroded ties lose their grip; corroding shelf angles expand and push the brick above them out of plane. Wet sheathing and insulation behind a steel-stud wall can deteriorate for years before anyone sees it inside. A sealant replacement caught on time is maintenance; a bowing veneer panel over a sidewalk is an engineered repair with temporary shoring.

## How we repair it

1. **Diagnose from the wall, not the parking lot** — lift or stage access, probe joints, open selected inspection holes at shelf angles and window heads
2. **Map the condition by elevation** — what is sealant, what is flashing, what is steel, what is brick
3. **Open shelf angle lines** where needed: remove brick, clean and coat or replace corroded steel, install new flashing with end dams and drip edge, rebuild with a proper **soft joint** below the angle
4. **Retrofit ties** where existing ties have corroded or were never anchored into studs
5. **Cut out and replace sealant** at window perimeters and expansion joints — joint prep, primer, backer rod at the right depth, and a sealant matched to the substrates
6. **Repair limestone trim and coping** with dutchman repairs, pinning or replacement, and repoint with a compatible mortar
7. **Restore weeps** and leave a written record of what was found behind the brick
