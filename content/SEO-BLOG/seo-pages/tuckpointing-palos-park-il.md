## Why Canopy Wrecks One Wall and Not Another

Masonry damage is not caused by cold. It is caused by **water present in the wall when the temperature crosses freezing** — and Chicagoland delivers dozens of those crossings each winter.

A sunny south elevation dries within hours of a storm. A **north or east elevation under heavy canopy stays damp for days.** Same house, same weather, same mortar — but one wall enters every freeze cycle saturated and the other does not.

Three ways the tree cover compounds it:

**Slow drying.** Shade plus still air under canopy means moisture lingers in the masonry far longer.

**Clogged gutters.** Heavy leaf load overwhelms gutters, they overflow, and water runs down the wall — which is why so much local damage sits in a vertical band under one gutter section rather than spread evenly.

**Biological growth.** Algae, moss and lichen establish on persistently damp brick. The growth itself is mostly cosmetic; what it tells you is that the wall is staying wet.

**If you have a green-tinged elevation in Palos Park, look at the mortar joints on that wall first.** They are almost always the ones that have gone.

## What Tuckpointing Does

Mortar is engineered as the **sacrificial** part of a masonry wall — deliberately softer than the brick, so it absorbs movement and moisture stress and wears out first. Replacing mortar is cheap; replacing brick is not.

**Repointing is the structural half: grinding or raking failed mortar out to a sound depth, then packing fresh mortar in layers.** Tuckpointing, as most homeowners use the word, includes tooling that joint so the finished repair matches the profile the house was built with. We do both as one operation.

Once joints open, water runs behind the brick face instead of off it. It freezes there, expands about nine percent, and works the wall apart from the inside.

## Signs Your Palos Park Home Needs Tuckpointing

- **Mortar you can dig out with a screwdriver** — the fastest honest test there is.
- **Receded joints** sitting behind the brick face, having lost the depth needed to shed water.
- **Sandy grit** on sills, steps and along the foundation line.
- **Green or black film** on a shaded elevation — a moisture signal worth following.
- **Step cracks** running diagonally through the joints, often at corners.
- **Efflorescence** — chalky white bloom — meaning water is moving through the wall.
- **Spalling brick faces**, flaking or popping.
- **An open-jointed chimney**, exposed on four sides and always the fastest-failing masonry on the property.
- **Damage concentrated under one gutter run** — a classic canopy-and-leaf-load signature.

## Risks of Waiting

Deferred repointing does not stay a repointing problem. Open joints admit water; freeze-thaw then spalls the brick faces, corrodes the steel lintels over windows and doors, saturates the chimney, and eventually loosens brick outright. Each step up that ladder costs multiples of the one below, and repointing at the right time is by a wide margin the cheapest rung.

On a wooded lot the clock runs faster on the shaded side, which is exactly the elevation homeowners look at least.

## Our Process

1. **Free on-site assessment** — every elevation, plus chimney, outbuildings, sills, lintels, grade, gutters and downspouts.
2. **Mortar matching** — color, sand and, critically, hardness matched to your existing mortar.
3. **Grinding and raking** to a sound depth, typically two to two and a half times the joint width. Never skimmed over.
4. **Cleaning the joints** so new mortar bonds to clean masonry.
5. **Repointing in layers**, packed and compacted with no voids behind the face.
6. **Tooling** to match the original joint profile at the right firmness.
7. **Gentle cleaning** of biological growth where present — soft brushing and masonry-safe cleaners, never high-pressure washing, which strips the fired face off brick.
8. **Proper cure**, protected as conditions require.
