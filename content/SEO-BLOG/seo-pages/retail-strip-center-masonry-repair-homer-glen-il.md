That construction is why **strip center masonry repair in Homer Glen** is a different job from repointing a century-old building in Chicago. Very little here is failing because the mortar is old. It is failing because of how the building was detailed, coated and used.

> A strip center is two buildings sharing a roof: a dressed-up front elevation that tenants and shoppers look at, and a block rear wall that nobody does. On most centers we assess, the rear wall is where the water is actually getting in.

## What strip center masonry repair actually covers

On a typical Homer Glen retail center the masonry scope falls into five areas. Most assessments find problems in at least three of them.

| Area | Typical construction | What fails |
|---|---|---|
| Storefront elevation | Brick or manufactured stone veneer over block or steel stud | Open joints at piers, rusting lintels over openings, cracking at the sign band and canopy line, failed sealant at storefront frames |
| Rear and side walls | Single-wythe CMU, painted or elastomeric-coated | Control-joint cracking, coating failure, water straight through to tenant back rooms |
| Parapet and coping | Block parapet with metal or precast coping | Open coping joints, displaced caps, base flashing pulling away from the block |
| Site masonry | Dumpster enclosures, monument signs, planters, screen walls | Vehicle impact, salt damage, loose caps |
| Penetrations | Sign anchors, conduit, HVAC lines, downspout straps | Twenty years of tenant turnover drilled into the wall and never sealed |

## Why the rear wall leaks first

A brick-veneer storefront is a drained wall: water that gets past the brick is supposed to run down the cavity and out through flashing and weep holes. A **single-wythe concrete block rear wall** has no cavity. The only things keeping water out are the block itself, the mortar joints and whatever coating was rolled on.

Block is porous, and it shrinks. Builders handle the shrinkage with **control joints** — vertical breaks sealed with caulk. When that sealant ages out and the coating chalks and cracks, every control joint and every shrinkage crack becomes an open path into a tenant's stockroom, kitchen or restroom. Add an open coping joint above, and the parapet feeds water into the block core from the top.

The result is the call every retail property manager knows: a tenant complaint about a wet back wall, a roofer who finds nothing wrong with the roof, and a stain that keeps coming back.

## Signs your Homer Glen retail center needs masonry repair

- **Wet or stained interior back walls**, especially after wind-driven rain from the west.
- **Vertical cracks through the block** on the rear elevation, or sealant missing from control joints.
- **Peeling, blistering or chalking coating** on block walls.
- **Rust staining** below steel lintels over rear doors and storefront openings.
- **Cracks at the sign band and canopy line** on the front elevation.
- **Displaced coping or open joints** on the parapet when viewed from the roof.
- **Broken block at dumpster enclosures, loading corners and drive aisles.**
- **Efflorescence** — white salt bloom — on veneer piers near sidewalks treated with de-icer.

## Risks of waiting

On retail property, the masonry is rarely the expensive part of a leak. Damaged tenant inventory, mould remediation in a back room, a restaurant tenant's health inspection, a tenant withholding rent or refusing to renew — those are the costs. A cracked block wall left through two winters also lets freeze-thaw open the cracks wider, and a loose coping cap over a sidewalk becomes a liability issue rather than a maintenance one.

## Our strip center masonry repair process

1. **Walk every elevation and the roof.** Front, rear, sides, parapet, enclosures and site walls — photographed and marked on a site plan.
2. **Separate the causes.** Masonry, sealant, coating, roofing and drainage problems are identified separately so the right trade is paying for the right repair.
3. **Write the scope by building, elevation and item**, with quantities — so the owner can allocate it between common-area and tenant-specific work and phase it.
4. **Phase around tenants.** Store-by-store on the front, around delivery and trash schedules on the rear, with overhead protection at entrances.
5. **Repair:** rout and reseal control joints, repoint and replace damaged units, rebuild impact-damaged corners, reset or replace coping, replace rusted lintels, seal penetrations.
6. **Protect:** apply a breathable, compatible block coating or masonry sealer where the wall needs it, then hand over photos, quantities completed and lien waivers.

## Materials we use

Type N or Type S mortar matched to the existing block and veneer; replacement CMU matched for size and face; brick or manufactured stone matched to the storefront; urethane or hybrid sealant for control joints and storefront perimeters; galvanized or primed steel lintels; vapor-permeable elastomeric coatings for single-wythe block. We will not seal a block wall with a film that traps water inside it.

## What drives the cost of strip center masonry repair

No honest contractor prices a retail center over the phone. The cost depends on:

- **Linear footage** of each elevation and parapet.
- **Repair type** — sealant and repointing are far cheaper than unit replacement or rebuilding.
- **Coating condition** — spot repair versus full recoating of rear walls.
- **Access** — lifts in drive aisles and parking fields, work around parked cars and deliveries.
- **Phasing** — the more phases tenants need, the more mobilizations.
- **Hidden steel** — rusted lintels found once the work opens up.
