## What a lintel is and why steel lintels fail

A **lintel** is the beam that carries the brick over an opening. On most Naperville brick homes it is an **L-shaped steel angle** with the brick sitting on its horizontal leg. Older homes may also have brick arches or stone lintels.

Steel rusts when water reaches it through worn mortar joints, missing or failed flashing, or blocked weep holes. Rust takes up several times the space of the steel it came from, so a rusting lintel swells and pushes the brick above it upward. This is called **rust jacking**, and it causes most of the cracking we see over windows and garage doors.

## Signs you need lintel repair

- **Rust staining** or flaking steel along the top of a window, door or garage opening.
- **Horizontal cracks** along the lintel line or **stair-step cracks** from the corners.
- **Brick lifting** or a visible gap between the steel and the brick above.
- **Sagging brick** over a wide opening, especially garage doors.
- **Windows or doors that stick** after years of working fine.
- **Caulk at the top of the window** that keeps splitting.

## Risks of waiting

Rust doesn't stop on its own. Each year the steel loses strength and the brick above it cracks further, and water gets in through those cracks to the window frame and the wall. Wide garage openings can eventually sag. Replacing one lintel early is a contained job; waiting can mean rebuilding more brick, replacing damaged window trim and repairing interior water damage.

## How we repair lintels in Naperville

1. **Inspect every opening** and note which lintels are rusting, which are cracked and which are fine.
2. **Support the brick above** with temporary shoring.
3. **Remove the brick** directly above the opening and take out the old steel.
4. **Install new steel** sized to the opening, primed or galvanized, with proper bearing at each end.
5. **Install flashing** over the lintel, with end dams where needed, and open or add **weep holes**.
6. **Rebuild with matched brick** and mortar matched in color and joint profile.
7. **Seal** the joint between the masonry and the window or door frame, and walk you through the finished work.
