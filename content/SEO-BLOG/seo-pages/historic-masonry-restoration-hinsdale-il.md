## Why Historic Masonry Cannot Be Repaired Like Modern Masonry

There is one technical fact that governs everything about this work, and getting it wrong is the single most common way historic homes are damaged by well-meaning contractors.

**A masonry wall is designed so the mortar is softer than the brick.** Mortar is the sacrificial layer — it absorbs the movement and moisture and wears out first, because replacing mortar is cheap and replacing brick is not. Pre-1920 walls in Hinsdale were built with lime-rich mortar and softer, often handmade brick, and that relationship holds.

Modern Portland-based mortar is dramatically harder. Point a soft historic wall with it and the relationship inverts: the mortar becomes the strong element, the brick becomes the weak one, and every freeze-thaw cycle drives the stress into the brick faces instead of the joints. The result shows up in three to eight winters as **spalled, popped and delaminating brick** — permanent damage, replacing a cheap repair with an expensive one.

| | Historic wall (pre-1920) | Modern wall (post-war) |
|---|---|---|
| **Mortar** | Lime-rich, soft, breathable | Portland-based, hard, dense |
| **Brick** | Softer, often handmade, variable | Machine-made, dense, uniform |
| **Joint removal** | Hand-raked, careful, shallow tools | Power grinding acceptable |
| **Failure if mismatched** | Brick faces spall and are lost | Mortar erodes early |
| **Sealing** | Breathable only — walls must dry | Wider range of options |

## Signs a Hinsdale Historic Home Needs Masonry Restoration

- Mortar joints receded well behind the face of the brick, or joints that rake out as sand.
- **Spalling brick** — faces flaking, popping or delaminating, especially below sills and near grade.
- Cracked, eroded or displaced **limestone sills, lintels and water tables**.
- Open joints and deteriorated crowns on original chimneys, which on these houses are often tall, multi-flue and heavily exposed.
- **Efflorescence** blooming on interior or exterior masonry.
- Step cracks through joints at corners and openings.
- Prior repairs that stand out — wrong-color mortar, smeared joints, or hard gray mortar over soft historic brick, which is a repair that needs undoing.

## Our Historic Restoration Process

1. **Assessment and documentation.** We walk the building, photograph conditions, evaluate the existing mortar's composition and hardness, and identify prior inappropriate repairs.
2. **Mortar analysis and matching.** We formulate a mix matched to the original in hardness, color, sand and texture — soft enough to protect the brick, durable enough to serve.
3. **Careful joint preparation.** Joints are raked by hand where the brick is soft, using power tools only where the masonry can safely take it. Aggressive grinding on a soft historic wall widens joints permanently and damages brick arrises.
4. **Repointing in lifts.** Mortar is packed in layers and compacted, then tooled to the original joint profile while at the right firmness.
5. **Brick and stone repair.** Failed units are replaced with salvaged or reproduction matches; limestone is repaired with dutchman inserts or matched patching where possible rather than replaced.
6. **Chimney, parapet and trim work.** Original chimneys, coping and ornamental stone are restored to shed water properly.
7. **Gentle cleaning and breathable protection.** No sandblasting, no high-pressure washing, no film-forming sealers — all three damage historic masonry. Where protection is warranted, we use breathable products that let the wall dry.

## Materials We Use

**Lime-based and lime-modified mortars** custom-blended to the original; matched sands for true color; **salvaged and reproduction brick** sourced to match size, color and texture; **Indiana limestone** and matched patching compounds for sills, lintels and trim; and breathable, vapor-permeable masonry sealers only. ## Risks of Deferring Historic Masonry Work

On a historic wall, the risk of waiting is not just a bigger repointing bill — it is the permanent loss of original material. Once the face spalls off a hundred-year-old brick, that brick is gone, and every replacement unit is a compromise against the original. The same is true of eroded limestone detail. Restoration is always cheaper and always more authentic when it happens before the units themselves start failing.
