Brick replacement is different from brick repair. Repair re-secures and repoints brick that's still sound; replacement is for brick that's past saving — where the hard fired face has popped off, the unit has cracked through, or it's crumbling. Once brick reaches that point, leaving it in the wall only lets water spread the damage to its neighbors.

## What Brick Replacement Involves

Done properly, brick replacement is careful work:

- **Removing the failed brick** cleanly without damaging the surrounding sound brick
- **Sourcing matched replacements** in size, color, texture, and finish
- **Setting the new brick** with mortar matched in color, hardness, and tooling
- **Repointing the surrounding joints**, which are usually part of the problem
- **Correcting the root cause** — a rusting [lintel](/services/lintel-repair), failed [sealant](/services/caulking), missing weep holes, or water intrusion

That last step is what separates a lasting repair from one that fails again. Replacing brick without fixing why it failed just resets the clock on the same damage.

## Signs You Need Brick Replacement

- **Spalling** — brick faces flaking, pitting, popping, or crumbling away
- **Brick cracked through** the body of the unit, not just the joint
- **Loose, shifted, or bulging brick** you can move or that's pushing out of plane
- **Gaps** where whole bricks have failed or fallen out
- **Rust staining and cracking above windows and doors** — a corroding lintel lifting the brick

## The Risk of Waiting

A spalled or failed brick is an open door for water. Once the hard outer face is gone, the soft core soaks up moisture far faster than sound brick, and every Kane County freeze-thaw cycle pushes the deterioration into the surrounding brick and mortar. A few failed bricks become a spreading patch; a small matched repair becomes a section rebuild. On older Elgin brick especially, the longer you wait, the harder the matching gets and the larger the repair becomes.

## Materials and Matching

Brick styles and colors change over the decades, so matching older Elgin brick takes real attention — the wrong choice stands out permanently. We match dimensions, color range, and surface texture, and we match mortar in color, sand, tooling, and hardness. A too-hard modern mortar against softer historic brick traps water and causes more spalling, so on Elgin's older masonry we're deliberate about using a compatible, softer mix. For structural elements behind the brick, like lintels, we use corrosion-resistant materials.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### When does brick need to be replaced instead of repaired in Elgin?
Brick that has spalled — lost its hard outer face — must be replaced, because the exposed core absorbs water and deteriorates fast. Cracked or loose brick can sometimes be re-secured and repointed. We assess each affected area in your Elgin home and replace only what truly needs it, then fix the underlying cause.

### Can you match the replacement brick to my Elgin home?
Yes — matching is the craft in this work. We source replacement brick to match size, color, texture, and finish as closely as possible, and match mortar color and tooling. On Elgin's older and historic brick, careful matching is exactly where we focus so the repair blends in.

### What causes brick to spall and fail in Elgin?
The main driver is water plus freeze-thaw: moisture enters through failed mortar, cracked sealant, or porous brick, then freezes and pops the face. Rusting steel lintels, hard mortar against soft historic brick, and building movement also fail brick. We diagnose the cause so the replacement actually lasts.
