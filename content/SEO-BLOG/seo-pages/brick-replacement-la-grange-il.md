Two things decide whether the result looks right: **how much brick really needs replacing**, and **how well it is matched**.

### How much needs replacing

| Condition of the brick | Typical approach |
|---|---|
| Sound brick, worn joints | Tuckpointing only, no replacement |
| Scattered spalled or cracked units | Spot replacement, brick by brick |
| Clusters of damage near downspouts, steps or the base | Replace the cluster and fix the water source |
| Loose brick at corners, chimneys or parapets | Take down and relay the section |
| Bulging or cracked-through areas | Section rebuild, with an engineer's view if movement is active |

### How brick is matched

- **Size**: older brick is often a different size than current modular brick, so a match has to fit the existing courses.
- **Color and range**: most walls have a blend of shades, not one color.
- **Texture**: smooth, wire-cut or sand-struck faces read differently.
- **Weathering**: a century of exposure softens color, so new brick may need to be chosen a shade off to blend in.
- **Mortar**: the joint color, sand and profile must match too, or the patch still shows.

## Signs your La Grange home needs brick replacement

- **Brick faces flaking or popping off**, especially low on walls, near downspouts or on chimneys.
- **Bricks cracked through**, not only along the joints.
- **Loose bricks** that move when pushed.
- **Old patches** in the wrong brick or gray cement that stand out.
- **Damage concentrated under a painted or sealed area**, where moisture has been trapped.

## Risks of waiting

Once a brick face spalls, the softer inside of the brick is exposed and absorbs even more water. Each winter the damage deepens and spreads to the units around it. Left long enough, spot replacement turns into a section rebuild, and loose brick at chimneys or parapets becomes a falling hazard.

## How we replace brick in La Grange

1. **Survey the wall** and mark which bricks need replacement and which only need repointing.
2. **Identify the cause**, such as downspouts, failed sills, hard mortar or a sealer.
3. **Source and show the match**: current or salvaged brick, and a mortar sample.
4. **Cut out damaged brick** carefully so surrounding units are not disturbed.
5. **Clean and prepare** each opening.
6. **Set new brick** in compatible mortar, matched in color, strength and joint profile.
7. **Rebuild sections** where needed, with ties, flashing and weep holes.
8. **Tool and clean** the joints to blend in and walk through the work with you.

## Materials we use

Matched current brick or salvaged brick of a similar era; lime-rich or Type N mortar for older, softer brick, chosen so the mortar is never harder than the brick; masonry ties and flashing where sections are rebuilt; and gentle cleaners only. We do not use hard cement mortars, cement surface patches, or film-forming sealers on old brick.
