# Foundation Masonry Repair in Frankfort, IL

Emerald Masonry LLC provides foundation masonry repair in Frankfort, IL — repairing step cracks in block foundation walls, cracked brick at the brick ledge, failed parging, window wells and the older stone foundations around the historic downtown. We tell you plainly when a crack needs a structural engineer before it needs a mason. Licensed, bonded and insured; call **(708) 288-1696** for a free on-site assessment.

Frankfort's housing splits into two very different foundations.

Most homes are in the subdivisions built from the 1990s through the 2000s — **poured concrete or concrete block basements and crawlspaces**, with brick veneer sitting on a brick ledge formed into the top of the wall. Underneath them is Will County's **glacial clay**, which swells when it is wet, shrinks in a dry August, and pushes against basement walls when it freezes and when the sump cannot keep up.

Around the old downtown along the Old Plank Road Trail, the older houses and commercial buildings often sit on **stone foundations laid in lime mortar**, a century or more older than the subdivisions and repaired, over the years, with whatever was at hand.

The work on each is different. So is the question of when to call an engineer.

## What foundation masonry repair covers in Frankfort

- **Block foundation step cracks** — repointing cracked bed and head joints, replacing broken block faces, restoring the parge coat
- **Brick ledge and veneer cracks** above grade — cutting out cracked brick, relaying units, rebuilding mortar joints and flashing where it is missing
- **Parging** — the cementitious skin over exposed foundation, which spalls when water gets behind it
- **Window wells** — masonry wells, block surrounds and the foundation openings they protect
- **Stone foundations** — repointing in compatible lime mortar, resetting loose stone, rebuilding failed sections
- **Steps and stoops** bearing on the foundation

Poured-concrete crack injection is a specialist trade of its own; on a poured wall our work is usually the brick ledge, veneer and parging above it, and we will tell you if the crack itself needs injection or an engineer.

## Reading foundation cracks: what each pattern means

### Stair-step cracks in block

A crack that steps through the mortar joints of a block wall is the most common pattern in Frankfort basements. If the wall is **plumb** and the crack is narrow and even, it is usually settlement or shrinkage and is a masonry repair. If the wall has a **horizontal crack** along a course or is visibly bowing inward, that is soil pressure, and it is engineer first. Our guide to [step cracks in brick and block walls](/blog/step-cracks-in-brick-walls-chicago) shows the difference.

### Cracks at the brick ledge

Veneer cracking that starts at the brick ledge is often not the foundation at all — it is thermal movement, rusting steel over a garage or window opening, missing expansion joints on a long wall, or seasonal settlement of the slab beside it. A crack that runs continuously from the foundation up through several courses of brick is the one that deserves a closer look.

### Spalling parge and efflorescence

White salts and flaking parge on the exposed foundation mean water is moving through the wall — usually a grading, gutter or downspout issue first. [Parging and foundation coatings](/blog/parging-foundation-coating-chicago) covers when re-parging is the fix and when it just hides the cause.

## When a structural engineer comes first

We would rather say this plainly than have a homeowner pay twice. **Call an engineer before a mason** when you see:

- A block wall **bowing or leaning** inward
- A **horizontal crack** running along a block course
- A crack that is **wider at one end** or measurably growing between seasons
- **Doors and windows binding**, or floors visibly out of level
- Veneer separating from the house at a corner

An engineer decides whether the wall needs bracing, anchoring or rebuilding. We then carry out the masonry to their repair plan. [When masonry needs a structural engineer](/blog/when-masonry-needs-a-structural-engineer-il) explains the hand-off.

## Risks of waiting

Clay soil does not give a foundation crack a quiet year. Each wet spring and each freeze puts the same pressure on the same weak joint, so a stable step crack gradually becomes a displaced one, and an open brick-ledge crack lets water into the veneer cavity where it rusts ties and lintels. The repair that is a few days of repointing in year one can be a section rebuild in year five.

## Our process

1. **Walk the exterior and the basement or crawlspace**, mapping every crack with width and pattern
2. **Check grading, gutters, downspouts and window wells** — the water source usually sits outside the wall
3. **Flag anything that needs an engineer** before quoting the masonry
4. **Written scope** with quantities: joints to repoint, units to replace, parging area, well and stoop work
5. **Repair** with matched mortar and brick, flashing restored where it was missing
6. **Photographs before and after**, so you have a baseline for the next spring

## Materials

On block and brick we use **Type N or Type S mortar** depending on exposure and below-grade contact, matched to the existing colour where it shows. On the old downtown stone foundations we use a **lime-rich mortar** softer than the stone — hard cement on old stone traps moisture and accelerates the very deterioration you are trying to stop.
