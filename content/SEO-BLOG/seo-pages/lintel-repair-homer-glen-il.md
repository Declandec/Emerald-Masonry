## What a Lintel Is — and Why It Fails

A lintel is the horizontal support that carries the brick across the top of an opening. On almost every brick home in Homer Glen it's a steel angle iron tucked behind the brick above each window, door, and garage. You don't see it, but it's doing real structural work every day.

The problem is that most lintels are bare or only lightly painted steel. Water works its way behind the brick, sits on top of that steel angle, and has nowhere to drain. Over years of Will County freeze-thaw cycles, the steel corrodes — and here's the part that damages your house: **rust takes up more space than the steel it came from.** As the lintel rusts, it swells and lifts, jacking the brick course above it upward. Masons call this **rust jacking**, and it's the mechanism behind almost every cracked, sagging opening we're called out to fix.

## Warning Signs You Need Lintel Repair

Most homeowners spot a lintel problem from the ground. Look above your windows, doors, and garage for:

- **Orange or brown rust stains** streaking down the brick beneath an opening — usually the first sign.
- **A horizontal crack** in the mortar joint directly above a window, door, or garage.
- **Sagging, stepped, or dropped brick** across the top of an opening.
- **Gaps opening up at the ends** of the lintel, where the steel bears on the brick.
- **A visibly bowed, swollen, or flaking steel angle** peeking out above the opening.

Any one of these means water is already reaching the steel. Catching it early is the difference between a clean repair and a rebuild.

## The Risk of Waiting

A failing lintel doesn't heal — it accelerates. The longer rusted steel sits behind your brick, the more it expands, the more brick it displaces, and the more water it lets into the wall behind the facade. What starts as a stainable, savable lintel becomes a full replacement with a large section of brick to rebuild. On a wide garage span, waiting can turn a straightforward repair into a major masonry project. Addressing it while the steel is only surface-rusted is always the cheaper path.

## Repair or Replace: How We Decide

Not every lintel needs to be torn out. During the free estimate we assess the actual condition of the steel:

- **Repairable:** if the steel is still straight, solid, and only surface-rusted, we save it — wire-wheel the corrosion back to sound metal, apply a rust-converting primer, repaint it, and reseal the joint above so water stops collecting there.
- **Replacement:** if the steel is scaling, swollen with rust, or sagging, it has lost the strength to carry the brick and must be replaced.

When replacement is the honest answer, we temporarily support and remove the brick above the opening, take out the failed steel, set a new galvanized and primed lintel with proper end bearing on each side, and reset the brick with matched mortar. Where it belongs, we address flashing and weep drainage so the new lintel sheds water instead of trapping it — the whole point is to keep the replacement from rusting the way the original did.
