## Why tuckpointing in Evergreen Park is usually wall-by-wall

Mortar wears out from water and freeze-thaw cycles, so it fails fastest where the wall gets wettest. On the homes we see in Evergreen Park, that pattern is consistent:

| Part of the house | Why it fails first | What we usually find |
|---|---|---|
| **Chimney above the roof** | Exposed on all four sides, no eave protection | Open joints, loose top courses, cracked crown |
| **West and south walls** | Take the most wind-driven rain and sun cycling | Recessed, sandy joints across the elevation |
| **Front porch and steps** | Splash-back, de-icing salt, standing water | Crumbling joints and loose brick at the base |
| **Under downspouts and failed gutters** | Concentrated water running down the face | A vertical strip of eroded mortar |
| **North and east walls under eaves** | Sheltered, dry for longer | Often still sound |

Many competitor pages sell tuckpointing as a whole-house job. Sometimes it is. But often the honest answer is to repoint the chimney and one or two elevations now, and look at the rest again in a few years.

## Signs your Evergreen Park home needs tuckpointing

- **Mortar recessed** a quarter-inch or more behind the brick face.
- **Sandy, crumbling joints** that scrape out with a key or screwdriver.
- **Stair-step cracks** running through the mortar joints.
- **Loose bricks** at the chimney top, porch corners and garage door edges.
- **Daylight or sand** at chimney joints, seen from the attic.
- **Water stains** on interior plaster below windows or along the chimney breast.

## Risks of waiting

Open joints let rain into the wall. In a Chicago-area winter that water freezes and thaws many times, widening the gaps and starting to break the brick faces. The longer a failed joint is left, the more likely the repair turns from tuckpointing into [brick repair](/services/brick-repair) or a chimney rebuild. Catching the worst wall early is almost always cheaper than waiting until every wall needs it.

## How we tuckpoint Evergreen Park homes

1. **Walk every elevation.** We check each wall, the chimney, the porch and the garage, and tell you which need work now and which can wait.
2. **Identify the original mortar and joint.** Colour, sand, strength and the joint profile (concave, flush, raked or struck) are recorded before anything is cut.
3. **Make sample patches** so you can approve the colour once cured.
4. **Cut out joints** to a uniform depth, usually at least twice the joint width, without chipping brick edges.
5. **Repoint in layers** so deep joints are packed solid.
6. **Tool the joints** to the original profile so repaired walls match the rest of the house.
7. **Clean and cure**, brushing mortar off the brick faces and protecting the work from fast drying and frost.

## Materials

We generally use Type N or a softer lime-rich mortar matched to the brick, never a mortar harder than the brick itself. The colour is built from sand and pigment to match the existing joints, not a stock grey. Where a brick has already broken up, we replace it with salvaged or closely matched brick. We do not seal brick over fresh mortar; if water management is the issue, see our [masonry sealing](/services/masonry-sealing) service for when sealing helps and when it doesn't.
