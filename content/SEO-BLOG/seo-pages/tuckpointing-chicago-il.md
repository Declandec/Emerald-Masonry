## What Tuckpointing and Repointing Actually Are

Brick walls don't usually fail because of the brick — they fail because of the mortar between the brick. Mortar is the sacrificial layer that takes on Chicago's freeze-thaw cycles and lake-effect moisture, and over decades it recedes, cracks and turns sandy. **Tuckpointing** (also called **repointing**) is the process of removing that deteriorated mortar to a sound depth and filling the joints with fresh mortar.

Technically, repointing is the structural repair — cutting out the bad mortar and packing in new. Tuckpointing traditionally adds a crisp finish line for appearance. In everyday Chicago use, both words mean the same thing: restoring your mortar joints so the wall sheds water instead of soaking it up. Done right, it's one of the highest-value repairs a masonry building can get.

## Signs You Need Tuckpointing

Chicago's climate is hard on masonry, and the warning signs tend to show up in the same order. Watch for:

- **Receding, crumbling or sandy joints** — if you can dig mortar out with a key or screwdriver, it's failing.
- **Gaps and hairline cracks** in the mortar lines.
- **Spalling brick** — faces flaking, popping or crumbling as trapped water freezes and expands.
- **Efflorescence** — white, chalky staining that means water is moving through the wall.
- **Water in the basement** or damp interior walls after rain or snowmelt.

> If you can scratch mortar out of a joint with your house key, that joint is no longer protecting your brick. That's the moment to call for tuckpointing.

## The Risk of Waiting

Failing mortar is a small problem that becomes an expensive one. Once joints open up, water gets behind the brick, freezes, and pushes the masonry apart. Spalled and cracked brick can't be repointed — it has to be cut out and replaced, which costs far more than mortar. Left long enough, water migrates to lintels, sills and the foundation, and a straightforward tuckpointing job turns into structural repair. Repointing on schedule is the cheapest way to protect a Chicago masonry building.

## Materials: The Right Mortar for Chicago Brick

Mortar choice is where a lot of tuckpointing goes wrong. **Type N** mortar is softer and flexible; **Type S** is harder and stronger. That does not mean stronger is always better. Much of Chicago's older brick — especially soft **Chicago common brick** on the sides and backs of bungalows, greystones and two-flats — is relatively soft and needs a softer mortar to move with it.

> Use a too-hard mortar on soft historic brick and the mortar wins — the brick face cracks and spalls instead. On old Chicago brick, matching mortar hardness matters as much as matching color.

We match **color, joint profile and hardness** so the repair blends in and protects the brick rather than damaging it. On harder face brick and commercial structures, a Type S mortar is often correct — the point is that the mortar is chosen for your building, not applied one-size-fits-all.
