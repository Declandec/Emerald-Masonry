## Lintel repair in Batavia, IL

A lintel is the horizontal steel angle that spans the top of a window, door or garage opening and carries the weight of the brick above it. **Lintel repair means restoring or replacing that steel support — and rebuilding the brick it holds up — once corrosion, cracking or sagging sets in.** If you are seeing rust stains or cracks above an opening on your Batavia home or building, the lintel underneath is almost certainly the cause, and it will only get worse until it is addressed. ## Signs your lintel is failing

Lintel problems are easy to spot once you know where to look. Above your windows, doors and garage openings, watch for:

- **Orange or brown rust stains** streaking down the brick or mortar right below the lintel line.
- **Stair-step cracks** climbing diagonally through the mortar joints above the opening.
- **Open gaps** in the horizontal joint directly over the window or door.
- **A sagging or dropping brick course** — the row of brick just above the opening no longer sits level.
- **Bulging or lifting brick**, where the masonry looks pushed outward or upward.
- **Chipped or spalling brick faces** near the corners of the opening.

Any one of these is worth a look. Several together mean the steel is actively deteriorating.

## The rust jacking mechanism

Most lintel failures come down to one process: **rust jacking**. The steel lintel is hidden inside the wall, so moisture that gets past the brick and mortar sits against it. Over time the steel rusts — and here is the key part: **rust occupies far more space than the solid steel it forms from, expanding to several times the original thickness.** That expansion has to go somewhere. Because the steel is trapped inside the wall, the growing rust physically pushes up and out against the brick resting on the lintel. That is what lifts the brick course, opens the mortar joints and drives the stair-step cracking. The rust does not just weaken the support — it actively pries your masonry apart.

## Why waiting makes it worse

A rusting lintel does not stabilize on its own. Once the mortar joints open and the brick lifts, more water gets into the wall, which accelerates the corrosion, which lifts the brick further — a loop that keeps compounding. What starts as a cosmetic rust stain can progress to loose, displaced brick, a compromised opening and eventually falling masonry over a doorway or walkway. Catching it while it is still a lintel-and-a-few-bricks repair is far less involved and less costly than waiting until a section of wall has to be rebuilt.

## Our lintel repair process

Every job is a little different, but the sequence is consistent:

1. **Assess and shore the opening.** We evaluate the lintel and the surrounding brick, then temporarily support the opening so the wall stays safe while we work.
2. **Remove the affected brick course.** We carefully take out the brick sitting on the failed lintel, salvaging sound units where we can for a matched rebuild.
3. **Swap or treat the lintel.** Heavily corroded steel is replaced with a new lintel. Where the steel is still sound, we clean it back, treat it and protect it against further rust.
4. **Rebuild and repoint.** We reset the brick on the new or restored lintel, tie it back in, and repoint the joints so the repair blends into the existing wall.

## Materials we use

We install **galvanized or painted steel lintels** sized to the span and load of the opening, so the new support resists corrosion far better than the bare steel it replaces. Brick is rebedded and the joints are finished with **Type N or Type S mortar**, matched to the wall so the repair holds up and looks right. On older brick-and-limestone buildings we take extra care to match the mortar profile and color to the original masonry.

## What affects the price

There is no flat rate for lintel work — the cost depends on your specific situation. The main factors are:

- **Number of openings** that need work.
- **Length and size of the lintel** required for each span.
- **How much brick** has to be removed and rebuilt.
- **Access** — first-floor windows versus upper stories or tight side yards.
- **Matching** older or specialty brick and limestone.
