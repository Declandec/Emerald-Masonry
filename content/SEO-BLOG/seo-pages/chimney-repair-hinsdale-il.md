## Pre-war chimneys: tall, decorative and soft

Older Hinsdale homes often have chimneys that are a feature of the house: corbelled tops, decorative brickwork, multiple flues, clay chimney pots, and stacks that rise well above steep slate or tile roofs. They take weather on every side, every winter, with no shelter.

These chimneys were laid in soft, lime-rich mortar. Repointing them with hard modern mortar is one of the most common mistakes we see, because it traps water in the brick and makes the faces spall. We match the original mortar's colour, sand and softness, and use salvaged or closely matched brick when units need replacing.

## Teardown-era chases: a shell that has to shed water

Many newer Hinsdale homes have big masonry or stone chimney chases. Often the flue inside is a manufactured metal system, and the brick or stone is a veneer around a framed or block chase. That masonry has one job: keep water out. When it leaks, the cause is usually one of these:

- **The crown or chase cover** is flat, cracked, or has no overhang, so water runs straight down the face.
- **Flashing** at the roof line was face-nailed or caulked rather than counter-flashed into the mortar joints.
- **Stone veneer** was set without a drainage gap or weeps, so water behind it has no way out.
- **Mortar joints** at the top of the chase have opened from freeze-thaw.

## Signs your Hinsdale chimney needs repair

- **Loose or missing bricks** at the top of the stack, or mortar on the roof.
- **Open, sandy or recessed joints**, often worst on the west and south faces.
- **A cracked or crumbling crown**, or a cap that has rusted or pulled loose.
- **Water stains** on ceilings or walls next to the chimney, or in the attic.
- **White efflorescence or spalled brick faces** on the stack.
- **A lean** or a gap where the chimney meets the roof or the side wall.
- **Loose or cracked chimney pots** on an older stack.

## Risks of waiting

A chimney is the most exposed masonry on the house. Small open joints become loose top courses in a winter or two, and a loose top is a falling-brick hazard above a roof, a driveway or a walk. Leaks at the flashing rot roof decking and framing long before they show inside. Repointing and a new crown cost far less than a rebuild, so the time to call is when joints are open, not when bricks are loose.

## How we repair chimneys in Hinsdale

1. **Inspect from the roof.** We look at the crown, cap, flashing, mortar, brick and lean, and note the roof material so we protect it.
2. **Identify the type.** Solid pre-war stack or veneer chase, because that changes the repair.
3. **Find the water.** Crown, flashing, mortar or veneer drainage. We repair the cause, not just the stain.
4. **Repoint** open joints with mortar matched in colour and hardness, never harder than the brick.
5. **Replace or rebuild** spalled brick or loose top courses, using salvaged or matched brick on older stacks.
6. **Form a new crown** with a proper overhang and drip edge, or install a correctly sized chase cover.
7. **Re-flash** with step and counter flashing let into the mortar joints, coordinating with your roofer on slate or tile.

## Materials we use

Lime-rich or **Type N** mortar matched to the original on pre-war chimneys; salvaged or closely matched face brick; reinforced crown mortar or a precast crown with a drip edge; stainless or copper flashing on older homes; and breathable water repellent only where a chimney genuinely needs it. We do not coat chimneys with film-forming sealers.
