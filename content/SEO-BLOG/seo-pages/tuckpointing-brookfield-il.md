## What Tuckpointing Actually Is

Tuckpointing (also called repointing) is the repair of the **mortar joints** between brick — not the brick itself. Over decades the mortar weathers, cracks, and erodes back from the face of the wall, opening the joints to water. Tuckpointing means cutting that failed mortar out to a sound depth and packing in fresh mortar, finished to match the original joint profile.

Done right, it is the single most important maintenance a brick building gets. Done wrong — with the wrong mortar or a sloppy grind — it traps water and damages the brick it was supposed to protect.

## Signs Your Brookfield Home Needs Tuckpointing

Walk the walls and chimney and look for:

- **Crumbling, sandy mortar** — if a key or screwdriver scrapes it out easily, the joint has failed.
- **Recessed or missing joints** — gaps where mortar has eroded back or fallen out entirely.
- **Hairline cracks running through the mortar** — often in stair-step patterns near corners and openings.
- **Damp interior walls, musty smells, or stained plaster** — water finding its way in through open joints.
- **Spalling brick** — flaking faces, which often means water has been sitting in failed joints through freeze-thaw winters.
- **A chimney with visible gaps** — chimneys weather first because they are exposed on all four sides and the top.

## The Risk of Waiting

Open mortar joints are not a cosmetic problem — they are an entry point. Once water is moving into the wall, Brookfield's freeze-thaw winters expand it, widening the joints and starting to pop the faces off the brick. What begins as a wall that "needs repointing" becomes a wall that needs brick replacement, and eventually one with damaged ties, wet interior framing, and a far larger bill. Repointing on time is cheap insurance against all of that.

## Our Tuckpointing Process

1. **Assess the wall.** We determine how much actually needs repointing — a full wall, a chimney, or isolated joints — and identify any brick or lintel issues hiding behind the mortar.
2. **Grind out the failed mortar.** We cut the joints to a sound depth (typically about an inch) without nicking the brick edges, which is where rushed jobs ruin a wall.
3. **Match the mortar.** We mix mortar to match the original color and strength — critical on Brookfield's older lime-rich walls.
4. **Repoint and tool the joints.** We pack the mortar in fully and finish it to the original joint profile so the repair disappears into the wall.
5. **Clean down.** We leave the brick face clean, with no mortar smears or haze.

## Mortar Matching — Why Strength Matters as Much as Color

The mistake that wrecks old brick is repointing it with modern, high-Portland mortar. Brick laid in Brookfield in the 1920s through 1950s was set in softer, lime-rich mortar designed to be **weaker** than the brick, so the joint — not the brick — absorbs movement and freeze-thaw stress. Repoint that wall with a hard mortar and every winter the expansion has nowhere to go but the brick faces, which spall. We match color so the repair is invisible and strength so it is durable.

## What Affects the Price

We will not pretend a per-square-foot number means anything sight unseen. The real cost drivers are:

- **Extent** — a few failing joints versus a whole elevation or every joint on a chimney.
- **Height and access** — ground-floor work versus second-story walls or a chimney that needs staging or a lift.
- **Joint condition** — lightly weathered joints versus deeply eroded ones that need extra cutting and fill.
- **Match difficulty** — a straightforward gray match versus a tinted, tooled-profile match on a historic wall.

**How do I know if my Brookfield home needs tuckpointing or just a small repair?**

If you can rake a key or screwdriver into the mortar and it crumbles to sand, or you see gaps, hairline recesses, and missing mortar across a wall, that wall needs repointing. **Will the new mortar match my older Brookfield brick?**
