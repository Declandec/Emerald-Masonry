## What Brick Repair Actually Covers

**Brick repair in Naperville, IL** is the targeted repair or replacement of damaged brick units and the mortar around them, restoring both the wall's appearance and its ability to keep water out. It covers several distinct problems that often show up together:

- **Spalling brick** — the face of the brick flaking, popping, or crumbling away, exposing the softer interior.
- **Cracked brick** — vertical, diagonal, or "step" cracks running through brick and mortar.
- **Loose or shifting brick** — units that have lost their mortar bond and moved.
- **Missing brick** — gaps where brick has fallen out entirely.
- **Brick replacement** — swapping out brick that is too far gone to save.

### The building science behind the damage

Nearly all of it comes back to water and temperature. Brick and mortar are slightly porous, so they absorb moisture. When that trapped water freezes, it expands roughly nine percent — and Naperville runs through dozens of freeze-thaw cycles every winter. Each cycle pushes microscopic cracks a little wider. Over years, that pressure blows the face off a brick (spalling) or breaks the bond between brick and mortar (loose brick and cracking). Failing mortar joints accelerate everything, because open joints are an open door for water. That's why good brick repair and [tuckpointing](/services/tuckpointing) so often go together — you fix the brick and close the joints that were feeding the problem.

## Signs You Need Brick Repair in Naperville

Homeowners usually notice one of these first:

- **Flaking or spalling faces** — brick surfaces shedding, with sandy debris collecting below.
- **Step cracks** — cracks that climb the wall following the mortar joints in a staircase pattern, a classic sign of movement or settlement.
- **Loose, hollow, or missing brick** — especially at corners, chimneys, and near grade.
- **White, chalky efflorescence** — mineral salts left behind by water moving through the masonry.
- **Gaps and receding mortar** — joints you can slide a key into.

If you can see any of these from the driveway, there is more happening inside the wall than on its surface.

## The Risk of Waiting

Masonry damage is progressive, and it does not pause between seasons. A single cracked joint lets water reach the brick behind it; that brick spalls; the spalled area lets in more water, which reaches the next brick. What could have been a modest repair in the fall becomes a larger section — sometimes a partial rebuild — a year or two later. Water that gets past the outer wythe can also reach framing, insulation, and interior finishes. Repairing early is almost always the cheaper path, and it keeps the damage contained to brick rather than letting it become a structural or water-intrusion problem.

## Materials We Use

The materials make the difference between a repair that lasts and one that fails in a few years. We use **matched replacement brick**, and we choose the mortar deliberately — typically **Type N** for standard residential brick, and a softer mix on older, softer brick so the mortar, not the brick, takes the stress of expansion. On the wrong-hardness mortar, modern high-strength mixes can actually crack historic brick. Where sealing is warranted, we use breathable, vapor-permeable sealers rather than film-forming coatings that trap water behind them. If damage is extensive, we'll talk through [brick replacement](/services/brick-replacement) for the affected area.
