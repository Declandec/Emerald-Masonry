## The One Rule That Governs Historic Masonry

Everything about this work follows from a single piece of building science, and getting it wrong is the most common way old buildings are damaged by well-intentioned contractors.

**A masonry wall is designed so the mortar is softer than the brick.** Mortar is the sacrificial layer — it absorbs movement and moisture stress and wears out first, because mortar is cheap to replace and 170-year-old brick is irreplaceable.

Modern **Portland-based mortar** is dramatically harder than the lime mortar these walls were laid with. Point a soft historic wall with it and the relationship inverts: the joints become the strong element, the brick becomes the weak one, and every freeze-thaw cycle drives stress into the brick faces. The result shows up within three to eight winters as **spalled, popped and delaminating brick** — a cheap repair converted into an expensive and permanent loss.

| | Historic wall (pre-1920) | Modern wall (post-war) |
|---|---|---|
| **Mortar** | Lime-rich, soft, breathable | Portland-based, hard, dense |
| **Brick** | Softer, often handmade, variable | Machine-made, dense, uniform |
| **Joint preparation** | Hand-raked, careful, shallow tools | Power grinding acceptable |
| **Failure if mismatched** | Brick faces spall and are lost forever | Mortar erodes early |
| **Cleaning** | Gentle only — no blasting, no high pressure | Wider tolerance |
| **Sealing** | Breathable products only | Wider range of options |

## Signs a Historic Naperville Building Needs Restoration

- Mortar joints receded well behind the brick face, or joints that rake out as loose sand.
- **Spalling brick** — faces flaking, popping or delaminating, worst below sills and near grade.
- Cracked, eroded or displaced **limestone sills, lintels, water tables and foundation stone**.
- Open joints and deteriorated crowns on original chimneys, which on these houses are tall and heavily exposed.
- **Efflorescence** blooming on interior or exterior masonry.
- Step cracks through joints at corners and around openings.
- **Prior inappropriate repairs** — hard gray Portland mortar smeared over soft historic brick, wrong-color patches, or a previously sandblasted facade. These are repairs that need undoing.

## Our Restoration Process

1. **Assessment and documentation.** We walk the building, photograph conditions, evaluate the existing mortar's hardness and composition, and flag prior bad repairs.
2. **Mortar analysis and matching.** A mix formulated to the original — soft enough to protect the masonry, durable enough to serve.
3. **Hand joint preparation.** Joints raked by hand where the brick is soft, with power tools used only where masonry can safely take them. Aggressive grinding on a soft wall permanently widens joints and chews the brick edges.
4. **Repointing in lifts**, packed and compacted, then tooled to the original joint profile at the right firmness.
5. **Brick and stone repair.** Failed units replaced with salvaged or reproduction matches; limestone restored with dutchman inserts or matched patching rather than replaced wholesale.
6. **Chimneys, parapets and trim** restored to shed water properly.
7. **Gentle cleaning, breathable protection.** No sandblasting, no high-pressure washing, no film-forming sealers — all three damage historic masonry permanently.

## What Drives Restoration Cost in Naperville

- **Scope** — one elevation, a full facade, or the whole envelope including chimneys and parapets.
- **Mortar matching complexity** — a custom historic blend is more work than an off-the-shelf mortar.
- **Hand work required** — soft brick means hand-raking, which is slower than grinding by design.
- **Access and staging** — tall Italianates, steep roofs and downtown storefronts each need different setups.
- **Sourcing** — salvaged period brick and matched stone are not always immediately available.
- **Undoing prior bad repairs** — removing hard mortar from soft brick without further loss is delicate, time-consuming work.
- **Review and permitting** — landmark or district review adds documentation.

## Why Deferring Costs More on a Historic Building

On a 150-year-old wall, waiting is not just a larger repointing bill later — it is the permanent loss of original material. Once the face spalls off a handmade brick, that brick is gone, and every replacement is a compromise against the original. The same applies to eroded limestone detail and to carved trim. Restoration is always cheaper, and always more authentic, when it happens before the units themselves begin failing.
