## What Tuckpointing and Repointing Actually Are

Tuckpointing (often called repointing) is the process of removing deteriorated mortar from between brick or stone and replacing it with fresh, matched mortar. The work has three core steps:

- **Grinding out failed mortar** — we cut out the old, soft, or crumbling mortar to a consistent depth without nicking the brick faces.
- **Repacking the joints** — we pack new mortar firmly into the cleaned joints in layers so there are no voids behind the surface.
- **Tooling the profile** — we strike the joints to match the original profile (concave, grapevine, flush, raked, or weathered) so the repair disappears into the wall.

> Tuckpointing isn't cosmetic patching. It rebuilds the weather seal that keeps water out of your wall and out of your basement.

"Repointing" technically refers to the grind-and-repack step, while traditional "tuckpointing" adds a fine contrasting fillet line for a crisp finished look. In practice, around Clarendon Hills the two words are used interchangeably — and either way the goal is the same: a wall that sheds water and looks right.

## Signs Your Clarendon Hills Home Needs Tuckpointing

Older brick and stone homes don't fail all at once — the mortar goes first. Look for:

- **Receding or crumbling joints** — mortar that's set back from the brick face, or that you can rake out with a screwdriver or even your finger.
- **Gaps and cracks** between bricks or running along the joint lines.
- **Loose, shifting, or hollow-sounding brick** that has lost its mortar bond.
- **Water or dampness in the basement**, efflorescence (white chalky staining), or spalling brick faces that are flaking off.
- **Sandy residue** collecting at the base of the wall — that's your mortar washing away.

On many Clarendon Hills homes, the north and west elevations and the chimney show wear first, because those faces take the brunt of wind-driven rain and freeze-thaw cycling.

## The Risk of Waiting

Failed mortar joints are an open door for water. Once moisture gets behind the brick, our Chicago-area freeze-thaw cycle takes over: water freezes, expands, and pushes the masonry apart. What starts as a few crumbling joints turns into spalled brick faces, displaced wythes, a leaning chimney, or a wall that needs full rebuilding instead of repointing.

> Tuckpointing on schedule is one of the least expensive things you can do for a brick home. Putting it off is one of the most expensive.

Catching it at the mortar stage keeps your original brick — which on older Clarendon Hills homes is often irreplaceable — exactly where it belongs.

## Materials: Why Mortar Matching Matters

The single biggest mistake in tuckpointing is using the wrong mortar. We match three things:

- **Type / strength.** Older Clarendon Hills homes were often built with softer lime-rich mortar. Packing modern, over-hard portland mortar into those joints can crack the surrounding brick because the mortar is supposed to be the "sacrificial" element, not the brick. We choose lime or portland blends appropriate to the wall.
- **Color.** We tint and sand-match the mortar so repointed sections blend into the existing wall instead of standing out as a patch.
- **Profile.** We tool the joints to the same profile as the rest of the home so the repair reads as original.

This is the difference between tuckpointing that disappears and lasts decades, and a repair that looks wrong and traps water.
