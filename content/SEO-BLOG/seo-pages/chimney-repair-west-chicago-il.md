## Why Chimneys Fail First

Your chimney is the hardest-working masonry on the house. It is exposed on all four sides, it has no roof overhang shielding it, and its top — the crown — takes rain and snow straight down. In West Chicago's freeze-thaw climate, water that soaks into the crown and the mortar joints freezes, expands, and pries the masonry apart cycle after cycle. That is why chimneys almost always need repair years before the walls below them do.

## Signs Your West Chicago Chimney Needs Repair

From the ground (and what we confirm up top):

- **A cracked or crumbling crown** — the concrete cap at the top, cracked or spalling, lets water straight into the chimney.
- **Crumbling or missing mortar joints** — open joints near the top weather fastest.
- **Spalling brick** — flaking, popping faces, or brick fragments in the gutters or on the roof.
- **White efflorescence staining** on the chimney brick — a sign water is moving through it.
- **Rust stains or a damaged metal cap.**
- **Stains on the ceiling or wall around the chimney** inside the house — flashing or crown failure letting water in.
- **A visibly leaning chimney or a gap opening between the chimney and the house** — a structural warning that needs prompt attention.

## What Chimney Repair Includes

Depending on what the inspection finds, chimney repair can mean any of:

- **Repointing (chimney tuckpointing)** — grinding out and replacing failed mortar joints.
- **Crown repair or recasting** — sealing cracks or pouring a new sloped crown that sheds water away from the flue.
- **Flashing repair** — sealing the junction where the chimney meets the roof, a top source of interior leaks.
- **Brick replacement** — cutting out spalled brick and setting matching units.
- **Partial or full rebuild** — taking the chimney down to a sound course (often the roofline) and rebuilding it true and weather-tight.

## Repair or Rebuild? How We Decide

The dividing line is structure and extent. **If the chimney is straight, the brick is sound, and only the joints and crown are worn, repointing plus crown work restores it.** If the brick is spalling through, the chimney is leaning, or the top courses are loose and gapped, patch repairs only buy a season — a partial or full rebuild from the roofline up is the lasting fix. We make that call on-site, show you what we are seeing, and recommend the smaller option whenever it is genuinely the right one.

## Our Chimney Repair Process

1. **Inspect top to bottom.** We assess the crown, joints, brick, flashing, and cap, and check whether the chimney is plumb.
2. **Diagnose the water path.** Chimney problems are water problems — we find where it is getting in.
3. **Scope the right repair.** Repoint, recast the crown, reflash, replace brick, or rebuild — matched to what the chimney actually needs.
4. **Do the work with matched materials.** Color- and strength-matched mortar, a properly sloped crown, and correct flashing details.
5. **Clean down and confirm.** We leave the roof clean and the chimney weather-tight.

## Materials and Why They Matter

A chimney repair is only as good as its details: a crown poured with the right slope and overhang so water drips clear of the brick instead of running down it; mortar matched in strength so freeze-thaw stress lands in the joint, not the brick face; and flashing sealed so the roof-to-chimney joint stops leaking for good. Skipping any of these is why so many "repaired" chimneys are leaking again within a couple of winters.
