## Why Parapets Deteriorate Faster Than the Wall Below

The wall below your roofline has a fundamental advantage: it is only exposed on one face, and it has a heated interior behind it that helps drive moisture out. A parapet has neither.

- **Exposed on both faces.** Weather hits the street side and the roof side, so it takes roughly twice the wetting and has no dry side to migrate toward.
- **No interior heat.** Nothing dries it from behind between storms, so it stays saturated far longer.
- **It carries the roof's water.** Every gallon leaving the roof edge passes at or near the parapet, concentrating flow exactly where the masonry is weakest.
- **Horizontal surfaces on top.** Coping is a horizontal joint line at the highest, most exposed point of the building — and horizontal masonry joints hold water rather than shedding it.
- **Thermal movement.** The parapet expands and contracts more freely than the restrained wall below, which opens joints and cracks corners over time.

Then Will County freeze-thaw does the rest: dozens of crossings of the freezing point each winter, each one expanding the water trapped inside saturated masonry.

## Signs Your Frankfort Parapet Needs Repair

- **Cracked, loose, displaced or missing coping stones or caps** along the top of the wall.
- **Open, sandy or receded mortar joints**, on the roof side as often as the street side.
- **Spalling and flaking brick** on either face.
- **Efflorescence** — white staining — on the parapet or on the wall below it.
- **A visible lean, bow or bulge** in the parapet, sighted along the wall from a corner.
- **Interior water staining** on the top floor near exterior walls, or at the roof-to-wall junction.
- **Step cracks** at parapet corners, a classic thermal-movement signature.
- **Vegetation growing** out of the joints — proof of persistent saturation.
- **Debris on the roof or sidewalk** below the wall.

Loose masonry at a roof edge above a public sidewalk, an entrance or a parking area is a life-safety and liability matter, not deferred maintenance.

## The Parapet Repair Ladder

| Repair | What it addresses | When it applies |
|---|---|---|
| **Repointing** | Failed mortar joints, both faces | Sound brick, plumb wall |
| **Coping replacement** | Cracked, displaced or improperly sloped caps | Water entering at the top of the wall |
| **Through-wall flashing** | No drainage path for water inside the wall | Recurring leaks despite sound joints |
| **Brick replacement** | Spalled and flaking units | Localized face damage |
| **Partial rebuild** | Loss of cohesion in a section | Deep joint failure, movement |
| **Full parapet rebuild** | Leaning or structurally failed wall | Bulging, out of plane, unsafe |

The item building owners most often skip is **through-wall flashing**, and it is frequently the actual fix. A parapet without a proper flashing and drainage path has nowhere to send the water that inevitably gets in — so it sends it down inside the wall and into the top floor.
