## Ignore the Cost Ranges You Found Online

You have probably seen pages quoting masonry work by the hour or the square foot. Those numbers are not lies exactly, but applied to your specific house they are close to meaningless — and they anchor people badly.

Two walls of identical size can need completely different work. One wants surface repointing. The other wants joints ground to full depth, thirty spalled units cut out and replaced, and a corroding lintel addressed. **Same square footage. Not remotely the same job.**

What actually drives your number:

- **How many elements** are in scope — joints only, or joints plus chimney plus two lintels plus sills.
- **Height and access** — ground-level work versus staging on a two-story elevation or a chimney above the roofline.
- **How hard the existing mortar is** to grind out. Post-war Portland-based mortar takes longer than soft lime mortar.
- **Whether brick needs matching**, and whether a match exists in current production or requires salvage.
- **What is found once work starts** — a rusting lintel behind the brick, spalling worse than it looked from the ground.
- **Season** — cold-weather work needs protection and adds time.

**A contractor who gives you a figure without seeing those has either padded it or will revise it later.** Ask for the drivers instead.

## The Order That Protects Your House

This is the part worth printing out.

**1. Anything actively letting water in.** Chimney crown and flashing, failed sills, open mortar joints on the weather-beaten elevation, failed caulking at window perimeters. Water is the mechanism behind essentially everything else on this list.

**2. Corroding steel.** Rust staining below a window means a **steel lintel** is rusting and expanding inside the wall. It gets worse every winter and eventually lifts the brick above the opening.

**3. Damage the water already caused.** Spalled brick, deteriorated joints, failed sills. Real repairs — but pointless before steps 1 and 2.

**4. Protection.** Breathable sealing where warranted, after joints are sound.

**5. Cosmetic.** Matching, cleaning, appearance.

**Phasing by what is most visible is the expensive way to do this.** The chimney crown you cannot see from the driveway is frequently what is causing the spalled brick you can.

## Mortar: Match the Era

**The governing rule is that mortar must be softer than the brick it holds**, so the joints take the stress and the brick does not. Mortar is the sacrificial layer — cheap to replace, unlike brick.

Oak Forest's post-war brick is denser and machine-made, laid with harder **Portland-based mortar**. That usually means **Type N** for above-grade repointing, with **Type S** where conditions genuinely warrant. A mix too soft erodes out early; a mix too hard spalls the faces. We match hardness first, then color, sand and joint profile.

The competing pages on this search do not mention mortar matching at all. It is the single most consequential technical decision in the entire job.

## Signs Your Oak Forest Home Needs a Full Assessment

- **Rust staining** running down from a window, door or garage opening.
- **A horizontal crack** in the joint directly above an opening.
- **Spalling brick faces** — flaking, popping or crumbling.
- **Mortar you can rake out** with a screwdriver, or joints receded behind the brick.
- **White efflorescence** on the brick.
- **A cracked chimney crown**, rusted or tar-patched flashing, staining on the stack.
- **Water stains inside** near exterior walls or the chimney chase.
- **Step cracks** running diagonally through joints at corners.
- **More than one of the above** — which is the actual signal you need a scope rather than a single repair.

## All at Once, or Phased?

Honest answer: **per item, doing it all at once is cheaper**, because setup, access and staging get paid for once rather than three times.

But that only helps if you can fund it. A well-sequenced three-year plan — water sources this year, steel next, damage after — protects the house better than deferring everything until one large project is affordable, because in the meantime the water keeps arriving.

## What Waiting Costs

Every deferred item makes the next one worse. Open joints wet the brick; wet brick spalls; the same water corrodes the lintels and ties inside the wall. By the time somebody calls about the thing they finally noticed, the scope is often three times what it would have been two winters earlier.
