## Why chimneys go first

A chimney is the most punished masonry on any building, and the reason is geometry.

Every wall on your house has weather on one face and conditioned space on the other. A chimney above the roof line has **weather on all four faces plus the top**, no roof over it, and no heat behind it. It swings through the full range of an Illinois day on every side.

Then add the freeze-thaw cycle. A Chicago-area winter does not freeze once and hold — it thaws in the afternoon and refreezes overnight, dozens of times a season. Every one of those cycles is an expansion event inside wet masonry.

That is why the top four feet of a chimney is almost always in worse condition than the forty feet of wall below it.

## The order that matters

### 1. The crown

The concrete or mortar cap across the top of the stack, around the flue. It is the chimney's roof, and it should be **sloped, with an overhang and a drip edge, and isolated from the flue** so the two can move independently.

What is usually there instead is a flat mortar wash troweled level with the brick. It cracks, and once it cracks it funnels water straight into the middle of the masonry. A cracked crown is the single most consequential cheap defect on a house.

### 2. The cap

A cover over the flue opening. Without one, rain, snow, animals and debris go directly down the flue. With one, they do not. See [chimney crown versus chimney cap](/blog/chimney-crown-vs-chimney-cap) for the distinction — they are different components and people routinely conflate them.

### 3. The flashing

Where the stack passes through the roof. Proper work uses step flashing and counterflashing **let into the masonry**, not a bead of sealant smeared at the joint. Most "chimney leaks" that turn out not to be masonry at all are flashing. See [chimney flashing leaks](/blog/chimney-flashing-leaks-chicago).

### 4. Then the masonry

Repointing, spalled unit replacement, and — where the deterioration has gone through — rebuilding to sound work.

Doing four before one through three is the definition of money spent twice.

## Signs to look for

- **Crumbling, sandy or missing mortar joints** on the stack
- **Spalling or flaking brick faces**, particularly on one weathered side
- **A cracked, flat, or missing crown**
- **No cap** over the flue opening
- **White efflorescence** on the brick — water is actively moving through it
- **Rust staining** around the flashing or a damper
- **Water stains** on the ceiling or wall near the chimney
- **Daylight through joints** seen from inside the attic
- **The stack visibly out of plumb** against a downspout or door frame — that one is urgent
