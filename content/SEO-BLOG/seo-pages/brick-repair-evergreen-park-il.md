## What Brick Repair Actually Covers

Brick repair is the work of restoring deteriorated brickwork without tearing down and rebuilding the wall. On a typical Evergreen Park home that means one or more of the following:

- **Repointing** — grinding out failed, crumbling mortar joints and packing in fresh, color-matched mortar. This is the single most common brick repair, and it's what keeps water out of the wall.
- **Resetting loose or shifted brick** — pulling and re-bedding bricks that have worked loose so the wall is tight again.
- **Patching spalled brick faces** — addressing bricks whose faces have flaked or popped off, either by replacing the individual unit or, where appropriate, dressing the area.
- **Step-crack and small-section repair** — stitching and repointing the stair-step cracks that run through mortar joints on a settling or water-damaged wall.
- **Chimney brickwork** — repairing the exposed brick and joints on the chimney stack, which almost always deteriorates first.

The honest judgment homeowners ask about most is **brick repair versus brick replacement**. The rule we work by: if the *mortar* is failing but the *brick* is sound, you repoint — that's repair. If individual brick *units* have genuinely failed — spalled through, cracked apart, crumbling — we cut out and replace just those, and blend them in. Wholesale [brick replacement](/services/brick-replacement) is only for sections that have truly gone, and on Evergreen Park's solid, well-built masonry that's the exception, not the rule. If a contractor's first move is to quote you a full tear-off, get a second opinion.

## Why Evergreen Park Brick Needs Repair Now

Evergreen Park is a compact, tight-knit Cook County village wedged between Chicago's Beverly and Mount Greenwood neighborhoods and Oak Lawn, and it's built wall-to-wall with **1940s–50s Chicago brick bungalows, Georgians, and Cape Cods** on small lots, plus the older commercial brick along the 95th Street corridor. That housing stock is now 70 to 80 years old — and that's exactly the age when the original mortar reaches the end of its service life.

Here's the building science behind it. Mortar is designed to be the *sacrificial* element in a wall: softer than the brick, it wears and absorbs the stress so the brick doesn't have to. Over decades of Chicago **freeze-thaw** cycles — water soaking into the joints, freezing, expanding, and thawing again, over and over each winter — the mortar slowly loses its binder and turns sandy. Once the joints open up, water gets behind the brick, and then the freeze-thaw starts attacking the brick faces themselves, causing **spalling**. That's why brick repair timing matters: repoint while it's still a mortar problem, and you protect the brick; wait, and you're now also replacing brick.

On most Evergreen Park homes the deterioration shows up first on the **chimney** and the **north- and west-facing weather walls**, which take the most wind-driven rain and stay damp the longest.

### Signs your brickwork needs attention

- Mortar joints you can scratch or rake out with a screwdriver
- Small piles of sandy mortar grit at the base of the wall or on window sills
- Spalling, flaking, or popped brick faces
- Stair-step cracks running diagonally through the mortar joints
- Loose, shifted, or hollow-sounding bricks
- White, chalky **efflorescence** blooming on the brick (a moisture flag)
- Water stains or dampness on the inside of an exterior wall

## The Risk of Waiting

A failing mortar joint is an open door for water. Once moisture is moving through the wall, it accelerates everything — more freeze-thaw spalling, efflorescence, rusting steel lintels above windows, damp interior walls, and step cracks that widen. A repointing job that would have handled a wall becomes a repair-and-replace job across several elevations. The cheapest version of this work is almost always the one you do sooner.

## Our Brick Repair Process

1. **Free on-site assessment.** We look at the whole exterior — walls, chimney, lintels, sills — identify what's actually failing, and separate cosmetic issues from ones letting water in.
2. **Grind and rake the failed joints** to a sound depth (not a thin skim over old mortar — that's the shortcut that fails in a few years).
3. **Mix matched mortar.** We match the color and, importantly, the hardness of your original mortar so the repair moves with the wall instead of stressing the brick.
4. **Repoint and reset.** We pack the joints full, reset any loose brick, and replace individual failed units with close-matching brick.
5. **Tool the joints** to match your home's existing profile so the repair reads as original.
6. **Clean up and, where useful, seal** to shed water going forward.

## Materials We Use

The heart of a lasting brick repair is **mortar matching**. Evergreen Park's mid-century homes were laid in relatively soft mortar, and matching that — often a **Type N** rather than a hard, high-strength **Type S** — matters, because too-hard mortar on older brick doesn't flex and ends up spalling the brick face instead of protecting it. We match mortar color to your existing joints, source close-matching brick for replacements, and tool joints to the original profile. Where appropriate we finish with a breathable masonry sealer that sheds water without trapping moisture in the wall.
