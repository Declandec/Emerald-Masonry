## Parapet Wall Repair in Berwyn, IL

Parapet wall repair in Berwyn, IL means correcting the section of masonry that rises above the roof — resetting coping, installing through-wall flashing, repointing the faces, and rebuilding what has moved. Here is the physics that explains almost every parapet failure in Berwyn:

**A parapet is a wall with no inside.**

Every other wall on your building has heated interior space on one face. That means it dries from one side, it stays warmer, and it has a roof edge or an overhang shedding water above it. The parapet has **weather on both faces, no thermal buffer, and a flat horizontal top surface collecting every inch of rain and every snow melt** that lands on it.

So it is not bad luck that your parapet is the worst masonry on the building. It is the most punished masonry on the building — by a wide margin.

And the failure almost never begins in the brick. **It begins at the coping.**

## Why Coping Is Where It Starts

The coping is the cap on top of the parapet — limestone, cast stone, clay tile or metal on Berwyn's building stock. Its entire job is to keep water out of the top of the wall.

It fails in a predictable order:

1. **The joints between coping units open.** Mortar or sealant in a horizontal joint sits in standing water and cycles through freeze-thaw harder than anything else on the building.
2. **Water enters the top of the wall** and runs down inside the masonry, where you cannot see it.
3. **It saturates the parapet from within**, freezing and expanding through Cook County winters.
4. **Joints erode from the inside out** and the wall loses cohesion.
5. **The parapet bows, leans, or displaces**, usually outward toward the street, because that face freezes hardest.
6. **Coping units shift**, and now there is a real hazard above a sidewalk.

**By the time the brick face looks bad, water has been in the wall for years.** The visible symptom is the last step, not the first.

There is a second detail almost nobody addresses: **through-wall flashing at the base of the parapet.** Water that gets into a parapet has to be intercepted and directed back out before it reaches the roof junction and the wall below. If that flashing was never installed, or was installed and destroyed during a past roof job, the parapet will leak into the building no matter how good the coping is. ## Signs of Parapet Failure on a Berwyn Building

- **Open, cracked or missing joints between coping stones.**
- **Coping units that have shifted, tilted or dropped** out of line.
- **A parapet that reads out of plumb** from across the street — sight down the top edge.
- **Bulging or bowing** in the wall face above the roof line.
- **Step cracks** running diagonally through the parapet brick.
- **Spalled, flaking or crumbling brick faces**, especially on the street side.
- **White efflorescence** on the parapet or the top few feet of wall.
- **Persistent leaks at the roof–wall junction** that survived a roof replacement.
- **Staining on the top-floor interior ceiling perimeter.**
- **Loose brick or mortar debris on the roof or sidewalk** — urgent.
- **A vertical crack where the parapet meets a neighboring building's party wall.**

## Our Parapet Repair Process

1. **Free on-site assessment** — from the roof and from the ground, checking plumb, coping condition, joint condition, flashing presence and the roof junction. We photograph and document it.
2. **Determine repair versus rebuild, section by section.** Parapets rarely fail uniformly; a 90-foot wall may need 30 feet rebuilt and 60 feet repointed. We scope it that way instead of pricing the whole run at the worst condition.
3. **Protect the roof and the public way** — staging, overhead protection, debris containment, sidewalk protection where required.
4. **Remove coping** carefully, salvaging sound stone or tile for reuse wherever possible.
5. **Take down failed masonry** to sound, plumb, well-bonded work — no rebuilding on top of a compromised base.
6. **Install through-wall flashing** at the parapet base with end dams, integrated with the roof system, so water inside the wall is intercepted and discharged rather than continuing into the building.
7. **Rebuild** with salvaged and matched brick, correct bond pattern, and mortar matched for hardness, color and sand — always softer than the brick it holds.
8. **Reset coping** on a proper bed, with the correct slope, and joints detailed with backer rod and sealant rather than rigid mortar alone where movement demands it.
9. **Repoint sound sections** — joints cut back to solid mortar and packed in lifts.
10. **Coordinate counter flashing and the roof membrane** with your roofer so the two systems actually interlock.
11. **Final walkthrough and documentation** for your records, your board, or your insurer.
