## What Tuckpointing and Repointing Actually Are

Mortar is the sacrificial layer in a masonry wall. It is meant to be softer than the brick around it, so that as the wall expands, contracts, and takes on moisture, the mortar — not the brick — absorbs the stress and wears first. Over decades of Chicago-area freeze-thaw cycles, that mortar recedes, cracks, and turns sandy. Once the joints open up, water gets behind the brick, freezes, expands, and starts pushing the wall apart from the inside.

**Tuckpointing in Berwyn, IL is the process of removing that deteriorated mortar and replacing it with fresh, correctly matched mortar.** Repointing is the structural half of the job — raking or grinding out the old joints to a sound depth and packing in new mortar. Tuckpointing, in the way most homeowners mean it, includes finishing those joints cleanly so the repair matches the wall. Emerald Masonry does both as one job: sound repair plus a finish that disappears into the original brickwork.

## Signs Your Berwyn Home Needs Tuckpointing

Berwyn's brick tells you when it needs help. Watch for:

- **Crumbling or receding mortar joints** — if you can scrape mortar out with a screwdriver or a key, or the joint has recessed noticeably below the brick face, the mortar is done.
- **Sandy debris** collecting on window sills, foundation ledges, and the ground along the wall — that's your mortar, disintegrating.
- **Spalling brick** — faces of the brick flaking, popping, or crumbling. This is a warning that water is already getting in and freezing behind the surface.
- **Step cracks** — cracks that climb diagonally through the mortar joints in a stair-step pattern, often a sign of movement that needs attention before it worsens.
- **Efflorescence** — the chalky white residue on the brick that means water is moving through the wall and evaporating on the surface.

On a Berwyn two-flat, these signs often show up first on the weather-facing elevation and around the chimney, where exposure is worst.

## The Risk of Waiting

Failed mortar is not a cosmetic problem. Once joints open, water enters the wall, and Berwyn's freeze-thaw winters turn that water into a wedge that expands with every cold snap. What starts as a few soft joints becomes spalled brick, then displaced brick, then — on a chimney or a parapet — a section that has to be rebuilt instead of simply repointed. Repointing a wall is a modest project; rebuilding one because it was left too long is a much larger one. The most cost-effective time to tuckpoint is always before the brick itself starts to fail.

## Our Tuckpointing Process

Emerald Masonry follows the same disciplined process on every Berwyn home:

1. **Inspect and assess.** We evaluate the extent of joint failure, the condition of the brick, and any related issues at chimneys, lintels, and sills.
2. **Grind and rake the joints.** We cut out the deteriorated mortar to a sound depth — deep enough for a durable bond, careful not to damage the brick edges.
3. **Match the mortar.** We mix mortar to match the color, texture, and — most importantly — the hardness of your original mortar, typically a Type N or a lime-based mix for Berwyn's older, softer brick.
4. **Repoint and pack.** We pack the fresh mortar fully into the cleaned joints in proper layers so there are no voids for water to hide in.
5. **Tool and finish.** We tool the joints to match the original profile, then let the mortar cure properly so it reaches full strength.

## Materials and Why Mortar Matching Matters

The single most important decision in a Berwyn tuckpointing job is the mortar mix. A common, costly mistake is using a hard, high-Portland modern mortar on soft century-old brick. That hard mortar won't flex, so the brick faces take the stress instead — and within a few winters they spall and pop. We match mortar hardness to the brick, use color matching so the repair blends, and tool the joint to the original profile.
