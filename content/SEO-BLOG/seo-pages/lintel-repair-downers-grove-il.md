## What a Lintel Is and What Lintel Repair Involves

A lintel is the horizontal support that carries the weight of the brick above a window, door, or garage opening. On most brick and brick-veneer homes it's a steel angle iron (an "L"-shaped bar) tucked into the mortar joint just above the opening. That steel holds up hundreds of pounds of masonry so your window frame doesn't have to.

The problem is that steel and water don't mix. When moisture gets behind the brick and pools on an unprotected lintel, the steel begins to rust. As rust forms it expands to several times the volume of the original steel — this "rust jacking" physically pushes the brick apart, opens cracks, and eventually causes the lintel to sag and lose its load-bearing strength.

Lintel repair depends on how far things have gone:

- **Where the lintel can be saved** (light surface rust, steel still straight and sound), we wire-wheel the corrosion down to clean metal, apply a rust-converting primer, repaint, and reseal the surrounding brick so water stays out.
- **Where the lintel has failed** (scaling, swelling, or sagging steel), it needs replacement. We carefully remove the brick courses above the opening, cut out and lift out the old lintel, set a new galvanized or primed steel lintel with proper bearing on each end, then reset the brick on fresh mortar with a clean, weather-tight joint line.

Either way, the goal is the same: a straight, protected, load-bearing lintel and brick above the opening that sheds water instead of trapping it.

## Signs You Need Lintel Repair in Downers Grove IL

Steel lintels almost always warn you before they fail. Watch for:

- **Orange or brown rust stains** streaking down the brick below a window or door — the single most reliable early sign.
- **Step cracks** running diagonally up and outward from the top corners of a window or door opening.
- **Sagging, bowing, or cracked brick** directly over an opening, sometimes with a visible dip in the mortar line.
- **Gaps or an opening mortar joint** just above the window where the lintel sits.
- **Visible rust, flaking, or swelling** on the exposed steel edge itself.
- **Doors or windows that suddenly stick** as the opening shifts.

If you see any of these, it's worth a look. A quick, free inspection today is a lot cheaper than a rebuilt wall next year.

## The Risk of Waiting

A lintel doesn't fail gracefully. As the steel keeps rusting and expanding, the brick above the opening loses its support. First you get cracks, then loose and displaced brick, and eventually **brick can fall out of the wall over the opening entirely** — a real safety hazard over a doorway or a walkway.

Waiting also multiplies the cost. Early rust conversion on a still-sound lintel is a modest, targeted repair. Once the steel has scaled through and the brick has shifted, you're looking at full lintel replacement plus rebuilding the masonry above it. Every DuPage County winter of freeze-thaw makes it worse. Catching it early is always the smaller job.

> A rust stain under your window isn't cosmetic — it's the steel lintel telling you water is already sitting on it. That's the moment to call, not after the brick starts to move.
