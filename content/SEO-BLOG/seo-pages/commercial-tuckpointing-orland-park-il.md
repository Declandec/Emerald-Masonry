## Where the joints fail on Orland Park's commercial stock

Most of it is **1970s-onward brick and stone veneer** over backup, which fails at the details rather than in the brick:

- **Parapets and coping joints** — exposed on both faces, unheated, and standing over walkways
- **South and west elevations** — driving rain and the widest temperature swings
- **Below sills, copings and projections**, where water runs back onto the wall
- **At and above lintels and shelf angles**, where corroding steel opens the joint from behind
- **Base courses** — splash-back, snow banked by plows, and de-icing salt at every entrance
- **Around penetrations** — vents, conduit, sign anchors, and abandoned fastener holes

The mechanism is the same everywhere: water enters an open joint, freezes, expands roughly nine percent, and forces the masonry apart — dozens of times each Cook County winter.

## Our process

1. **Assess every elevation and the roof edge** — from a lift or staging rather than the parking lot, because that is where the parapet and upper-elevation conditions actually are.
2. **Sound and probe.** Drummy brick and true joint depth do not show up in photographs.
3. **Write the scope** — quantities by elevation, cut-out depth, mortar specification, access method, phasing, and the assumptions each quantity rests on.
4. **Plan access and protection** — staging or lift, overhead protection over entrances, parking and drive-aisle management, tenant notice.
5. **Cut out to sound material** by grinding or hand-raking, without damaging unit edges.
6. **Repoint in matched mortar** — colour, sand, texture and, critically, hardness matched to the units.
7. **Tool the joints** to the existing profile at the right point in the set, which is what compacts and closes the joint surface.
8. **Address sealant, flashing and weeps** where the assessment found the real water path.
9. **Clean down and walk it with you**, elevation by elevation.

## Keeping a retail centre trading

- **Phase by elevation.** One frontage under staging at a time; the rest operate normally.
- **Overhead protection** at entrances and anywhere work sits above a path of travel.
- **Access planning** so parking, drive aisles and accessible entrances stay usable.
- **Written notice** to tenants before work reaches their frontage, with dates.
- **Off-hours where it helps.** Grinding is the loudest phase and frequently movable; mortar placement is daylight- and temperature-dependent, and we are straight about which is which.
- **Daily cleanup**, because a tidy site is one tenants tolerate.

## Materials

Type N for most of Orland Park's later commercial brick; more lime-rich, lower-Portland mixes on older or softer masonry; Type S only where the units genuinely warrant it. Matched sand and pigment for colour. Non-staining sealants where limestone or cast stone is involved. Breathable penetrating repellents rather than film-forming coatings that trap moisture in a wall.
