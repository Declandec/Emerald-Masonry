## Chimney repair in Bridgeview: why the top fails first

Most of Bridgeview's housing went up in the decades after World War II, so the typical brick chimney here is now 60 or more years old. Below the roof, it is protected by the house. Above the roof, it is exposed on all four sides and the top, takes wind-driven rain, snow and every freeze-thaw cycle, and has no heat behind it to dry it out. That is why the mortar and brick at the top wear out decades before the walls below.

Many of these chimneys also vent a **furnace or water heater**. Exhaust moisture can condense inside the chimney and soak the brick from within, which is why chimneys fail even on houses where nobody has used the fireplace in years.

## Chimney sweep or mason: who to call

When we looked at what comes up for chimney repair in Bridgeview, it was mostly chimney sweep and fireplace cleaning listings. A sweep is the right call for the flue and liner. The brick and mortar are a mason's job.

## Signs your Bridgeview chimney needs repair

- **Recessed or crumbling mortar** in the top courses.
- **Spalling**: brick faces flaking or popping off.
- **A cracked crown**, or pieces of crown on the roof.
- **White efflorescence** or dark, wet staining on the chimney.
- **Brick or mortar debris** in the gutters or on the roof.
- **Water stains** on ceilings or walls next to the chimney.
- **A chimney top that leans** or has a visible gap where it meets the roof.

## Risks of waiting

Water that gets into the top of a chimney runs down inside it. It loosens more brick each winter, rusts the damper and flue components, stains ceilings and can rot roof framing around the chimney. A chimney that needs a crown and tuckpointing this year can need a full rebuild above the roof a few winters later, and loose brick at the top is a hazard to anyone below.

## How we repair chimneys in Bridgeview

1. **Inspect the chimney** from the roof: brick, mortar, crown, cap and flashing.
2. **Recommend the repair**: crown and cap, tuckpointing, brick replacement, or a rebuild above the roofline.
3. **Protect the roof** and the area below.
4. **Tuckpoint**: grind out deteriorated joints and repoint with mortar matched in color and joint profile.
5. **Replace spalled brick** with matched brick.
6. **Rebuild the chimney top** where needed, from the roofline up.
7. **Pour or form a new crown** that overhangs the brick and sheds water, and install a cap.
8. **Check the flashing** and repair or coordinate replacement where it has failed.

## Materials we use

Mortar matched to the existing joints in color and strength; matched face brick from current or salvaged stock; crown mixes suited to exposure, formed with an overhang and drip edge; stainless or galvanized chimney caps; and compatible sealant and counterflashing details at the roof line. We use breathable water repellents on chimney brick only where they make sense, never film-forming coatings that trap water.
