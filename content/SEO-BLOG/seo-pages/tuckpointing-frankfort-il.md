## What tuckpointing is

Mortar is the sacrificial part of a brick wall. It's designed to be softer than the brick so it wears out first and can be renewed. **Tuckpointing** (also called **repointing**) is removing deteriorated mortar from the joints and replacing it with new mortar, then tooling it to match the original joint profile. Done on time, it keeps water out of the wall and stops freeze-thaw damage from moving into the brick itself. See [what repointing actually means](/blog/what-repointing-actually-means) for the terms.

| **Bottom courses** | Splash-back, snow piles, sprinkler spray | Repoint the base, fix grading and downspouts |
| **Sheltered walls** | Little exposure | Often fine — leave them alone |

## Signs your Frankfort home needs tuckpointing

- Mortar joints recessed behind the face of the brick
- Cracked, crumbling or sandy mortar that scrapes out with a key
- Gaps or missing mortar at corners and around windows
- Stair-step cracks through the joints
- White efflorescence or damp-looking brick near failing joints
- Loose bricks on the chimney or at garage corners
- Leaks or stains inside below a window or around the fireplace

Try the [screwdriver test for failing mortar](/blog/how-to-tell-if-mortar-is-failing-screwdriver-test) on a few joints if you're not sure.

## Risks of waiting

Once mortar joints are recessed or open, water gets into the wall every time it rains. In a Will County winter that water freezes and expands, widening the joints and starting to break the faces off the bricks — called **spalling**. At that point the job goes from repointing to [brick repair or replacement](/services/brick-replacement). Water behind the brick also rusts lintels and wets the framing. Read [what happens if you wait too long to tuckpoint](/blog/what-happens-if-you-wait-too-long-to-tuckpoint).

## Our tuckpointing process in Frankfort

1. **Walk the house with you** and mark which walls, corners and chimney areas need work.
2. **Match the mortar** — color, sand and joint profile — and plan a test area.
3. **Protect** windows, landscaping, the roof and driveway.
4. **Grind out the mortar joints** to a sufficient depth for the new mortar to bond properly, without damaging brick edges.
5. **Clean and dampen** the joints.
6. **Pack new mortar** in layers and tool it to match the existing profile.
7. **Clean up and check the cured color**, then point out anything else we saw — lintels, caulk, crown — so you can decide on it.
