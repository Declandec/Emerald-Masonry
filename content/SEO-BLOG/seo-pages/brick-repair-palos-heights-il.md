## What Brick Repair Actually Involves

There is a distinction most homeowners never get explained clearly, and it determines the whole scope of the job.

**Mortar failure is repointing. Brick failure is replacement.**

Mortar is the sacrificial layer in a masonry wall — deliberately made softer than the brick so it absorbs the movement and moisture stress and wears out first. Replacing mortar is cheap; replacing brick is not. When only the joints have gone, [tuckpointing](/services/tuckpointing) restores the wall and the brick is untouched.

But once water has been entering long enough to saturate the **brick units themselves**, freezing inside them breaks the faces off in layers. That is **spalling**, and a spalled brick cannot be repaired back to sound — it has to be cut out and replaced with a matched unit. Most real Palos Heights jobs are a combination: replace the failed units, repoint the failed joints, and fix the water source that caused both.

## Signs Your Palos Heights Brick Needs Repair

- **Spalling faces** — brick flaking, shelling or crumbling, worst near grade, below sills, and on shaded north elevations.
- **Cracked brick**, particularly cracks running through the units rather than following the joints.
- **Loose brick** that moves under hand pressure.
- **Step cracks** climbing diagonally through the mortar joints, often at corners or near the garage.
- **Rust staining** running down from above a window or door — a corroding steel lintel expanding inside the wall.
- **Receded or sandy mortar joints** that rake out under a screwdriver.
- **Efflorescence**, the chalky white bloom proving water is moving through the wall.
- **A bulge or bow** in the wall plane — a structural condition that needs evaluation, not a patch.
- **Brick debris** collecting at the base of the wall or in the landscaping.

## Why Brick Fails in Palos Heights Specifically

**Freeze-thaw is the engine.** It is not extreme cold that destroys masonry but repeated crossings of the freezing point with water present — dozens each winter here, plus more in the shoulder seasons. Every crossing expands trapped water about nine percent inside the brick.

**Local moisture keeps walls wetter.** Palos Heights sits along the **Cal-Sag Channel** with the Palos forest preserves and Lake Katherine nearby, and the village has genuinely heavy mature tree cover. Shaded, wooded lots mean north and east elevations that stay damp for days after a storm — going into each of those freeze cycles saturated.

**The housing stock has a shared age.** Much of Palos Heights was built from the 1950s through the 1970s: **brick ranches, split-levels, Georgians, Cape Cods and story-and-a-halfs**, with some newer construction and larger homes toward the west and south. Post-war brick is dense, but its Portland-based mortar is now sixty to seventy years old — right at the end of a normal service life.

**Water management is usually the real culprit.** Overflowing gutters, downspouts discharging at the foundation, grade sloping toward the house, and splash-back at grade concentrate water exactly where the spalling shows up.

## What Waiting Costs

Spalling is progressive. Once a brick loses its fired face, the soft porous body underneath absorbs water far faster, so that unit deteriorates more quickly and starts wetting its neighbors. A dozen brick becomes fifty; fifty becomes a section rebuild. And because spalling means water is already inside the wall, the steel lintels and ties in there are corroding on the same timeline.
