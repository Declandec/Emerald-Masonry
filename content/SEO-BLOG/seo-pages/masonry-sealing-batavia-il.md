Sealing is one of the cheapest, most effective ways to extend the life of sound masonry — but only when it is the right product applied at the right time. Done wrong, it does real harm.

## What Masonry Sealing Actually Does

Brick, block, and limestone are porous. They absorb water — from rain, snowmelt, and splashback — and in our climate that absorbed water freezes, expands, and breaks the masonry apart from the inside over repeated winters. A masonry sealer (a water repellent) reduces how much liquid water the wall takes in, which slows freeze-thaw damage, reduces efflorescence, and helps keep interiors dry.

**The critical detail is breathability: the right masonry sealer blocks liquid water from getting in while still letting water vapor escape, so the wall stays protected without trapping moisture inside.** That single property is the difference between protecting your brick and quietly destroying it.

## Why the Wrong Sealer Ruins Brick

This is the part homeowners are rarely told. A glossy, film-forming sealer — the kind that makes brick look "wet" — can seal moisture inside the wall. Water still gets in through other paths (the top of a wall, a crack, the back side), and now it can't get out. Come winter, that trapped water freezes and pops the face off the brick. We get called to repair spalling that was caused by the wrong sealer more often than people would guess.

We use **breathable, vapor-permeable water repellents** designed for masonry. They protect without forming a trapping film, and they don't leave that artificial sheen.

## When Sealing Makes Sense — and When It Doesn't

Sealing is the right call when:

- The masonry is **sound** but porous and exposed to heavy weather.
- You've just **tuckpointed or repaired** a wall and want to protect the investment.
- You're fighting **recurring efflorescence** or damp interiors traced to water absorption.

Sealing is the **wrong** first move when the joints are failed, the brick is cracked, or there are open gaps — because a sealer cannot bridge a gap. **Sealing is the final step after masonry is sound, never a shortcut around tuckpointing and repair.** We will tell you honestly if your wall needs repair before it needs sealing.

## Our Sealing Process

1. **Inspect.** We assess the masonry's condition and identify any joints, cracks, or gaps that must be repaired first.

3. **Clean.** We gently clean the surface so the sealer can penetrate properly.
4. **Specify the right product.** We select a breathable repellent suited to your specific brick, block, or limestone.
5. **Apply.** We apply the sealer at the correct coverage rate for full, even penetration.

**Will sealing my brick trap moisture and make things worse?**

Only if the wrong product is used. A film-forming sealer can trap moisture inside brick and cause spalling, which is why we use breathable, vapor-permeable sealers that block liquid water from getting in while still letting water vapor escape. **Should I repoint or repair before sealing?**

Yes. Sealer is not a substitute for repair. Failed mortar joints, cracks, and open gaps must be tuckpointed and repaired first, because a sealer cannot bridge a gap and will just let water in behind it. Sealing is the final protective step after the masonry is sound — never a shortcut around repairs.
