## Repair vs. replacement: which do you need?

Not every problem brick has to come out. If a brick face is flaking but the unit behind it is still solid, targeted repair and repointing usually restore it. When a brick is cracked through, spalled to the core, or sounds hollow when tapped, replacement is the right call. The judgment matters: over-replacing wastes money and risks a visible patch, while under-repairing leaves water paths that spread damage. During your estimate we tap, inspect, and mark exactly which units need replacing and which need repair — you get the least-invasive fix that actually lasts.

## Signs your Bolingbrook brick needs attention

- **Spalling or flaking faces** — the front surface of the brick is popping, peeling, or shedding grit, often leaving a pitted crater.
- **Cracked or loose bricks** — individual units you can wiggle, or bricks split by a visible fracture line.
- **Step cracks** — diagonal cracking that follows the mortar joints in a staircase pattern, a sign of movement or settlement.
- **Open or crumbling joints** — mortar that has receded, cracked, or turned to sand, letting water behind the wall.
- **White staining (efflorescence) or damp interior walls** — evidence water is already migrating through the masonry.

## The risk of waiting

Brick damage is progressive. One spalled face becomes an open path for water, and every freeze-thaw cycle drives that water deeper — popping neighboring faces, eroding joints, and eventually reaching the wall ties and structure behind the brick. What starts as a few crumbling faces on a north wall can turn into a bowing veneer section, a failing lintel, or interior moisture damage. Catching it while it is still a repair, rather than a rebuild, is almost always the cheaper path.

## Our brick repair and replacement process

1. **Inspect and mark.** We identify every damaged unit, check the mortar joints, and look for the underlying cause — a failed lintel, clogged weep hole, or past sealing that trapped moisture.
2. **Remove damaged units.** Compromised brick is carefully cut and removed without disturbing the sound brick around it.
3. **Source matching brick.** We match size, color, and texture — the step most contractors rush.
4. **Reset the brick.** New or reclaimed units are laid back into the wall in the correct bond, plumb and aligned with the surrounding courses.
5. **Repoint and finish.** Joints are packed with the correct mortar and tooled to match the existing profile, so the repair blends in.

## Brick matching: where the job is won or lost

The hard part of any brick repair is not removing the old brick — it is making the new brick disappear. Brick varies by **size** (older Bolingbrook homes may use dimensions that modern standard brick does not match), by **color** (kiln batches and decades of weathering shift the tone), and by **texture** (smooth, wire-cut, tumbled, or sand-faced). A mismatch on any one of those turns a repair into an eyesore that draws the eye straight to the patch.

We treat matching as its own step. We identify the brick line where we can, source the closest current production or reclaimed brick, and — when a brick is truly discontinued — pull replacement units from an inconspicuous area (a garage return or below-grade course) and fill that spot with the closest blend. Done right, a finished repair reads as original wall, not a repair.

## Materials we use

- **Matching brick** — current-production, special-order, or reclaimed units chosen to match your wall's size, color, and texture.
- **Type N mortar** — the standard for most residential above-grade brick veneer, flexible enough to move with the wall.
- **Type S mortar** — used where higher strength is warranted, such as at or below grade or on load-bearing masonry.

Matching the mortar matters as much as matching the brick: the color and the tooled joint profile have to line up with what is already there.
