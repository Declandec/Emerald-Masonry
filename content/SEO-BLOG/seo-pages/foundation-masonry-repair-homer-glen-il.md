## Reading the Cracks: What Each Pattern Means

Not all foundation cracks mean the same thing, and the pattern tells you most of what you need to know.

- **Hairline vertical cracks.** Often shrinkage from curing, common and usually minor.
- **Stair-step cracks** climbing diagonally through the mortar joints. Differential settlement or soil movement — worth evaluating, especially if widening.
- **Horizontal cracks**, typically partway up a block wall. **This is the one to take seriously in Homer Glen.** It indicates lateral soil pressure pushing the wall inward, which is exactly what saturated clay does.
- **Cracks wider at one end** than the other. Rotational movement — the wall is not just cracked, it is moving.
- **Bowing or bulging** visible when you sight along the wall. The wall is deflecting under load and needs professional evaluation before anything else is scoped.
- **Cracks with displacement** — where one side sits proud of the other. Active movement.

## Signs Your Homer Glen Foundation Needs Masonry Repair

- **Mortar crumbling or washing out** from between blocks, brick or stone.
- **Step cracking** through the joints of a block or brick foundation.
- **Horizontal cracking or bowing** in a block wall.
- **Spalling and flaking** in the first courses above grade where splash-back keeps masonry wet.
- **Parging falling away in sheets**, exposing the wall behind it.
- **Water entering the basement** at the wall–floor joint or through visible joints.
- **Efflorescence** — white powdery deposits on interior basement walls.
- **Doors and windows** on the level above sticking or going out of square.
- **A damp or musty basement** with no plumbing source.

## Why Homer Glen Foundations Deteriorate

**Clay soil movement.** The dominant factor. Saturated clay expands and presses laterally against the wall; dry clay shrinks and withdraws support. That cycle repeats seasonally, year after year, and block walls take it in the form of horizontal cracks and bowing.

**Water at grade.** The base of a foundation is the wettest masonry on any property. Rain splashes back, downspouts discharge beside the wall, grade slopes the wrong way, and soil holds moisture against the masonry for days. Mortar in constant contact with wet soil erodes — and every gallon that soaks the clay also feeds the pressure cycle above.

**Large lots and mature landscaping.** Homer Glen's characteristically large, heavily wooded lots mean substantial tree root systems near foundations, drawing moisture unevenly from the clay in dry spells and adding another movement variable.

**Freeze-thaw.** Will County runs through dozens of freeze-thaw cycles each winter with no lake buffer. Water inside a saturated joint expands as it freezes and works the mortar apart from within, concentrated at the wettest part of the building.

## Our Foundation Masonry Repair Process

1. **Free on-site inspection.** We evaluate the foundation inside and out, read the crack patterns, and check grading, downspouts and drainage — determining whether the wall is deteriorating, actually moving, or both.
2. **Diagnosis before scope.** Water management problems get identified up front, because repairing masonry while leaving the water source in place guarantees a repeat.
3. **Joint preparation.** Failed mortar raked and cleaned out to sound material.
4. **Repointing.** Joints packed with mortar matched to the wall — an appropriate structural mix on block, a softer, more forgiving mix on old brick and stone.
5. **Crack repair and rebuilding.** Cracks repaired appropriately to their cause; failed block or brick replaced with matched units; deteriorated sections rebuilt.
6. **Parging and protection** of the exposed portion where appropriate, to shed water and protect the masonry.
7. **Drainage recommendations.** Grade, downspout extensions and splash control — the low-cost measures that protect the repair for decades.

Where a wall shows genuine structural movement rather than masonry deterioration, we will say so plainly and tell you what kind of evaluation it needs. Not every foundation problem is a masonry problem, and we would rather tell you that than sell you repointing on a wall that is moving.

## What Happens If You Wait

Foundation masonry is where deferral compounds fastest, because the water and the soil pressure keep arriving whether you address them or not. Open joints admit water into the wall and the basement.
