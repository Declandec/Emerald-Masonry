## Scoping backwards from August

1. **Assess in winter or early spring**, not in May. An assessment done in April leaves no time to order matching units or to bid the work properly.
2. **Identify long-lead items early** — matched or reclaimed brick, stone, custom coping, steel lintels. Availability, not labour, is what most often blows a summer schedule.
3. **Size the scope to the window.** A realistic June-to-August scope is smaller than most people assume once mobilisation, staging, curing and demobilisation are counted.
4. **Rank what must be in this summer** — safety first, then active water entry — and what can honestly wait.
5. **Phase by elevation** so a partial completion is still weathertight and safe rather than an open wall.
6. **Build in weather contingency.** A rained-out fortnight in July is a normal event, not an excuse.
7. **Set a hard demobilisation date** ahead of staff return, with punch and cleanup inside it.

## What fails on Oak Lawn institutional buildings

Oak Lawn's schools, parish buildings and municipal stock are largely **mid-century masonry**, much of it with **additions from several eras** attached to an older core — plus gymnasiums, annexes, field houses and boiler rooms. The village's major post-1967 rebuilding left a distinctive mix of eras standing side by side.

- **Parapets and roof edges** — the most exposed masonry, standing over entrances and walkways
- **Lintels over wide openings** — gym doors, entrance banks, corridor window runs, service and loading doors, all carrying more masonry on bare steel
- **Cold joints where additions meet the original building** — different eras, different movement, and a classic concealed water path
- **Base courses** — splash-back, snow piled by plows, and heavy de-icing salt at every entrance
- **Chimneys and boiler stacks** — tall, unheated, and the most commonly deferred element on any campus
- **Limestone banding, sills and entrance surrounds** on the older stock, needing soft mortar and gentle cleaning

Because eras differ across one building, **a single mortar specification is wrong somewhere.** We specify by elevation and by addition, not by building.

## Our process

1. **Survey the whole campus**, building by building, from a lift or staging rather than the ground.
2. **Sound and probe** to separate what is weathered from what has lost its bond.
3. **Document by elevation** with photographs tied to locations.
4. **Write a phased scope** with quantities and stated assumptions, sized to the window and biddable on one basis.
5. **Correct water paths first** — flashing, coping, drainage, weeps.
6. **Repoint in compatible mortar**, matched for colour, sand, texture and hardness by era.
7. **Replace failed units and treat or replace lintels**, with flashing restored at heads.
8. **Clean gently, seal last**, on sound masonry after curing.
