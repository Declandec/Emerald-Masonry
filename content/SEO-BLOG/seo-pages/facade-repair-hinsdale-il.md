## Confirm the review path before you scope the work

We say this to every downtown owner and manager: **call the village first, and build the answer into the schedule.**

Requirements differ by property, by whether the elevation faces a street, by whether the work changes appearance, and by proximity to the historic district. What matters commercially is not the specific rule — it is that **submittal and review time is real time**, and a scope written as though it does not exist will be late.

Practically, that means:

- Establishing early whether the elevation is subject to review
- Preparing **physical match samples** — mortar color and profile, brick, stone — rather than describing a match in words
- Photographing existing conditions thoroughly **before** anything is disturbed
- Keeping the schedule honest about the approval window rather than compressing the masonry to absorb it

We prepare that package as part of the assessment. It is also, not incidentally, the same package that protects the owner if anyone later disputes what the wall looked like beforehand.

## What actually fails on a Hinsdale commercial façade

Downtown Hinsdale is a mix: older brick and limestone commercial buildings, mid-century infill, and later office and retail stock along the corridors. The failures sort into a predictable list.

**Mortar joints on the weather elevation.** Receded behind the face of the units, letting volume in during driven rain.

**Steel lintels over storefront openings.** Corroded steel expands as it rusts and lifts the courses above, opening a horizontal crack across a display window. Rust staining down the brick is the early warning and usually precedes the crack by years.

**Limestone and cast stone.** Sills, water tables, band courses and lintels. Stone fails at the **wash** — when the top surface loses its pitch and its drip edge, water stops running off and starts running back into the wall beneath.

**Coping and the parapet.** The cap along the top of the wall is the roof of that wall. Open coping joints let water directly into the parapet core, where it freezes and works the masonry apart from inside.

**Sealant joints.** At storefront framing, at window and door perimeters, at movement joints. The shortest-lived component on the building and frequently decades past its service life.

**The brick-to-stone junction.** Two materials that absorb and move at different rates, meeting at a line. Deterioration reliably starts there.

## Matching is the whole job on a reviewed elevation

On an ordinary industrial building, a serviceable repair is a serviceable repair. **On a Hinsdale storefront, the repair is judged on how it looks when it is finished** — by the village, by the tenant, and by everyone who walks past it.

So matching gets treated as a deliverable:

- **Mortar color and texture** matched to a cured sample, not a wet one — wet mortar is a different color than cured mortar, and the difference has embarrassed a lot of contractors.
- **Joint profile** tooled to the original — concave, weathered, grapevine, struck. The profile is half of how a wall reads and it also determines how the joint sheds water.
- **Brick** matched by size, color, texture and era. Reclaimed period brick where current production will not match; salvage from a concealed elevation where nothing available is close enough.
- **Stone** repaired rather than replaced wherever the unit can be saved — patching with matched material, or a **Dutchman repair** letting a sound piece of matched stone into a failed area, before considering full replacement.
- **A sample panel** built and reviewed before the elevation proceeds.

And one rule we will not bend: **no sandblasting and no high-pressure washing.** Abrasive cleaning strips the weathered outer skin off limestone and soft brick permanently, leaving a softer, more absorbent surface that deteriorates faster than the one you started with. If cleaning is part of the scope it is the gentlest method that produces an acceptable result, tested on an inconspicuous area first.

## Working on a downtown block that stays open

Hinsdale's business district is tight — narrow lots, active sidewalks, constrained parking, tenants whose revenue depends on the door staying open. Staging is not an afterthought on a job like this; it is a large part of what the job costs and nearly all of what makes it tolerable.

- **Entrances stay usable**, with overhead protection wherever work is above a doorway or public walk
- **Staging and access** are resolved at the assessment, because where the equipment sits determines both price and disruption
- **Noisy cutting** is scheduled away from peak business hours where the scope allows
- **Tenants get written notice** in advance of each elevation
- **The sidewalk gets cleaned daily**, which sounds trivial and is the thing merchants remember

## Phasing a façade scope by risk

Few owners fund an entire façade in one year. The phasing should follow **risk**, not appearance.

| Priority | What belongs here | Why it leads |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Loose units, failing coping, spalled stone above an entrance or sidewalk | Falling material is the only failure that becomes an incident |
| 2 | Active water entry — open coping, corroded lintels, failed flashing | Water inside the wall turns repair into rebuild |
| 3 | Accelerating deterioration on the weather elevation | Quantities grow every season this waits |
| 4 | Appearance — stained, patched or mismatched areas | Real value, no urgency |

Each phase is priced separately in the written scope so an owner can commit to phase one and know exactly what phase two is.

## What drives the cost

**Elevation height and staging** on a constrained downtown lot. The **proportion of stone and cast stone to brick**, because stone work is slower and more skilled. **How much steel** is in the scope. Whether **match samples and submittals** are required. **The extent of damage found once joints are opened.** And **how much of the work has to be staged around open businesses.** We price scope, we say what is excluded, and we say in writing how hidden conditions get handled before anyone signs.
