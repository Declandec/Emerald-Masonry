## The Problem We Get Called About Most Here

Not deterioration — **previous repairs**.

Elmhurst's older housing is desirable and well-maintained, which means a lot of it has been worked on. And a substantial share of that work was done with modern **Portland-based mortar** on brick that cannot take it.

Here is why that matters. **A masonry wall is designed so the mortar is softer than the brick.** Mortar is the sacrificial layer — it absorbs movement and moisture stress and wears out first, because mortar is cheap to replace and hundred-year-old brick is not.

Point a soft pre-1930 wall with hard modern mortar and the relationship inverts. The joints become the strongest element, and every freeze-thaw cycle drives stress into the brick faces instead. Three to eight winters later the faces begin popping off — **permanent loss of original material, caused by the repair.**

| | Historic wall (pre-1930) | Modern wall (post-war) |
|---|---|---|
| **Mortar** | Lime-rich, soft, breathable | Portland-based, hard, dense |
| **Brick** | Softer, variable, often locally made | Machine-made, dense, uniform |
| **Joint prep** | Hand-raked, shallow tools | Power grinding acceptable |
| **If mismatched** | Brick faces spall and are lost | Mortar erodes early |
| **Cleaning** | Gentle only — never blasting | Wider tolerance |
| **Sealing** | Breathable products only | Wider range |

If your brick started flaking a few years after somebody repointed it, that is not coincidence.

## Signs an Elmhurst Historic Home Needs Restoration

- Mortar joints **receded well behind the brick face**, or raking out as loose sand.
- **Spalling brick** — faces flaking, popping or delaminating, worst below sills and near grade.
- **Hard gray mortar standing proud** of softer, warmer-toned original brick — a prior bad repair.
- A **band of spalling** corresponding exactly to where repointing was done.
- Cracked, eroded or displaced **stone sills, lintels and water tables**.
- Open joints and deteriorated crowns on original chimneys — tall and heavily exposed on these houses.
- **Efflorescence** blooming on interior or exterior masonry.
- **Step cracks** through joints at corners and around openings.
- Evidence of **past sandblasting** — an unnaturally rough, open brick surface that now absorbs water freely.

## Our Restoration Process

1. **Assessment and documentation.** We walk the building, photograph conditions, evaluate the existing mortar's hardness and composition, and flag prior inappropriate repairs.
2. **Mortar analysis and matching.** A mix formulated to the original — soft enough to protect the masonry, durable enough to serve.
3. **Hand joint preparation.** Joints raked by hand where the brick is soft; power tools only where masonry can safely take them. Aggressive grinding permanently widens joints and chews the brick arrises.
4. **Undoing bad repairs** where hard mortar has to come out, carefully, without taking more brick with it.
5. **Repointing in lifts**, packed and compacted, then tooled to the original joint profile at the right firmness.
6. **Brick and stone repair.** Failed units replaced with salvaged or reproduction matches; stone restored with dutchman inserts or matched patching rather than wholesale replacement.
7. **Chimneys and trim** restored to shed water properly.
8. **Gentle cleaning, breathable protection.** No sandblasting, no high-pressure washing, no film-forming sealers.

## Why Deferring Costs More on an Old Building

On a century-old wall, waiting is not just a bigger repointing bill later — it is the permanent loss of original material. Once the face spalls off a hundred-year-old brick, that brick is gone, and every replacement is a compromise against the original. Restoration is always cheaper, and always more authentic, when it happens before the units themselves start failing.
