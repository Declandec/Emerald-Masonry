## What CMU block repair actually means

**CMU** stands for *concrete masonry unit* — the grey concrete block that makes up the side and rear walls of most office-warehouse, flex, retail and light-industrial buildings in Orland Park, plus a lot of school, church-hall and multi-unit construction. Some of it is smooth block, some is decorative split-face or ground-face block on the street side, and most of it has been painted at least once.

Block behaves differently from brick. It is more porous, it **shrinks** as it ages rather than expanding, and on most commercial walls it is a single wythe — there is no cavity behind it to drain water away. That means CMU block repair is not one task but four, and they have to be done in order:

| Step | What it fixes | Why order matters |
|---|---|---|
| Crack and unit repair | Shrinkage cracks, step cracks, broken or spalled block | Coating over a moving crack just cracks the coating |
| Control-joint resealing | Failed sealant in vertical movement joints | These are full-height water paths once the caulk fails |
| Repointing | Eroded or cracked mortar joints | Coatings will not bridge open joints |
| Block filler + breathable coating | The porous block face itself | Only lasts if everything beneath it is closed |

## Signs your Orland Park block building needs repair, not just paint

- **Peeling, blistering or bubbling paint**, especially in vertical stripes or low on the wall.
- **Vertical cracks** running through block — often directly beside or at a failed control joint.
- **Stair-step cracks** following the mortar joints, common near corners and openings.
- **Cracked or missing sealant** in control joints and around door and window frames.
- **White salt bloom (efflorescence)** pushing through paint — a sign of water moving through the wall.
- **Dark damp patches** on the interior face of exterior walls after wind-driven rain.
- **Chipped or broken block** at dock doors, corners and drive aisles from vehicle impact.
- **Rust staining** at steel lintels over doors and overhead doors.

## Risks of painting over failed CMU

If the block is repainted without repair, the owner pays for a coating that cannot do its job. Water still enters through cracks and joints, gets trapped behind a coating that is too tight to let it out, and the paint delaminates — usually within a couple of winters. Meanwhile freeze-thaw keeps opening the cracks, the interior finish at the back of the tenant space keeps getting wet, and the next tenant inherits a leak the building owner thought was solved. Repainting also hides cracks from the next inspection, so problems grow unnoticed.

## Our CMU block repair process in Orland Park

1. **Walk every elevation**, including the rear and the roof edge, and map cracks, joints, coating failure and damaged units on a site plan.
2. **Find the water source.** Open parapet caps, failed roof-edge flashing, grade and downspout problems are separated from the block itself.
3. **Repair cracks** — routing and filling non-structural cracks, and replacing broken or spalled units with matched block (split-face matched for face texture where it shows).
4. **Cut out and reseal control joints** with backer rod and a high-movement sealant.
5. **Repoint** eroded or cracked mortar joints with compatible mortar.
6. **Apply block filler and a breathable coating** — or hand off to your painter with the wall ready to coat, if you prefer to use your own.
7. **Document**: photos, quantities completed, and lien waivers with each payment.

If a crack shows signs of structural movement — offset faces, widening over time, bowing walls — we will tell you so and recommend an engineer's review before masonry repair.

## Materials we use

Replacement CMU matched for size, face and colour; Type N or Type S mortar depending on the wall; closed-cell backer rod with urethane or hybrid sealant for control joints; acrylic block filler; and **vapour-permeable elastomeric or acrylic masonry coatings** suitable for exterior single-wythe block. We avoid film-forming sealers and non-breathable paints on block walls.

## What drives the cost of CMU block repair

- **Number and type of cracks** — hairline shrinkage cracks versus step cracks and broken units.
- **Linear footage of control joints** to cut out and reseal.
- **Unit replacement** — especially matching decorative split-face block.
- **Coating area** — spot repair versus full filler-and-coat.
- **Access** — boom or scissor lifts, work around docks and parked cars.
- **Phasing** around tenants, deliveries and business hours.
