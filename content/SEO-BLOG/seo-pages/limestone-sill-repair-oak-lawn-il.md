## The Three Sills on Oak Lawn Homes

### Natural limestone sills
Cut from quarried stone, usually Indiana limestone. You can often see faint bedding lines and a slightly varied texture. Limestone is durable but porous; it fails by **delaminating** along those layers when trapped water freezes, by cracking across the span, and by losing its slope if the mortar bed underneath settles.

### Cast stone sills
Precast concrete made to look like limestone, common on post-war and later homes. Cast stone is uniform in color, sometimes shows small air pockets, and has a crisply molded drip groove. It fails differently: **surface scaling**, cracking from corroding reinforcement on longer sills, and open end joints.

### Brick rowlock sills
A course of brick laid on edge and tilted outward, very common on bungalows and ranches. It has no stone at all; its weak points are the **mortar joints running across the sill**, which take direct rain and ice. Once those joints open, water goes straight down into the wall below.

Knowing which one you have tells you what repair makes sense. A spalled limestone sill may want a dutchman; a scaling cast sill may be better replaced; a rowlock sill usually needs repointing, sometimes relaying, with the right outward pitch.

## Signs Your Oak Lawn Window Sill Needs Repair

- **Flaking or spalled brick directly under a window**, often the first symptom anyone notices.
- **A sill that holds water** or tilts back toward the window instead of shedding outward.
- **Open or cracked joints** at the ends of the sill, or across a rowlock sill.
- **Cracks through the stone**, especially mid-span on wide picture-window sills.
- **Peeling paint or stains on the interior sill and drywall** below a window.
- **A drip groove painted over or filled in**, so water wraps under the sill instead of dropping clear.

## Why Waiting Costs More

A failing sill rarely stays a sill problem. Water that gets behind it soaks the brick and the steel **lintel** at the window below on a two-story home, rusts it, and starts the freeze-thaw cycle that breaks up face brick. Caught early, the job is a sill repair and some joint work. Left several winters, it becomes [brick repair](/services/brick-repair) under every affected window and sometimes [lintel repair](/services/lintel-repair) as well.

## Our Limestone Sill Repair Process

1. **Identify the sill and the water path.** Limestone, cast stone or rowlock; slope, drip, end joints, the brick below.
2. **Decide repair, reset or replace.** We save sound stone. Loose pieces and badly eroded units get replaced with matched material.
3. **Repair the stone.** Pin cracks, cut out damaged sections and fit **dutchman** inserts, or apply matched patching mortar on minor loss.
4. **Reset to slope.** Sills that have rolled back are lifted and rebedded with a positive outward pitch.
5. **Rowlock work.** Grind and repoint the cross joints, or relay the course where bricks have loosened.
6. **Seal the ends correctly.** Flexible [caulking](/services/caulking) over backer rod at the stone-to-brick ends, not hard mortar.
7. **Repair the brick below** with matched units and mortar, so the patch disappears into the wall.
