## Why the Parapet Goes First

Every other wall on a building has a warm side. A parapet does not. It stands above the roof with weather on the front, weather on the back, weather on top, and no heat behind it — so it stays wetter than any masonry on the property and cycles through more freezing and thawing.

Then there is the coping joint. Once the joints between coping units open, rain goes **into the top of the wall** rather than off it, and that is the one direction masonry has no defence against. Everything below gets wet from above.

And the back face — the roof-side face nobody looks at — is routinely in far worse condition than the street face. We have opened plenty of parapets that were presentable from the sidewalk and structurally spent from the roof.

## Repair or Rebuild: The Actual Criteria

| Condition found | Correct scope |
|---|---|
| Joints failed, masonry plumb and holding its bond | Repoint; address coping joints and flashing |
| Coping loose or joints open, wall otherwise sound | Reset coping, restore flashing, repoint |
| Top courses have lost their bond; mortar sandy through the section | Rebuild the affected courses |
| Wall leaning, bulging or rotating outward | Take down and rebuild, with flashing and anchorage restored |
| Deterioration continuing below the roof line | Rebuild down to sound masonry — deeper than the roof view suggests |

Repointing a parapet that needs rebuilding is the most common waste of money on these walls. Fresh joints on courses that have already lost their bond look correct for a season and change nothing structurally.

## The Two-Trade Seam

This is the part worth getting written down before anyone mobilises:

- **Through-wall flashing** inside the parapet — under the coping and at the base of the wall — is **masonry** work
- **Base flashing** and the roof membrane termination up the parapet's inside face is **roofing** work
- **Counterflashing**, let into a cut reglet in the masonry joint, sits at the boundary
- **Coping** is masonry, but its joints interact with the roofing detail directly below

Failures cluster where responsibility is assumed rather than assigned: counterflashing surface-caulked to the brick instead of let into a joint, new membrane terminated against masonry that was about to be rebuilt, or through-wall flashing omitted because each trade thought the other had it.

We define that boundary in the written scope, and where you have a roofer we will coordinate sequencing directly with them. ## Our Repair Process

1. **Inspect from the roof, not the street.** The back face and the roof tie-in tell the real story; the sidewalk view hides it.
2. **Sound and plumb the wall.** Drummy masonry and lean determine repair versus rebuild.
3. **Define the trade boundary** with any roofing scope, in writing.
4. **Protect below** — overhead protection over sidewalks and entrances before anything is disturbed.
5. **Take down what is spent**, to sound material, salvaging usable units.
6. **Rebuild plumb** with matched brick in mortar matched for colour, texture and hardness.
7. **Install through-wall flashing** so water entering the wall drains back out.
8. **Reset the coping**, bedded correctly with properly sealed joints.
9. **Coordinate the roof edge** so counterflashing and membrane meet the masonry cleanly.
