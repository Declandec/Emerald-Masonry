## What Brick Repair Involves

Brick repair means addressing the brick units themselves — distinct from tuckpointing, which repairs the mortar joints between them. Real brick repair covers:

- **Replacing spalled or broken brick** — cutting out failed units and setting matching ones.
- **Repairing cracked brick and stair-step cracking** — which often signals movement or a rusting lintel behind the wall.
- **Resetting loose or displaced brick** — common at corners, sills, and parapets.
- **Fixing the underlying cause** — a failed joint, a rusted lintel, missing flashing, or a worn sealer letting water in.

**Good brick repair always starts with the question "why did this brick fail?" — because the answer decides whether you are replacing five brick or fixing a leak behind the whole wall.**

## Signs Your Schaumburg Brick Needs Repair

Walk your home or building and look for:

- **Spalling** — the fired face flaking, popping, or crumbling off, often leaving a rough, lighter-colored crater.
- **Cracked brick** — single units or stair-step cracks climbing through the mortar joints.
- **Loose or bulging brick** — areas pushing out of plane.
- **White efflorescence staining** — a sign water is moving through the wall.
- **Brick fragments on the ground** near the foundation, under windows, or at the chimney.
- **Rust stains and cracking above windows and doors** — a classic sign of a corroding steel lintel.

## The Risk of Waiting

Spalling and cracking are progressive. Once the protective fired face is gone, the soft interior of the brick soaks up water freely, and Schaumburg's freeze-thaw winters expand that water and accelerate the breakdown. One failed brick becomes a cluster; a hairline crack becomes a path for water into the wall cavity. On commercial buildings along the Golf Road and Woodfield corridor, that often shows up as parapet and lintel damage that becomes a roof-edge leak. Catching it early keeps a brick repair from turning into a structural rebuild.

## Our Brick Repair Process

1. **Diagnose the cause.** We determine why the brick failed — joints, lintel rust, flashing, splashback, or a sealer issue — so the repair lasts.
2. **Remove the failed brick.** We carefully cut out the damaged units without disturbing the sound surrounding masonry.
3. **Source matching brick.** We match size, color, and texture as closely as the original allows, including salvaged units when needed.
4. **Reset and repoint.** We lay the new brick and point the joints with color-matched mortar of the correct type.
5. **Address the water source.** We repair the lintel, joints, flashing, or sealer that let water in — the step that makes the repair permanent.
6. **Clean down.** We leave the brick face clean and the repair blended into the wall.

## Materials and Brick Matching

The hardest part of brick repair is usually the match. Much of Schaumburg's residential stock dates from the 1960s through the 1990s, and brick from that era is often still available or matchable by dimension, color blend, and texture; for older or discontinued brick we source salvaged units. We point with the correct mortar type for the wall — a mortar harder than the brick will, over freeze-thaw cycles, push damage back into the brick face.

## What Affects the Price

We won't pretend a per-brick number means anything. The real cost drivers are:

- **Quantity and spread** — a handful of brick versus a large failing section.
- **Location and access** — ground-floor work versus second-story, parapet, or anything needing staging.
- **Brick matching** — easy match versus tracking down salvaged or custom units.
- **Underlying cause** — replacing brick is one thing; a rusted lintel or a leak behind the wall adds real scope.

**Why are the brick on my Schaumburg house spalling?**

Spalling happens when water gets into the brick, freezes, and pops the hard fired face off. In Schaumburg's freeze-thaw climate the usual culprits are failed mortar joints, a missing or worn sealer, splashback near grade, or rust expanding behind a steel lintel.
