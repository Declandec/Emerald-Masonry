## What Chimney Repair Actually Covers

"Chimney repair" is a catch-all for several distinct repairs, and a good contractor tells you which ones your chimney actually needs:

- **Crown repair** — the concrete cap on top that sheds water; it cracks and lets water straight down into the masonry.
- **Tuckpointing/repointing** — grinding out and replacing failed mortar joints in the chimney stack.
- **Flashing repair** — the metal seal where the chimney meets the roof, a leading source of leaks.
- **Brick replacement** — cutting out spalled or broken brick and replacing it with matching units.
- **Partial or full rebuild** — taking the chimney down to a sound course and rebuilding when the upper section is too far gone.

**The single most important thing about chimney repair is matching the fix to the failure — sealing a crown when the real problem is washed-out joints just wastes money.**

## Signs Your Crestwood Chimney Needs Attention

From the ground, with binoculars or a phone camera zoom, look for:

- **White, chalky staining (efflorescence)** on the brick — water is moving through the masonry.
- **A cracked or crumbling crown** at the very top.
- **Missing, recessed, or sandy mortar joints** in the stack.
- **Spalled brick** — faces flaking, popping, or lying in the gutter.
- **Rusted, lifting, or tarred-over flashing** where the chimney meets the roof.
- **Damp spots, staining, or a musty smell** on the ceiling or wall around the chimney inside.

If you see any of these, it is worth a look. **A cracked chimney crown is one of the most common and most overlooked causes of interior water damage in Chicago-area homes.**

## The Risk of Waiting

A chimney fails from the top down. A hairline crack in the crown lets water into the masonry; Crestwood's freeze-thaw winters then expand that water inside the brick and joints, prying the structure apart a little more each cycle. What begins as a $-modest crown seal can become spalled brick, then washed-out joints, then a leaning stack that has to be rebuilt — and by then the water has often reached the roof deck, the attic, or the ceiling below. Chimneys are also a safety item: a deteriorated flue or crumbling structure is not something to ride out another winter.

## What Affects the Price

We won't quote a chimney sight-unseen. The honest cost drivers are:

**How do I know if my chimney needs repair or a full rebuild?**

If the damage is limited to the crown, the flashing, or surface mortar joints, a targeted repair usually fixes it. If the brick above the roofline is leaning, the joints are washed out top to bottom, or the structure is spalling badly, a partial rebuild from a sound course up is often the smarter long-term fix. A close-up inspection is the only way to tell for sure.

**Why is there white staining on my Crestwood chimney?**

That chalky white film is efflorescence — mineral salts left behind as water moves through the masonry. It is a sign water is getting into the chimney, usually through a cracked crown or failed joints. Left alone, that same water freezes and thaws each winter and accelerates the damage.
