## Lintel repair in New Lenox: the brick-front house problem

Much of New Lenox was built from the 1970s onward, and a large share of its homes are **brick-front**: brick across the front face, siding on the sides and back. On these houses, the lintels over the garage, front windows and entry carry the brick, and their ends sit right at the **corner returns**, the short runs of brick that wrap the corners before the siding starts.

Those corners are weak points. Water runs down behind the brick, reaches the lintel, and at the ends often finds a gap in the flashing or a joint between brick and siding that has opened up. Rust starts there and pushes the brick up and outward.

| What you see | Where | Likely cause |

| Crack rising from a garage door corner | Lintel bearing end | Rust jacking at the end of the lintel |

## Signs you need lintel repair

- **Rust** or flaking steel visible over openings.
- **Cracks** at garage door corners or above window corners.
- **Gaps** where the brick front meets siding or trim.
- **Sagging or bowed** brick over a wide garage opening.
- **Split caulk** at lintel ends and window heads.
- **Garage doors or windows** that have started to bind.

## Risks of waiting

Rusting steel keeps expanding, so cracks widen every year and water gets into the wall framing behind the brick-front. Garage lintels carry the most brick on the house, and a badly corroded one can sag. Replacing a lintel early is a contained job; waiting can mean rebuilding more brick and repairing rotted framing and trim.

## How we repair lintels in New Lenox

1. **Inspect every opening** on the brick-front and note which lintels need replacement.
2. **Support the brick** above with temporary shoring.
3. **Remove the brick** over the opening and at the ends, and take out the old steel.
4. **Install new steel** sized to the span, primed or galvanized, with full bearing at each end.
5. **Flash with end dams** so water is turned out at the ends, not into the wall.
6. **Rebuild the brick and corner returns** with matched brick and mortar, and open the weep holes.
7. **Seal the joints** between brick, siding and trim, and walk you through the finished work.

## Materials we use

Primed or hot-dip galvanized steel angle lintels sized to the opening; flexible flashing with formed end dams and drip edges; weep vents; matched face brick and mortar; and suitable sealant at brick-to-siding and trim joints. Wide garage openings may call for heavier steel, and we tell you when an engineer should size it.
