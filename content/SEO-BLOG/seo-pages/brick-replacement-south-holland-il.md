## What brick replacement is — and how it differs from brick repair

Brick replacement is the process of removing individual brick units that are spalled, cracked, or structurally failed and installing new, matched brick in their place. It is a different job from **[brick repair](/services/brick-repair)**, which addresses mortar joints and minor surface issues, and from **[tuckpointing](/services/tuckpointing)**, which removes and renews deteriorated mortar between otherwise sound brick.

The simplest way to think about it:

> If the mortar is failing, you tuckpoint. If the brick itself is failing, you replace it.

When a brick's face is shedding, popping off, or breaking into pieces, no amount of repointing will save it — the unit has lost its structural skin and will keep deteriorating. That's when **[brick replacement](/services/brick-replacement)** is the right call. Our process involves carefully cutting out the failed units without disturbing the sound brick around them, then setting matched replacements.

## Signs you need brick replacement

Not every cracked brick needs to come out, but these signs usually mean replacement, not just repair:

- **Face shedding or spalling** — the outer surface of the brick is flaking, peeling, or sloughing off, exposing the softer core.
- **Popped faces** — the front of the brick has blown off entirely, leaving a recessed, rough hole.
- **Crumbling units** — brick that breaks apart or turns to powder when touched.
- **Prior bad patches** — previous repairs done with the wrong mortar or smeared-on filler that trapped moisture and made things worse.
- **Cracked or split brick** — units cracked through, often near lintels, corners, or at grade.
- **Bowing or bulging** — a section of wall pushing outward, which can signal failed brick and needs prompt assessment.

> Spalled brick doesn't heal. Once the face is gone, water gets in faster — and the damage spreads to neighboring units.

If you're seeing several of these on a South Holland home, it's worth a free, no-pressure look.

## The risks of waiting

Damaged brick is a water problem first and a cosmetic problem second. A spalled or popped brick exposes its porous core, which soaks up rain and snowmelt. In South Holland's climate, that water freezes, expands, and breaks the surrounding masonry apart — so one failed brick quickly becomes five. Left long enough, moisture migrates behind the wall, rots sheathing, rusts steel lintels, and can compromise the structure. Replacing a handful of brick now is far cheaper than rebuilding a wall section later.

## Materials: matching matters

The difference between a professional brick replacement and an obvious patch is the match. We pay attention to:

- **Brick matching** — size (older South Holland brick can differ from modern dimensions), texture (smooth, wire-cut, tumbled), and color blend across the wall.
- **Mortar match** — we tint and mix mortar to match the existing color, and we tool the joints to the same profile (concave, raked, weathered) so the repair reads as original.

A correct mortar match is also a performance issue: the wrong mortar can be too hard for older brick and cause more spalling, so we select the mix to suit your wall.
