## What a Lintel Is, and Why It Corrodes

A **lintel** is the beam carrying masonry across an opening. On most Frankfort commercial and multi-tenant stock it is a bare, ungalvanized **steel angle** set into the wall above a storefront, window, or overhead door.

Steel in masonry survives only while it stays dry. Water reaches it through an open joint above, a failed sill, missing or deteriorated flashing, or a sealant line nobody has touched in decades. It corrodes — and corroding steel **expands to several times its original volume**, with nowhere to go but up and out.

That is **rust jacking**: the horizontal crack above the opening, the lifted course, the brick pushed out of plane. Owners tuckpoint the crack; it returns in two seasons, because the crack was never the problem. The early sign is usually staining, covered in [lintel rust stains and orange streaks on brick](/blog/lintel-rust-stains-orange-streaks-brick).

## Treat or Replace

| Finding at the steel | Proportionate scope |
|---|---|
| Light surface corrosion, full section, sound bearing, no deflection | **Treat** — clean back, treat, prime, flash, repoint above |
| Scaling rust, no measurable section loss or movement | **Treat**, correct the water source, monitor |
| Section loss, pitting through, or swelling lifting the course | **Replace** |
| Measurable deflection or sag at mid-span | **Replace** |
| Loss of bearing at either end | **Replace**, rebuild the bearing pockets |
| Masonry above already loose or displaced | **Replace**, with shoring before removal |

The honest rows are the top two. A lintel with surface corrosion and full section is not an emergency, and saying so costs us the larger invoice.

## The Warning Signs

- A **horizontal crack** in the joint immediately above an opening
- **Orange staining** bleeding from the steel or running down the masonry below
- Brick above the opening **lifted, stepped or bowed** outward
- A **visible sag** at mid-span on a wide storefront or overhead-door header
- **Step cracks** angling up from the opening corners
- Doors or windows that **bind** under a masonry opening
- **Loose or drummy masonry** directly over the head — treat as urgent

## Our Repair Process

1. **Assess every opening on the elevation**, not just the reported one, and check bearing at both ends.
2. **Shore the masonry above** before any steel is disturbed — this is what separates a controlled replacement from a wall coming down over an entrance.
3. **Remove the course carefully and salvage** sound units, kept in order for reuse.
4. **Treat or replace the steel.** New angle is sized to the span, set with proper bearing, and protected against corrosion so the repair does not become the next generation's problem.
5. **Flash and drain the head** — through-wall flashing and weeps are what keep water off the steel; skipping this is why replacements fail early.
6. **Rebuild the masonry**, matched for colour, texture and mortar hardness, tooled to the existing joint profile.
7. **Protect below throughout** — overhead protection wherever the opening is a walkway or entrance.
