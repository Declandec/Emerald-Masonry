## What Chimney Repair Covers

"Chimney repair" is really a family of related fixes, and a good contractor matches the repair to the failure instead of selling one solution for every problem. On a typical Elmhurst chimney we handle:

- **Crown repair or replacement** — resealing or rebuilding the sloped cap that sheds water off the top of the stack.
- **Flashing repair** — resealing or replacing the metal that waterproofs the joint where the chimney passes through the roof.
- **Chimney tuckpointing** — grinding out failed mortar joints and repacking them with fresh, color-matched mortar.
- **Spalled and cracked brick replacement** — cutting out flaking or broken bricks and toothing in sound matched units.
- **Partial or full rebuild** — taking the stack down to a solid course (often the roofline) and rebuilding it when it's too far gone to patch.

### The building science: why chimneys fail

Brick and mortar are porous. They absorb water, and in the Chicago area that water freezes and thaws over and over from November through March. Each freeze expands the trapped moisture and pushes the masonry apart from the inside — the **freeze-thaw cycle**. On a chimney, that damage concentrates at the crown, the top few courses of brick, and the mortar joints, because those surfaces stay wettest. Add a cracked crown or failed flashing feeding extra water in, and deterioration accelerates fast.

## Signs Your Elmhurst Chimney Needs Repair

Most chimney problems are visible from the ground or the roofline if you know what to look for:

- **A crumbling or cracked crown** — chunks of the concrete cap missing or split.
- **Spalling brick** — faces of the brick flaking, popping, or shedding, often worst on the weather side.
- **White staining (efflorescence)** — a chalky white bloom on the brick, a reliable signal that water is moving through the masonry.
- **Daylight or gaps** — open mortar joints or visible separation between the chimney and the house.
- **A leaning or tilting stack** — a serious sign that the base or mortar has failed.
- **Water in the firebox or stains on the ceiling** near the chimney chase.
- **Rusted or lifting flashing** at the roofline.

If you're seeing rust-colored staining, sandy mortar you can scrape with a key, or brick faces on the ground after a hard freeze, the chimney is telling you it's time. The same freeze-thaw forces that attack a chimney also work on the rest of the house, which is why chimney issues often show up alongside the need for [expert tuckpointing and mortar joint repair](/services/tuckpointing) elsewhere on the brick.

## The Risk of Waiting

A chimney rarely fails gracefully. A cracked crown lets water into the stack, the water spalls the brick and washes out the mortar, and what would have been a crown seal and some tuckpointing becomes a full rebuild a couple of winters later. Worse, a chimney that keeps taking on water can begin to lean, and a leaning masonry stack is both a structural and a safety problem — falling brick is a real hazard on a two-story Elmhurst home. Catching it early is almost always the cheaper and safer path.

## Our Chimney Repair Process

1. **Inspect and diagnose.** We examine the crown, flashing, brick, and mortar, and trace where water is actually getting in — not just where it shows up inside.
2. **Repair the water source first.** Crown and flashing come before cosmetic work, because sealing joints under a broken crown just traps water.
3. **Tuckpoint or replace brick.** We grind out failed joints and repack them, and cut out and tooth in matched brick where faces have spalled.
4. **Rebuild when needed.** If the stack is past patching, we take it down to a sound course and rebuild it with matched brick and proper mortar.
5. **Cure and seal.** New mortar is allowed to cure, and we recommend breathable sealing where it makes sense to slow future water absorption.
