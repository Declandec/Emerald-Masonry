## What foundation masonry repair covers

Foundation masonry repair restores the brick, block, and mortar that make up your foundation wall. For a North Aurora home, that typically includes:

- **Repointing and repairing brick & block foundations** — grinding out failed, crumbling mortar joints below and at grade and packing in fresh, matched mortar to lock the wall back together.
- **Parging** — applying a smooth, protective parge coat over block or brick to shed water, hide surface damage, and add a moisture barrier.
- **Crack repair** — properly preparing and filling cracks in foundation masonry, including stair-step cracks that follow the mortar joints.
- **Rebuilding deteriorated sections** — when block or brick has spalled, flaked, or broken down past the point of patching, we take out the failed units and rebuild that section with matched material.
- **Addressing water entry at grade** — repairing and sealing the masonry where the foundation meets the soil line, the spot where most water and freeze-thaw damage starts.

## Signs your North Aurora foundation needs masonry repair

Catching foundation masonry problems early keeps the repair small. Watch for:

- **Crumbling mortar below grade** — joints that have turned to sand or gaps you can dig out with a screwdriver.
- **Spalling or flaking block** — the face of the block popping, flaking, or crumbling off, often from trapped moisture and freeze-thaw.
- **Stair-step cracks** — cracks that zig-zag up through the mortar joints of a block wall.
- **A persistently damp basement** — moisture, efflorescence (white powder), or musty smells along the foundation wall.
- **Bowing** — any inward lean or bulge in the foundation wall, which should be inspected promptly.

If you see one or more of these on a North Aurora home, it's worth a look. Many of these properties — especially older homes near the river — have foundations that have been quietly weathering saturated soil for decades.

## The risk of waiting

Foundation masonry damage is progressive. Open mortar joints and cracks let water into the wall; in a North Aurora winter that water freezes, expands, and pries the masonry further apart. Each freeze-thaw cycle makes the next one worse. What starts as a few soft joints can spread into spalled block, water intrusion, and ultimately whole sections that have to be rebuilt instead of repointed. Repairing and sealing early is almost always cheaper than rebuilding later.

> Foundations fail at the soil line first. Sealing the masonry where the wall meets the ground is the single most effective step against Fox Valley groundwater and freeze-thaw.
