## What Tuckpointing Actually Does

Mortar is engineered to be the **sacrificial** part of a brick wall. It is meant to be softer than the brick so that as the wall expands, contracts and takes on moisture, the mortar absorbs the stress and wears out first — because mortar is cheap to replace and brick is not.

**Repointing is the structural half of the work: grinding or raking the failed mortar out to a sound depth, then packing fresh mortar into the joint in layers.** Tuckpointing, as most homeowners use the word, includes tooling that joint so the finished repair matches the profile the house was built with. Emerald Masonry does both in one operation.

Once joints open up, water runs behind the brick face instead of off it. In a Chicagoland winter that water freezes, expands about nine percent, and starts pushing the wall apart from the inside. That is the whole mechanism, and repointing is what interrupts it.

## Signs Your Burbank Home Needs Tuckpointing

- **Mortar you can dig out with a screwdriver** — the fastest honest test there is.
- **Receded joints** sitting noticeably behind the face of the brick, meaning the joint has lost the depth it needs to shed water.
- **Sandy grit** accumulating on sills, steps and along the foundation line.
- **Step cracks** running diagonally through the joints, often at corners or near the garage.
- **Efflorescence**, the chalky white bloom that means water is moving through the wall.
- **Spalling brick faces** — flaking or popping, which means water has been getting in for years already.
- **An open-jointed or cracked chimney**, exposed on four sides and always the fastest-failing masonry on the property.
- **A deteriorating detached garage**, which in Burbank is frequently in worse shape than the house.

## What Waiting Costs

Deferred tuckpointing does not stay a tuckpointing problem. Open joints admit water; freeze-thaw then spalls the brick faces, rusts the steel lintels over windows and doors, saturates the chimney, and eventually loosens brick outright. The repair ladder climbs fast — repointing, then brick replacement, then rebuilding a section, then rebuilding a chimney. Repointing at the right moment is by a wide margin the cheapest rung.

## Our Tuckpointing Process

1. **Free on-site assessment.** Every elevation, plus the chimney, garage, sills, lintels and grade line — and where water is actually entering.
2. **Mortar matching.** Color, sand and, critically, hardness matched to your existing mortar.
3. **Grinding and raking.** Failed mortar cut out to a sound depth, typically two to two and a half times the joint width — never skimmed over.
4. **Cleaning the joints** so fresh mortar bonds to clean masonry.
5. **Repointing in layers**, packed and compacted so no voids remain behind the face.
6. **Tooling** to match the original joint profile at the right firmness.
7. **Cleaning and proper cure**, protected as conditions require.

## What Drives Tuckpointing Cost in Burbank

- **Extent** — one elevation versus the full house, plus chimney and garage.
- **Height and access** — ground level versus a second story, a chimney above the roofline, or tight alley access to a detached garage.
- **Existing mortar hardness** — post-war Portland-based mortar takes longer to grind out than soft lime mortar.
- **Matching difficulty** — an unusual brick color or sand blend takes more work to match convincingly.
- **Additional damage found** — spalled brick, a rusting lintel, a failed chimney crown or bad sills discovered during the walk.
- **Season and curing conditions** — cold-weather work needs protection and adds time.
