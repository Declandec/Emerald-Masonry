A chimney is the hardest-working masonry on your house. It stands above the roofline exposed to wind, rain, and sun on every side, and it has no overhang to shed water. In Lombard's climate that exposure adds up fast — which is why chimneys are usually the first masonry on a home to fail.

## What chimney repair actually covers

"Chimney repair" is a group of related fixes, not a single task. On a typical Lombard chimney we address:

- **The crown** — the concrete or mortar cap at the very top. When it cracks, water pours straight into the masonry below.
- **The brick and mortar** — repointing open joints and replacing spalled or loose brick.
- **The flashing connection** — where the chimney meets the roof, the most common leak point on the whole structure.
- **Waterproofing** — a breathable sealer applied after repairs to slow future absorption.

The goal is always the same: stop water from getting into the chimney, because water is what drives nearly every chimney failure.

## Signs your Lombard chimney needs attention

Homeowners usually notice one of these first:

- Pieces of brick face flaking off, or brick fragments on the roof or in the yard (spalling).
- White, chalky staining on the chimney (efflorescence — a sign water is moving through the masonry).
- Crumbling, sandy, or missing mortar between the bricks.
- A cracked or broken crown at the top.
- Water stains on the ceiling or wall near the chimney, or a damp smell in the firebox.
- The chimney visibly leaning or pulling away from the house.

If you can see daylight in a mortar joint or a chunk missing from the crown, water is already getting in.

## The risk of waiting

A small crown crack or a few open joints is inexpensive to correct. Left alone through a few Lombard winters, that same water saturates the brick, freezes, and spalls the masonry — and once enough brick faces pop, repointing is no longer enough and the structure needs rebuilding. Worse, a leaning or badly deteriorated chimney becomes a safety concern. Early repair is almost always the smaller, cheaper job.

## Our chimney repair process

1. **Inspection.** We examine the crown, brick, mortar joints, and the flashing connection, and trace where water is actually entering.
2. **Diagnosis.** You get a straight answer on whether a repair or a partial rebuild is the right call — and why.
3. **Crown repair or replacement.** We repair cracks or rebuild the crown so it sheds water away from the brick.
4. **Repointing and brick replacement.** Failed mortar is ground out and repointed; spalled brick is replaced with the closest available match.
5. **Flashing and water path.** We correct the masonry-to-roof connection so the leak is closed at its source.
6. **Waterproofing.** A breathable masonry sealer is applied to slow future water absorption without trapping moisture.

## Repair or rebuild? How we decide in Lombard

The dividing line is usually the roofline. Damage to the crown, flashing, and the top few courses is repairable. When the brick **above** the roofline is widely spalling, leaning, or has open joints across more than about a third of its face, a **partial rebuild from the roofline up** is the durable answer — it's almost always limited to the exposed portion, since the masonry inside the warm house rarely fails.
