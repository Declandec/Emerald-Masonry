## What Tuckpointing (Repointing) Actually Is

Tuckpointing, also called repointing, is the repair of the mortar joints between your bricks — not the bricks themselves. Over time those thin mortar lines break down faster than the brick, eroding back, cracking, and turning sandy. Tuckpointing removes the deteriorated mortar to a sound depth and replaces it with fresh, color-matched mortar.

In plain terms, the brick is usually fine — it's the "glue" between the bricks that wears out. **Tuckpointing restores the mortar joints that hold a brick wall together and keep water out, and it is the single most important maintenance a brick home gets.** Done on time, it is dramatically cheaper than letting a wall fail and rebuilding it.

## Signs Your Manhattan Home Needs Tuckpointing

Walk the perimeter of the house and the chimney and look for:

- **Crumbling or sandy mortar** — if you can scrape the joint with a screwdriver or rub it to powder, it has failed.
- **Recessed, gapped, or missing mortar** — joints eroded back behind the brick face.
- **Hairline cracks running along the joints.**
- **Spalling brick** — faces flaking or popping, a sign water has already gotten behind the wall.
- **White efflorescence staining** — minerals left behind as water moves through the masonry.
- **Damp spots inside** near a chimney or exterior brick wall.

**Mortar you can rub to sand with your thumb is a clear signal the wall needs tuckpointing — and the weather-exposed chimney is usually the first place it shows.**

## The Risk of Waiting

Mortar joints are a brick wall's first line of defense against water. Once they fail, rain and snowmelt soak in — and out here in Will County the freeze-thaw cycle takes over, freezing that trapped water, expanding it, and prying the wall apart a little more each winter. Soft joints become spalled brick, then lost brick faces, then damaged units that must be cut out and replaced, and in the worst cases a chimney or wall that needs partial rebuilding. Timely tuckpointing stops a small maintenance item from becoming a structural repair.

## Our Tuckpointing Process, Step by Step

1. **Assess.** We inspect the joints, the brick, and the existing mortar and map which areas need work and how deep the deterioration runs.
2. **Grind out the joints.** We cut the failed mortar out to proper depth — typically at least twice the joint width — without chipping the brick.
3. **Match the mortar.** We match color, texture, and type to your existing masonry, which matters most on older brick.
4. **Repoint and tool.** We pack the joints with fresh mortar and tool them to the correct profile so they shed water and match the original look.
5. **Clean.** We clean the brick face so the finished wall looks crisp, not smeared.

**Proper tuckpointing means grinding the joint out to depth — not smearing new mortar over the surface, a shortcut that fails within a few years and is one of the most common things we get called to fix.**

## Materials and Mortar Matching

Mortar is not one-size-fits-all. We typically use **type N** for most above-grade residential walls and **type O** or higher-lime mixes for softer, older brick that needs a more forgiving joint. The match is critical: a mortar harder than the brick will, over years of expansion and contraction, drive damage into the brick face itself. Getting the color and lime content right is the difference between a repair that disappears into the wall and one that stands out and shortens the masonry's life.

## What Affects the Price

We can't quote tuckpointing sight-unseen. The honest cost drivers are:

- **Mortar matching** — custom color and texture matching on older brick takes more care.
- **Joint depth and condition** — widespread, deeply eroded failure is more labor than isolated touch-ups.
