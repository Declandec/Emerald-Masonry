## Limestone sill repair in Tinley Park: the search sends you to the wrong shop

When we searched for limestone sill repair in Tinley Park, most results were directory pages for **stone polishing and countertop restoration**, plus general brick and siding listings. A polishing shop restores interior stone. It does not reset a sill set in mortar under a window, flash it or rebuild the brick ends around it.

So the first question is what you actually have under your windows, because Tinley Park homes use three kinds of sill and each one fails differently:

| Sill type | What it looks like | How it usually fails | Typical repair |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Natural limestone** | Buff or gray stone, slight natural texture, one long piece | Cracks across the middle, spalled face, open end joints | Pin and patch, dutchman, or replace in matching stone |
| **Cast stone (precast)** | Very even color and edges, sometimes with a visible aggregate | Corner chips, surface scaling, rust spots from reinforcing | Patch, or replace with a new precast unit |
| **Brick rowlock** | A row of bricks set on edge, tilted outward | Loose or cracked bricks, washed-out joints, water at the ends | Rebuild the rowlock with matched brick and mortar |

We confirm the sill type and condition on site before recommending anything.

## Signs your sill needs repair

- **A crack** running across the sill, often near the middle or under a mullion.
- **Spalling**: the face or front edge flaking off in layers.
- **A sill that tips back** toward the window instead of sloping out.
- **Open or cracked mortar joints** under the sill or at its ends.
- **Split sealant** between the sill and the window frame.
- **Water stains** on the interior drywall or trim below the window.
- **Rust stains** or chipped corners on cast stone sills.
- **Loose bricks** in a rowlock sill.

## Risks of waiting

A cracked or back-pitched sill lets water run into the wall right under the window. That water rots the window framing and interior trim, rusts the steel lintel of the window below on a two-storey wall, and freezes in the crack every winter, splitting the stone further. A crack that could have been pinned and patched in one season can become a sill that has to be replaced, along with the brick around it.

## How we repair limestone sills in Tinley Park

1. **Inspect every sill** on the house and note which need patching, piecing in or replacement.
2. **Check the pitch** with a level. A sill must slope out so water drains off.
3. **Patch or pin** sound sills with a single crack, using stainless pins and a repair mortar matched to the stone.
4. **Dutchman repair**: cut out a damaged section and set in a new piece of matching stone.
5. **Replace** failed sills: remove the brick ends, take out the old sill, install flashing, and set a new sill in mortar at the correct pitch.
6. **Rebuild the brick ends** and repoint around the sill in matched mortar.
7. **Seal** the joint between the sill and the window frame, keep the drip edge clear, and walk you through the finished work.

## Materials we use
