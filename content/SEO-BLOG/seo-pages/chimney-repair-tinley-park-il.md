Your chimney is the hardest-working masonry on the house. Every other brick wall has a roof over it and a heated interior behind it. A chimney has neither. It is exposed on four sides, it sits above the roofline in the wind, and it takes rain, snow, ice and roughly a hundred freeze-thaw cycles a Chicagoland winter with nothing protecting it. That is why chimneys almost always fail first — often decades before the walls below them.

## The Chimney Repair Ladder: From Cheapest to Most Involved

Most Tinley Park chimney problems fall on a predictable ladder. Knowing which rung you are on is the whole game, because each step up is meaningfully more expensive than the last.

| Repair | What it addresses | When it is the right call |
|---|---|---|
| **Chimney cap** | Rain, snow, animals entering the flue | Open or unused flue; always worth doing |
| **Crown repair or replacement** | Cracked top slab shedding water into the stack | Hairline to moderate crown cracking |
| **Flashing repair** | Water entering where the chimney meets the roof | Ceiling stains near the chimney, rusted or lifted flashing |
| **Repointing / tuckpointing** | Failed mortar joints in the stack | Receded, sandy or open joints, sound brick |
| **Brick replacement** | Spalled, flaking or cracked individual brick | Localized face damage on an otherwise sound stack |
| **Rebuild above roofline** | Structurally compromised or leaning chimney | Deep joint failure, widespread spalling, lean |

An honest contractor tells you which rung you are on. A less honest one sells you the top of the ladder every time.

## Signs Your Tinley Park Chimney Needs Attention

- **White staining (efflorescence)** on the chimney brick — proof water is moving through the masonry.
- **Spalling or flaking brick faces**, especially on the north and west sides that take the weather.
- **Mortar joints you can rake out with a screwdriver**, or joints that have receded behind the brick.
- **A cracked or crumbling crown** at the top of the stack.
- **Rusted, lifted or tar-patched flashing** where the chimney passes through the roof.
- **Ceiling or attic water stains** near the chimney chase.
- **A visible lean**, or a gap opening between the chimney and the house.
- **Brick or mortar debris** landing on the roof or in the gutters.
- **Daylight visible** through the stack from the attic.

If you are seeing a lean or falling debris, that is not a spring project — it is a safety issue on an occupied home.

## Why Tinley Park Chimneys Fail

Tinley Park spans the Cook and Will County line without a lake buffer, so it takes the full swing of Chicagoland temperature cycling. Water enters a chimney at the top — through a cracked crown or an uncapped flue — soaks into the masonry, freezes, expands, and works the joints and brick faces apart from the inside. The cycle repeats every time the temperature crosses freezing, which in a normal winter here is dozens of times.

Two things make it worse on a chimney specifically. First, there is no interior heat drying the masonry from behind the way there is on a house wall. Second, an unused chimney — increasingly common as homeowners convert to gas inserts or stop using fireplaces entirely — never gets the drying effect of a warm flue, so it stays wet longer after every storm.

Much of Tinley Park's housing was built from the 1960s through the 1990s, which means a large share of the village's chimneys are now 30 to 60 years old: old enough for original crowns and flashing to have failed, and old enough for the mortar to be at the end of its life.
