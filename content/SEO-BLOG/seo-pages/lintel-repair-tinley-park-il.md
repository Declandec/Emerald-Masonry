## What Lintel Repair and Replacement Actually Involves

In brick-veneer construction, every window and door needs something strong spanning the opening to carry the weight of the masonry above. That something is usually a **steel lintel** — an L-shaped steel angle set into the wall, hidden behind the front edge of the brick.

The problem is simple building science. Steel rusts, and rust occupies far more volume than the original metal — up to several times as much. As a hidden lintel corrodes, it swells. That expansion, called **rust-jacking**, has nowhere to go but out and up, so it physically pushes on the brick course sitting on top of it. That's why the first visible symptom is often a cracked or lifted line of brick directly above a window, long before anyone sees the steel itself.

Lintel repair addresses both halves of the problem: the failing steel and the brick it has damaged. Depending on how far the corrosion has gone, that means either treating and protecting steel that still has its structural cross-section, or removing and replacing a lintel that has rusted through — and then rebuilding the brick the rust-jacking disturbed.

## Signs You Need Lintel Repair in Tinley Park

Most homeowners spot a lintel problem from the ground once they know what to look for. On Tinley Park homes, watch for:

- **Orange rust stains or streaks** running down the brick and mortar below a window or door — this is corrosion product washing out of the hidden steel.
- **A cracked, stepped, or lifted brick course** directly above an opening, often with the crack tracing the line of the lintel.
- **A visibly sagging or bowed lintel**, where the steel dips in the middle of a wider opening.
- **Gaps opening up at the ends** of the lintel where it meets the surrounding brick.
- **Bowed or pushed-out brick** immediately above the window, or mortar joints there that have opened while the rest of the wall looks fine.
- **Flaking, swollen, or scaling steel** if any of the lintel is exposed.

If you're seeing rust stains but no cracking yet, that's the ideal time to call — the steel is telling you it's corroding before it has done structural damage to the brick.

## The Risk of Waiting

A lintel doesn't get better on its own; rust-jacking is a one-way process. What starts as a cosmetic rust stain becomes a hairline crack, then an open crack, then loose brick. Once the course above the opening loses its support and its bond, individual bricks can shift and eventually fall — which is both a safety hazard and a much larger repair than the lintel alone.

There's a water problem riding alongside the structural one. The cracks that rust-jacking opens let water into the wall, and that water reaches the *back* of the steel and the surrounding brick, accelerating corrosion and setting up freeze-thaw damage every Chicago winter. Addressing the lintel early breaks that cycle. Waiting compounds it.

## Our Lintel Repair Process

Every wall is a little different, but a typical lintel repair on a Tinley Park home follows the same disciplined sequence:

1. **Shore and protect the opening.** We support the load above the window or door so the wall stays stable while the lintel is out.
2. **Carefully remove the affected brick.** We take down only the brick that has to come out, salvaging sound units for reuse.
3. **Address the steel.** We remove a lintel that has lost cross-section and set a new one, or — where the steel is still sound — clean it back to bare metal, treat it against corrosion, and protect it.
4. **Reset the lintel with proper bearing and clearance**, so it carries the load correctly and water can drain rather than pool against it.
5. **Rebuild the brick course** with salvaged and matched brick, keeping the coursing and reveal consistent with the rest of the wall.
6. **Repoint and finish**, matching mortar color and joint profile so the repair reads as part of the wall, not a patch.

Good lintel work is invisible when it's done right. The goal is a window head that looks like nothing ever happened to it.
