## Brick Veneer Is Not Solid Brick

On a bungalow in Oak Lawn or Evergreen Park, the brick is the wall. On most Homer Glen homes, the brick is a **single layer of veneer** tied to a wood-framed wall, with an air space behind it. Veneer is designed to get wet. Rain soaks through the face, runs down the back of the brick, hits **flashing** at the bottom and over openings, and drains out through **weep holes**.

That design works well when every part of it is intact. When one part fails, the brick is usually the first place the damage shows, even though the brick itself was never the problem. Good brick repair on a veneer home starts by asking where the water or movement is coming from.

## Signs Your Homer Glen Brick Needs Repair

- **Cracked or spalled bricks** low on the wall, near downspouts, or where lawn sprinklers hit the front.
- **White efflorescence** on one area of the front, often below a window or beside the garage.
- **Stair-step cracks** in the mortar at garage corners, window corners or the ends of long walls.
- **A horizontal crack along the bottom courses** where the brick sits on the foundation ledge.
- **Weep holes you cannot find**, or ones buried under mulch, stone or raised soil.
- **Staining or soft drywall inside** behind a brick wall, or at windows set in brick.
- **Open joints where brick meets siding, trim, or stone accents.**

## What Causes Brick Damage on Newer Homes

### Trapped water behind the veneer
Weep holes mortared over during construction, covered by landscaping over the years, or caulked shut by a well-meaning painter leave water with nowhere to go. It saturates the bottom courses, which then crack and flake through freeze-thaw cycles.

### Settlement and shrinkage
Newer homes often show **hairline step cracks** in their first decade as the house settles and the materials shrink. Most stop on their own. We check width, pattern and whether the bricks are offset, and treat stable cracks as a repointing repair rather than a structural one.

### The brick ledge
Veneer sits on a ledge at the top of the foundation. Water pooling there, or soil and mulch piled against it, can crack the bottom course and the edge of the ledge itself.

### Transitions and sealant joints
Where brick meets siding, windows, doors, stone accents and the garage, the joint should be a **flexible sealant**, not mortar. By 20 or 30 years those sealant joints have usually dried out and opened, and water gets behind the brick there.

## Our Brick Repair Process in Homer Glen

1. **Trace the cause.** Weep holes, flashing, grade, downspouts, sprinklers, sealant joints and the crack pattern.
2. **Fix the water source** or confirm movement has stopped before touching the brick.
3. **Remove damaged units** by cutting out the mortar around them, without disturbing sound brick.
4. **Install matched brick** for color, size and texture, blended so the repair disappears.
5. **Repoint** cracked or open joints with mortar matched to the existing color and strength.
6. **Reopen or add weep holes** and clear the base of the wall.
7. **Reseal transitions** with [caulking](/services/caulking) over backer rod where brick meets other materials.

## Materials We Use

Matched face brick from the original line or the closest current match; Type N mortar tinted to the existing joints; weep vents where weeps were lost; and polyurethane or silicone sealant at movement joints. We do not coat sound veneer with film-forming sealers. A wall built to drain should be allowed to drain.
