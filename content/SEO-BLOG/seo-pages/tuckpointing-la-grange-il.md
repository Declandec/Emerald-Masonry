## What tuckpointing is (and what went wrong last time)

**Tuckpointing** — more precisely, **repointing** — means cutting deteriorated mortar out of the joints between bricks to a consistent depth and packing in new mortar in layers, then tooling it to the right profile. Done correctly, the new mortar is the sacrificial part of the wall: it is a little softer and more breathable than the brick, so moisture and movement go through the joints instead of the brick faces.

The bad repairs we find on La Grange homes nearly always fail on one of four points:

| What went wrong | What you see | What it does to the wall |
|---|---|---|
| **Mortar too hard** (straight Portland-heavy mix) | Grey, glassy joints; chipped or spalled brick edges next to them | Forces freeze-thaw stress into the brick, which cracks first |
| **Joints not cut deep enough** | New mortar falling out in thin strips | Shallow "skim" pointing has nothing to bond to |
| **Mortar smeared over brick faces** | Haze and wide, fuzzy joint edges | Changes the look of the house and traps moisture at the brick surface |
| **Wrong colour or joint profile** | Patchwork walls; flat joints where the original was tooled | Permanent visual damage on a street-facing elevation |

## Signs your La Grange home needs tuckpointing

- **Mortar recessed** a quarter-inch or more behind the brick face.
- **Sandy, crumbling joints** that scrape out with a key.
- **Cracks running along joints** in a stair-step pattern.
- **Brick edges chipping** beside newer, harder-looking mortar — the signature of an incompatible repair.
- **Patchy colours** from several rounds of spot repairs.
- **Water stains** on interior plaster below windows or around the chimney breast.
- **Loose bricks** at chimney tops, window heads and garage corners.

## Risks of waiting

Open joints let water into the wall every time it rains. Chicagoland's freeze-thaw cycles then work that water into the brick and the mortar behind it. On older solid-masonry homes that means wet plaster, spalling brick faces and, eventually, loose units at the chimney and along the roof line. If the last repair was too hard, every winter it stays in the wall costs you more original brick.

## Our tuckpointing process in La Grange

1. **Assess the wall and the history.** We identify the original mortar, any later repairs, the brick type and the original joint profile, and note where earlier work has damaged the brick.
2. **Match the mortar.** Strength, colour, sand and texture are matched to the *original* mortar, not to the last patch. Sample panels are made so you can approve the colour cured.
3. **Remove mortar carefully.** Joints are cut out to a consistent depth — typically at least twice the joint width — with the hard earlier repairs removed without widening the joints or chipping brick arrises.
4. **Repoint in layers**, packing deep joints in lifts rather than all at once.
5. **Tool the joints** to match the original profile.
6. **Clean and cure.** Excess mortar is brushed off the brick faces, and the new joints are protected and kept from drying too fast.
7. **Replace damaged brick** where the earlier repair has already ruined units, using salvaged or closely matched brick.

## Materials we use

Lime-rich or Type N/Type O-class mortars matched to the strength of the existing brick; mortar colour built from sand and pigment to match the original; salvaged or matched replacement brick. We do not use a hard Type S mortar on soft older brick, and we do not seal over fresh mortar. For more on why, see our guide to [lime vs Portland mortar](/blog/lime-vs-portland-mortar).
