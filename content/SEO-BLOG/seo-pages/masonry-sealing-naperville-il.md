## What Masonry Sealing & Waterproofing Actually Is

Masonry sealing means applying a water-repellent product to brick, block, or stone so the wall resists rain, snowmelt, and windblown moisture. The key word for **brick waterproofing in Naperville, IL** is *breathable*. Good masonry sealers are penetrating water repellents — they soak into the surface and dramatically reduce how much water the masonry absorbs, while still letting water vapor escape from inside the wall.

That breathability matters because brick and mortar are not meant to be airtight. Moisture naturally moves through masonry as vapor. A breathable sealer blocks liquid water from getting *in* but lets any vapor already in the wall get *out*. That's the opposite of a film-forming paint or coating, which can trap water behind it and cause serious damage.

> Sealing keeps liquid water out while letting vapor escape — that balance is what protects a wall instead of quietly damaging it.

## Sealing Only Works After the Mortar Is Sound

Here's the most important thing to understand, and where a lot of DIY and low-bid sealing jobs go wrong: **sealing is not a substitute for repair.** If your mortar joints are cracked, crumbling, or open, water is pouring in through those gaps — no sealer sprayed over the face of the brick will stop that.

That's why Emerald Masonry always inspects the masonry first. If we find failing joints, the [tuckpointing](/services/tuckpointing) comes first. If individual bricks are cracked, spalled, or loose, we handle that [brick repair](/services/brick-repair) before anything else. Only once the wall is structurally sound and watertight at the joints do we clean and apply the sealer. Seal over bad mortar and you simply lock the problem inside the wall — this is the number one reason we lead every conversation about **masonry sealing in Naperville, IL** with an honest inspection.

## Signs You Need Masonry Sealing

Naperville homeowners usually call us when they notice one or more of these:

- **Persistent efflorescence** — that white, powdery or crystalline residue on the brick. It's mineral salt left behind as water moves through and evaporates off the masonry. Recurring efflorescence means water is repeatedly getting into the wall.
- **Damp or musty interior walls** — cold, damp spots on the inside of an exterior wall, or a musty smell, often trace back to masonry absorbing water.
- **Saturated, dark brick after rain** that stays wet far longer than it should, especially on north- and west-facing walls.
- **A history of water damage** — stained drywall, peeling interior paint, or prior basement seepage near a masonry wall.

If any of these sound familiar, a sealing and waterproofing assessment is worth a call.

## The Risk of Waiting

In DuPage and Will County, the real threat is the **freeze-thaw cycle.** When saturated brick freezes, the trapped water expands. Do that over and over through a Chicagoland winter and the face of the brick begins to flake, crack, and pop off — a failure called **spalling.** Spalled brick can't simply be resealed; it has to be replaced, which costs far more than sealing would have.

Water that keeps entering the wall also feeds interior damage: ruined drywall, mold, rotted framing, and efflorescence that never quite goes away. Addressing it early with proper repair and **breathable waterproofing** is almost always the cheaper path.

> Every winter you wait, water in the brick freezes, expands, and does damage that a proper seal would have prevented.

Emerald Masonry follows the same disciplined process on every Naperville job:

## The Materials We Use — and What We Avoid

For **water-repellent brick in Naperville, IL**, we use breathable **siloxane- and silane-type** penetrating repellents. These chemistries bond inside the pores of the masonry, repel liquid water, and still allow vapor to pass through. That's exactly what you want on a brick wall in a freeze-thaw climate.

What we deliberately avoid are film-forming coatings and glossy "waterproof paints" that lay a plastic-like skin over the brick. They look like they're doing something, but they trap moisture behind the film. When that trapped water freezes, it drives spalling from the inside out. Choosing the right product is a big part of why professional **masonry waterproofing in DuPage County** outlasts a hardware-store spray job.
