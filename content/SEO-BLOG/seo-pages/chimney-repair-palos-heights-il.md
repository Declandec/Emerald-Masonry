## What Chimney Repair Covers

"Chimney repair" isn't one job — it's a ladder of work, from a quick weatherproofing fix to a structural rebuild:

- **Chimney cap** — the metal cover that keeps rain, snow, and animals out of the flue. Rusted or missing caps are the cheapest, fastest fix.
- **Chimney crown** — the sloped masonry or concrete slab at the very top that sheds water off the stack. Cracked crowns let water straight into the brick.
- **Flashing** — the metal seal where the chimney meets the roof. Failed flashing is the single most common source of chimney leaks.
- **Tuckpointing the stack** — grinding out and replacing failed mortar joints on the exposed brick.
- **Rebuild above the roofline** — when the mortar and brick at the top are too far gone to point, we take the stack down to sound masonry and rebuild it.

Where your chimney sits on that ladder is exactly what we determine at the free inspection.

## Why Chimneys Fail First — and Why Waiting Costs More

Your chimney is the most weather-abused masonry on the whole house. The walls of your home are only exposed to the elements on one face; a chimney is exposed on all four sides **and** the top. It gets soaked from every direction, and then Chicagoland's freeze-thaw cycles go to work — water gets into the crown, cap gaps, and mortar joints, freezes, expands, and pries the masonry apart. That's why the crown and the top courses of brick fail years before the walls below them.

Waiting turns a small job into a big one. A cracked crown that could have been sealed lets water into the stack; a season or two later that same water has rotted the mortar joints, spalled the brick, and turned a crown repair into a rebuild above the roofline. Worse, chimney leaks don't stay in the chimney — they run down into the attic, the ceiling, and the framing. Catching it early is always cheaper.

We never quote a chimney sight-unseen, because the price genuinely depends on the chimney. The factors that move it:

- **Extent of the damage** — how many courses of brick are spalled, and how much mortar has failed.
- **Crown, cap, and flashing condition** — whether each needs a repair or a full replacement.
