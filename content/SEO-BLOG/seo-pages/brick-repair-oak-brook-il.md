## What Brick Repair and Replacement Involves

Brick repair spans a range of work depending on what's failed:

- **Brick replacement** — removing spalled, cracked, or crumbling brick and setting matched replacement brick into the wall. Spalled brick (where the hard outer face has popped off) always needs replacement, because the soft exposed core soaks up water.
- **Crack repair** — addressing cracked brick and the cracks running through mortar and masonry, after determining whether the crack is cosmetic or structural.
- **Repointing the surrounding joints** — failed mortar is almost always part of the problem and gets renewed alongside the brick.
- **Root-cause correction** — fixing the source: a rusting [lintel](/services/lintel-repair), a failed [sealant joint](/services/caulking), missing weep holes, or water intrusion that's feeding the freeze-thaw damage.

## Signs Your Brick Needs Repair

- **Spalling** — brick faces flaking, pitting, popping, or crumbling
- **Cracks** running through bricks or stair-stepping through the mortar joints
- **Loose, shifted, or bulging brick** you can move or that's pushing out of plane
- **Gaps** where mortar or whole bricks have fallen out
- **Efflorescence** — white mineral staining that signals water moving through the wall
- **Rust stains or cracking above windows and doors** — a sign of a corroding steel lintel below the brick

## The Risk of Waiting

Damaged brick is an open door for water, and once water is in the wall, our Chicago-area freeze-thaw cycle accelerates everything. A few spalled bricks become a spreading patch; a hairline crack widens; a rusting lintel keeps expanding and lifts the brick above it. What could have been a small, matched repair turns into a large section rebuild — and on an Oak Brook facade, the larger the repair, the harder it is to make it disappear. Addressing brick damage early keeps the repair small, the matching tight, and the cost down.

## Materials and Matching

Matching is the craft in this work. We source replacement brick to match the original's dimensions, color range, and surface texture, since brick styles and colors change over the decades and a wrong choice stands out permanently. Mortar is matched in color, sand, and tooling profile, and in **hardness** to the existing masonry — a too-hard mortar against softer brick traps water and causes more spalling. For structural elements behind the brick, like lintels and shelf angles, we use corrosion-resistant materials so the repair outlasts the original detail.

### What causes brick to spall and crack on Oak Brook buildings?
The main driver is water plus our freeze-thaw climate. Moisture enters through failed mortar joints, cracked sealant, or porous brick, then freezes and expands, popping the brick face. Rusting steel lintels and shelf angles, building movement, and foundation settlement also crack brick. We diagnose the cause so the repair actually holds.

### Do I need to replace the brick or can it be repaired?
Cracked or loose brick can sometimes be re-secured and repointed, but brick that has spalled — lost its hard outer face — must be replaced, because the exposed core absorbs water fast and deteriorates further. We assess each affected area and replace only what needs it, then address the underlying cause.
