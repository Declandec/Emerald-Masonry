## What a Real Façade Assessment Produces

1. **A condition record mapped by elevation** — not general impressions, but located findings
2. **Photographs tied to locations**, so a finding can be revisited in two years
3. **An explanation of mechanism** — what is failing, and what is driving it
4. **Quantities with stated assumptions** — the basis every bid gets compared on

## Signs a Façade Needs Assessing Now

Some of these are budget items. The first two are not.

- **Masonry debris on the sidewalk, roof or entry canopy** — brick fragments or mortar below an elevation mean something above is releasing
- **Displaced coping, or a parapet out of plumb** when sighted along its length
- **Horizontal cracking above windows, doors or storefronts**, with the masonry above lifted — corroding steel
- **Tenant reports of water** at the building perimeter, especially at upper floors or after driving rain
- **Efflorescence** — white salt bloom — tracking down an elevation
- **Sealant that has split, hardened or pulled away** from one side of a joint
- **Staining tracks** running from a specific point above, which map the water path for you
- **Spalling brick faces**, meaning water has been getting past the joints for years

The first two should be treated as immediate: the area below gets cleared and the element assessed, because anything shedding masonry over a public walkway or an entrance is a liability question before it is a maintenance one.

## Inspect From the Wall, Not the Sidewalk

This is where most façade assessments go wrong. The elements that fail first — **parapets, coping, cornices, upper-elevation lintels and shelf angles** — are the ones a ground-level walk sees least. Binoculars do not find drummy masonry and a camera does not measure joint depth.

Proper assessment means getting to the wall by lift or staging, **sounding** the masonry to find debonded areas that look intact, and **probing joint depth** rather than judging mortar by its face. On a multi-storey Downers Grove office or retail building, the difference between a sidewalk assessment and a wall assessment is routinely the difference between a repointing scope and a parapet rebuild.

## What Fails on Downers Grove Commercial Buildings

**The downtown core** near Main Street and the BNSF station carries older brick commercial buildings — flat roofs, decorative parapets over the sidewalk, storefront lintels behind sign bands, and shared party walls.

**The corridors and office parks** along Butterfield Road, Ogden Avenue and Finley bring later construction: brick and stone veneer over steel or CMU backup, precast and panel joints, long control joints, storefront and curtain-wall perimeters, and shelf angles at floor lines.

Across both, water infiltration almost never comes through sound brick. It comes through:

- **Open or failed coping joints** at the parapet, admitting water into the top of the wall
- **Deteriorated through-wall flashing**, so water that enters cannot drain back out
- **Failed sealant joints** at windows, panels and dissimilar materials
- **Cracked or eroded sills** that have stopped throwing water clear
- **Corroding lintels and shelf angles**, expanding and lifting the masonry above them
- **Blocked weep holes**, converting a drainage wall into a wall that holds water

1. **Assess and document** — every elevation, the parapet, and the roof edge.
