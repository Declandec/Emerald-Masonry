## What Commercial Masonry Restoration Covers

Restoration is the umbrella term for the range of work that keeps a commercial masonry building sound:

- **Tuckpointing and repointing** — renewing failed mortar joints across the facade, the most common ongoing need.
- **Brick repair and replacement** — addressing spalled, cracked, and failed brick before water spreads the damage.
- **CMU block repair** — restoring concrete masonry unit walls common on industrial and newer commercial buildings.
- **[Lintel repair](/services/lintel-repair)** — replacing the corroded steel that carries brick over windows and doors.
- **[Parapet wall repair](/services/parapet-wall-repair)** — rebuilding the roofline masonry that's the most common source of leaks from the top down.
- **Sealant and caulking joints** — restoring the movement joints that keep water out.
- **Full facade restoration** — combining the above into a coordinated scope for an aging building.

## Signs a Commercial Building Needs Restoration

- **Failing or open mortar joints** across one or more elevations
- **Spalling or cracked brick**, especially below windows and at the parapet
- **Cracks above windows and doors** or rust staining — a sign of corroding lintels
- **A leaning, cracked, or displaced parapet** at the roofline
- **Water intrusion** showing up on interior walls or ceilings
- **Efflorescence** and staining across the facade

## The Risk of Waiting

On a commercial building, deferred masonry maintenance compounds. Open joints and failing parapets let water into the wall and the roof, where freeze-thaw cycles widen every crack each winter. A corroding lintel keeps expanding until it lifts and cracks the brick above it. What could have been routine tuckpointing turns into structural facade work, interior damage, and — with parapets and spalling brick over entrances and sidewalks — a genuine liability concern. Restoration on a planned schedule is always less expensive and less disruptive than emergency repair.
