Lintel replacement in Cicero, IL means removing the corroded steel spanning a window, door or storefront opening, installing new primed steel, **installing flashing above it**, and rebuilding the brick it lifted. Here is the part almost nobody mentions, and it is the part that decides whether you do this again in fifteen years.

**Your old lintel rusted for a reason.** Water was reaching it — through failed joints, a bad sill, a gap at the window perimeter, or simply the absence of anything directing water back out of the wall. **Putting new steel into that same wall without changing anything about the water means the new steel starts corroding on day one.**

Proper **through-wall flashing above the lintel** catches water inside the wall and sends it back out through weeps. It is the difference between a repair measured in decades and one measured in a decade and a half.

## Why Steel Fails Inside a Wall

Brick cannot span an opening on its own. In Cicero's pre-1930 masonry, that job usually falls to a **steel angle lintel** bearing on the brick at each side.

Steel is entirely reliable until it gets wet. Then it corrodes — and **rust occupies several times the volume of the metal it replaced.** Inside a rigid brick wall there is nowhere for that expansion to go, so it pushes upward and outward, lifting the courses above the opening.

That is **rust jacking**, and it explains why a steel problem shows up as a horizontal crack in a mortar joint rather than as anything obviously metallic.

**The staining you see is rust escaping. The problem is the rust that stayed inside.**

## The Progression

1. **Water reaches the steel** — through failed joints, a failed sill, cracked perimeter caulking, or no flashing at all.
2. **Corrosion begins**, hidden.
3. **Rust staining appears** on the brick below — the first visible sign, and the cheapest moment to act.
4. **A horizontal crack opens** in the joint above the opening.
5. **Brick displaces** — courses lift, mortar fails, units loosen.
6. **Section loss** means the lintel no longer reliably carries its load.

## Signs Your Cicero Building Needs Lintel Work

- **Orange or rust-colored staining** running down from the top corners of an opening.

- **Cracked or displaced brick** at the corners of an opening.
- **Windows sticking** as the opening distorts.
- **The same symptom on several openings** — common here, because they are all the same age.
- **Loose brick** above an opening — treat this as urgent.

## Our Replacement Process

1. **Free on-site assessment** of every opening, identifying which lintels are corroding and where water is entering.
2. **Fix the water source** — joints, sill, perimeter caulking, gutters — because the new steel inherits whatever the old steel had.
3. **Temporary support.** The masonry above the opening is shored before anything is removed. This is not optional and it is not visible in the finished job, but it is why the wall above stays where it belongs.
4. **Careful removal** of the brick courses over the lintel, salvaging sound units for reuse.
5. **Remove or treat the steel.** Corroded steel is replaced; steel with surface rust and full section is cleaned, treated and primed.
6. **Install new lintel** with proper bearing at each end and a protective coating.
7. **Install through-wall flashing above the lintel** with end dams, so water inside the wall is caught and directed back out rather than sitting on the new steel.
8. **Rebuild the brickwork** with salvaged and matched units, repointed with mortar matched for hardness, color and sand.
9. **Weeps and sealant** — weeps above the flashing kept open, new backer rod and sealant at the window perimeter.

**Steps 3 and 7 are the two that separate a real lintel replacement from a cosmetic one.**

## The Cost of Waiting

Lintel corrosion does not pause. Every winter the steel expands a little more, the crack opens a little wider, and more water reaches the steel through the crack the steel just made. What starts as staining on one window becomes cracked brick on four — and on a dense Cicero block, loose masonry above a doorway sits directly over a public sidewalk or a shared gangway.

There is also a compounding effect worth naming: **the same water rusting your lintels is working on your mortar joints and brick faces at the same time.** Lintel staining is frequently the most visible symptom of a wall-wide water problem.
