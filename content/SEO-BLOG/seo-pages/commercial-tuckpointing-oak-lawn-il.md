## Where the Joints Fail on Oak Lawn Buildings

Oak Lawn's commercial stock is largely post-war and now sixty-plus winters into Cook County freeze-thaw: **apartment and condo buildings**, **retail and mixed-use along the 95th Street corridor**, **medical office** around the hospital campus, plus **schools, churches and municipal buildings**.

Mortar fails first where water concentrates:

- **Parapets and coping joints** — exposed on both faces and the top, unheated, and standing directly over the sidewalk
- **South and west elevations** — driving rain and the widest temperature swings
- **Below sills and copings**, where water runs back onto the wall
- **At and above lintels**, where corroding steel opens the joint from behind
- **Base courses** — splash-back, snow banked all winter, and de-icing salt
- **Around penetrations** — vents, conduit, sign anchors, and old fastener holes

The mechanism is the same on every one: water enters an open joint, freezes, expands roughly nine percent, and forces the masonry apart — dozens of times each winter.

## The Signs a Board or Manager Should Act On

- Mortar you can **scratch out with a key**, or joints that have visibly receded
- **Mortar debris or brick fragments** on the walk or roof below
- **Efflorescence** — white salts — on the wall face
- **Damp interior walls** or tenant complaints of water at the perimeter
- **Spalling brick faces**, meaning water has been getting past the joints for years
- **Displaced coping** or a parapet that is out of plumb when sighted along its length
- Anything loose **above a walkway or entrance** — that one is not a budget item, it is a safety item

## Our Commercial Tuckpointing Process

1. **Assess every elevation, the parapet and the roof edge** — from staging or a lift where needed, because ground-level inspection systematically misses the worst of it.
2. **Sound and probe.** Drummy brick and true joint depth do not show up in photographs.
3. **Write the scope** — quantities, cut-out depth, mortar specification, access method, phasing, and the assumptions each quantity rests on.
4. **Plan access and protection** — staging or lift, overhead protection over walkways and entrances, parking and drive-aisle management, tenant notice.
5. **Cut out to sound material**, typically around two to two and a half times the joint width, by grinding or hand-raking without damaging the brick edges.
6. **Repoint in matched mortar** — colour, sand, texture and, critically, hardness matched to the units.
7. **Tool the joints** to the existing profile at the right moment in the set, which is what compacts and closes the joint surface.
8. **Clean down and walk it with you**, elevation by elevation.

## Materials

Type N for most post-war Oak Lawn brick; more lime-rich, lower-Portland mixes on older or softer masonry; Type S only where the units genuinely warrant it. Matched sand and pigment for colour. Non-staining sealants where limestone or cast stone is involved. Breathable penetrating repellents rather than film-forming coatings that trap moisture in a wall.
