## Control joints: why the same crack keeps coming back

This is the single most misdiagnosed condition on a Bolingbrook industrial wall, and it accounts for an enormous share of repeat repointing on the same lines.

A long concrete masonry wall **has to move.** Block shrinks as it cures and then responds to temperature across a run that may be two or three hundred feet long. **Control joints** are the designed relief — deliberate vertical breaks, filled with sealant rather than mortar, spaced so the wall relieves its stress at a planned location instead of choosing one.

Three things go wrong on this stock:

1. **Spacing that suited the drawing rather than the wall.** A south or west elevation in full sun moves more than the drawing assumed.
2. **Joints mortared solid by a prior repair crew** who read a designed gap as a defect. The wall then has no relief at all.
3. **Sealant long past service life**, so the joint no longer keeps water out even though it still moves.

The result is cracking that returns at the same vertical lines a few years after every repair. Repointing that crack without restoring the joint is buying the same repair again. ## The bottom eight feet

On a building with dock banks, the lowest section of wall lives a completely different life from everything above it:

- **Trailer and bumper impact** at dock positions, and dock-leveller vibration transmitted into the surround
- **Forklift and pallet-jack strikes** at corners, jambs and interior face
- **Road salt and de-icer** thrown up off the apron every winter
- **Snow piled against the wall** by yard plowing, held there for weeks
- **Standing water** where the apron has settled back toward the building

That combination produces spalled face shells, cracked jambs, failed sealant at the door surround and — where nobody installed or maintained them — masonry taking loads that **bollards and dock bumpers** were supposed to absorb. ## Reading the crack before pricing it

| Pattern | Likely mechanism | Right response |
|---|---|---|
| Vertical cracks at regular intervals | Control joint spacing or a joint mortared shut | Restore the joint; do not just repoint the crack |
| Cracking radiating from an opening corner | Movement concentrating at the weak point | Joint provision plus lintel check |
| Horizontal crack at a consistent height | Corroding steel — lintel or shelf angle | Remove and rebuild over the steel |
| Stepped or diagonal, widening | Possible settlement or structural movement | Structural opinion before masonry work |
| Local crushing or displaced units at low level | Impact | Rebuild plus protection |
