Here's the thing most homeowners don't realize: the same pressure that's fine for cleaning a concrete driveway will ruin a brick wall. Masonry cleaning is a masonry job, and it should be done by people who understand how brick and mortar behave — not just whoever owns a pressure washer.

## Why "Power Washing" Brick Is Different

Brick and mortar are porous, and the joints between them are softer than the brick. Hit that surface with high-pressure water and several bad things happen at once: the pressure erodes and blasts mortar out of the joints, it chips and etches the face of the brick, and it forces water deep into the wall — where, in Mokena's freeze-thaw climate, it freezes and accelerates spalling. We've been called to repair walls that were "cleaned" with too much pressure, and the cleaning did more damage than years of weather.

The correct approach for masonry is **soft-washing**: low, controlled pressure combined with the right cleaning solutions that do the actual work of breaking down dirt and killing organic growth. The chemistry lifts the staining; the water just rinses. That's how you get a clean wall without sacrificing the mortar.

## What Soft-Wash Masonry Cleaning Removes

- **Atmospheric dirt and grime** that dulls brick and stone over the years
- **Algae, mildew, and mold** — the green and black growth common on shaded, north-facing, and tree-lined Mokena walls
- **Efflorescence** — the white, chalky mineral bloom that surfaces as water moves through masonry
- **Organic staining** from leaves, soil splash, and run-off
- **General surface soiling** ahead of sealing, tuckpointing, or painting

## When Masonry Cleaning Makes Sense

- **Before [tuckpointing](/services/tuckpointing) or repair** — clean joints let everyone see the true condition of the mortar
- **Before [sealing or waterproofing](/services/masonry-sealing)** — coatings only bond properly to clean, dry masonry
- **To stop organic growth** — algae and moss hold moisture against the wall, which feeds deterioration
- **For curb appeal** — restoring the original color of a tired facade before a sale or simply to refresh it
- **For commercial properties** — keeping a storefront or building entrance looking maintained and cared for

## The Risk of Doing It Wrong

The danger with masonry cleaning isn't dirt — it's the cleaning itself. Excessive pressure permanently etches brick, strips mortar joints (turning a cosmetic job into a [repointing](/services/tuckpointing) job), and saturates the wall. Sandblasting, which some operators still use on brick, is even worse: it removes the hard, weather-resistant outer skin of the brick and leaves the soft, absorbent core exposed, dramatically accelerating decay. Once that face is gone it doesn't come back. The whole point of hiring a masonry contractor for cleaning is to get a wall that's both clean **and** undamaged.

We use controlled low-pressure soft-washing with the right cleaning solutions, which lifts dirt, algae, and staining without harming the masonry.
