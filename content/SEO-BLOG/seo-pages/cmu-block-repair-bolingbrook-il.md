## How CMU Differs From Brick

| | Brick | Concrete block (CMU) |
|---|---|---|
| **Unit** | Small, fired clay, dense skin | Large, cast concrete, absorbent throughout |
| **Water** | Fired face resists; joints are the path | **The unit itself absorbs** |
| **Movement** | Expands slightly over time | **Shrinks as it cures**, then moves thermally |
| **Needs** | Expansion joints | **Control joints** — different purpose |
| **Typical cracking** | Step cracks through joints | **Long straight vertical cracks** |
| **Common damage** | Spalled faces, failed joints | Shrinkage cracks, impact damage, face spalling |
| **Reinforcement** | Usually none in the wall | Often grouted cells and horizontal joint reinforcement |

Two of those rows explain most CMU problems.

**Block shrinks.** Concrete masonry units continue to shrink as they cure and then move with temperature. That movement has to go somewhere. **Control joints** are the designed answer — deliberate vertical breaks that let the wall move without cracking. Where they are missing, spaced too far apart, or were sealed once and never resealed, the wall relieves the stress the only way it can: **a long straight crack**.

**Block absorbs through its face.** A brick's fired outer skin resists water; a concrete block does not have one. On an unprotected or poorly coated block wall, the units themselves take on water — which is why freeze-thaw damage on CMU often shows as **face spalling across a broad area** rather than concentrated at joints.

## What Fails on Bolingbrook's Industrial Walls

### Shrinkage and control-joint cracking
The most common call. Straight vertical cracks, frequently at openings or at regular intervals along a long elevation. Usually addressable — but repairing the crack without restoring or adding a working control joint means it reopens.

### Impact damage at docks and drive aisles
**Loading dock corners, bollard surrounds, truck court walls and drive-aisle elevations** take hits brick walls never see. Broken and displaced units are common, and a broken face at grade lets water straight into a wall that is already absorbing splash and road salt.

### Face spalling
Freeze-thaw damage across the block face, worst at grade and on north elevations.

### Joint deterioration
Mortar joints failing the same way they do anywhere, plus **failed sealant in the control joints** — which is a caulking problem, not a mortar one, and gets missed constantly.

### Efflorescence and staining
White bloom telling you water is moving through the wall.

### Painted block problems
Enormously common here. Paint on block **traps moisture in an already absorbent material**, so coated walls that have been recoated repeatedly often hide spalling underneath.

## Signs Your Block Wall Needs Work

- **Long straight vertical cracks**, often at regular intervals or at openings.
- **Stair-step cracking** through the joints — a different signal, indicating movement.
- **Horizontal cracking, bowing or displacement** — evaluate before anything else.
- **Broken, cracked or displaced units** at dock corners and drive aisles.
- **Face spalling** across the block, worst near grade.
- **Open, sandy or missing mortar joints.**
- **Failed or missing sealant** in control joints.
- **Efflorescence** on interior or exterior faces.
- **Water staining inside** along the wall base or at openings.
- **Blistering or peeling paint**, often with spalled block underneath.

## Our Repair Process

1. **Free on-site assessment.** We read the crack pattern to distinguish shrinkage from movement, check control-joint condition and spacing, and identify where water is entering.
2. **Determine cause before scope.** A shrinkage crack, a movement crack and an impact break are three different repairs.
3. **Written scope with photographs**, sized for facility management or ownership.
4. **Phasing around operations** — dock schedules, shift changes, truck access.
5. **Unit replacement** — damaged block cut out and replaced with matched units, tied appropriately.
6. **Repointing** with mortar matched to the wall — **Type S** or **Type M** where structurally appropriate on block, particularly below grade.
7. **Control joints restored or installed** — cleaned, backer rod set, and sealed with a flexible sealant rather than mortar.
8. **Rebuilding** sections that have lost integrity.
9. **Breathable protection** where a coating is warranted — never a film-forming product that traps moisture in absorbent block.

## What Waiting Costs

On block specifically, deferral has a distinctive path. A crack or a broken unit lets water into a wall that already absorbs readily. Saturated block spalls its face and loses section. Where the wall carries **grouted cells and joint reinforcement**, that water reaches steel — and corroding steel inside a block wall is a structural conversation rather than a maintenance one.
