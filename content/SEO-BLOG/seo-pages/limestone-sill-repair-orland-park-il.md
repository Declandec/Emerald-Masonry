## What a sill does, and why it fails

The sill under a window (and the threshold under a door) is a sloped piece of stone or concrete set into the brick. It catches the water that runs down the window and drops it clear of the wall, usually with a small groove underneath called a **drip**. When it works, the brick below stays dry. When it doesn't, every rain soaks the same patch of wall.

Sills fail in a few predictable ways:

- **Cracking** — from freeze-thaw, from rusting anchors or from the house settling
- **Back-pitch** — the sill settles or was set flat, so water runs toward the window and into the wall
- **Open end and head joints** — the joints where sill pieces meet, or where the sill meets brick, open up
- **Surface breakdown** — older limestone and some cast stone erode, scale or delaminate
- **No flashing** — nothing under the sill to stop water that does get past it

## Signs your Orland Park sills need attention

- Cracks across or along the sill
- Sills that slope toward the window instead of away
- Gaps at the ends where the sill meets brick
- Efflorescence, stains or crumbling mortar in the brick under the window
- Water stains or soft drywall below the window inside
- Paint or caulk peeling around the window frame and sill

## Risks of waiting

Water that gets past a sill goes straight into the wall at its most vulnerable spot — the window opening. It wets the framing and the window buck, rusts the lintel of the window below on a two-storey house, and freezes in the brick under the sill until the faces spall off. A sill that might have needed sealing becomes a sill replacement plus brick and window-frame repair. ## How we repair sills in Orland Park

1. **Inspect every sill** — material, slope, joints, cracks and the brick below.
2. **Decide patch, reset or replace** for each one and explain why.
3. **Patch or seal** sound sills: cut out failed joints, patch with matched repair mortar, seal ends with flexible sealant.
4. **Reset** settled sills: remove, correct the bed so the sill slopes out, re-set and seal.
5. **Replace** broken sills: remove the old sill, install flashing where possible, set a new sill matched in size and color with the right slope and a drip.
6. **Repair the brick below** — repoint, replace spalled units — and leave everything sealed.
