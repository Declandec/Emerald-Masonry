## Newer brick veneer is a drainage wall

Most Mokena homes built from the 1990s onward are **brick veneer**: a single wythe of brick standing an inch or two off the sheathing, tied back to the structure with metal wall ties, with flashing at the base and over openings and weep holes to let water out.

That wall carries no building load. It is a rain screen, designed on the assumption that water **will** get behind the brick and needs a clear route back out. When that route is blocked or was never built correctly, water sits in the cavity — and then the brick starts failing.

Here is what actually goes wrong on newer homes:

- **Blocked weep holes.** Mortar droppings in the cavity, caulk applied by a well-meaning contractor, or mulch and soil piled against the base course. The drain is closed and the wall holds water.
- **Short, missing, or improperly lapped flashing.** At the base, over windows, over garage openings. Water that gets in has nowhere to go but down and in.
- **Corroding shelf angles.** The steel supporting veneer at floor lines and openings rusts when it stays wet, and rusting steel expands and lifts the brick above it.
- **Wall ties that failed or were never right.** Corroded, spaced too far apart, or never properly embedded. The veneer begins to separate from the wall behind it.
- **Missing or failed control joints.** Brick expands. Without joints to absorb it, the wall cracks where it can — usually diagonally, through the mortar, at an opening corner.
- **Water management outside the wall.** A downspout discharging at a corner, a roof edge with no kickout flashing dumping onto a wall, or grade sloping back toward the house.

Any of those will destroy good brick in twenty years. All of them are fixable — but only if the repair addresses the cause rather than the symptom.

## Repair or replace? The line

- **Scattered individual failed units** in a sound wall → brick repair, cut in one at a time
- **A continuous failed area** — spalled, cracked, or displaced → [brick replacement](/services/brick-replacement), rebuilt as a section
- **A wall bulging, separating, or out of plane** → rebuild the section **and** correct the ties, shelf angle, or movement causing it
- **Cracked, lifted brick in a line above an opening** → [lintel repair](/services/lintel-repair) first, then rebuild the masonry above

We will tell you plainly which one your wall is. Selling a rebuild where a repair holds is dishonest; patching a wall that has gone structural is worse.

## Our repair process

1. **Diagnose the cause.** Weeps, flashing, shelf angle, ties, control joints, drainage. Everything else depends on this step.
2. **Find the true edge of the failure.** Sounding and probing beyond the visible damage, so the repair reaches sound masonry.
3. **Support where needed and remove.** Failed brick and mortar out cleanly, without disturbing the brick that stays.
4. **Correct the detail.** New flashing, cleared cavity and weeps, shelf angle treated or replaced, ties addressed, drainage fixed.
5. **Reset with matched brick.** Sourced to match and dry-laid against your wall for approval before anything is set.
6. **Match the mortar.** Colour, sand, texture, joint profile — and hardness matched to the brick.
7. **Clean down and blend** so the repair reads as part of the original wall.
