## Why residential masonry restoration in Frankfort starts with a plan

A homeowner usually calls about one thing: a crack, a leaking chimney, a stoop that has pulled away. Often that one thing is connected to others. A rusting lintel might explain the crack. A failed crown might explain the wet ceiling. Fixing them one at a time, with different contractors, can mean paying to open the same wall twice.

A whole-house plan avoids that. We sort everything we find into three groups:

| Priority | What belongs here | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Do now** | Safety hazards and active water entry | Loose chimney brick, unsafe steps, cracked crowns, leaks |
| **2. Do soon** | Problems that get more expensive each winter | Worn mortar joints, rusting lintels, cracked sills, failed caulk |
| **3. Plan for** | Appearance and long-term upkeep | Mismatched old patches, staining, cleaning, minor joint wear |

## Signs your home is ready for a restoration plan

- **More than one masonry problem** on different sides of the house.
- **Repairs that keep coming back**, such as a crack that reopens or a stain that returns.
- **Worn mortar** at the chimney, the sunny side and the bottom courses.
- **Rust or cracks** above windows and garage doors.
- **Steps, stoops or porch walls** moving away from the house.
- **Stone veneer** with open joints, loose units or staining beneath.
- **You are selling, buying or about to replace windows or the roof**, and want the order of work right.

## Risks of waiting or patching piece by piece

Water keeps moving while repairs wait, and freeze-thaw makes small openings larger every winter. Piecemeal repairs also tend to leave a wall with several mortar colors and repairs that block one another. A roof or window project done before the masonry can get disturbed when the masonry work finally happens.

## How we restore homes in Frankfort

1. **Walk the entire house**, including the roof-level chimney and foundation, and photograph what we find.
2. **Find the causes**, such as drainage, flashing, rusting steel or past repairs.
3. **Write a prioritized plan** in the three groups above, with a line for each repair.
4. **Agree on phases** that fit your budget and the season.
5. **Match the materials**: brick, stone and mortar color, strength and joint profile.
6. **Do the work** in an order that makes sense, top down, keeping flashing and weep holes open.
7. **Clean gently** and walk through each finished phase with you.
8. **Leave you the plan**, updated, so the next phase is ready when you are.

## Materials we use

Mortar matched to the existing joints, softer lime-rich mixes on older soft brick and Type N or Type S on newer walls; matched face brick and stone; primed or galvanized steel lintels with flashing; limestone or cast stone sills; compatible crown mixes and chimney caps; and suitable sealant at frame joints. We use breathable water repellents only where they make sense, never film-forming coatings.
