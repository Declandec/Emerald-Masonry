## Why brick replacement in New Lenox is mostly a matching job

When we searched for brick replacement in New Lenox, the results were brick supply yards. That tells you what homeowners are really trying to do: find brick that matches. The yards can help, but on a 20- to 30-year-old house, the exact blend usually is not available any more. Manufacturers change clay sources, kilns and colour ranges, and blends built for one subdivision are often retired within a few years.

Here are the matching routes we use, and when each one makes sense:

| Matching route | Best for | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| **Closest current blend** from a local brick yard | Larger areas, whole sections | Colour range, size and texture can differ slightly |
| **Leftover brick** from the builder (garage, attic, shed) | Small to medium repairs | Often only a few dozen units |
| **Salvaged brick** of the same blend | Medium repairs where current brick is off | Takes time to source |
| **Borrowed brick** from a hidden wall | Small, highly visible repairs | The hidden spot is then patched with the closest current brick |
| **Blending** several units together | Multi-colour blends | Must follow the original mix ratio |

## Signs you need brick replacement

- **Spalled bricks** with faces flaking or popping off, often low on the wall or near downspouts.
- **Cracked bricks** at garage door piers, corners and around windows.
- **Impact damage** from cars, snow blowers, lawn equipment or a fallen limb.
- **Bricks damaged by other work**: window or door replacement, new vents or a removed fixture.
- **Rust-stained or broken bricks** above a window or garage door, a sign of a corroding steel lintel.

## Risks of waiting

A spalled or cracked brick takes in water. On veneer, that water should drain to the weep holes, but each freeze-thaw cycle breaks the damaged brick further and can spread to its neighbours. Broken brick at garage piers and corners can loosen and fall. Replacing a handful of bricks now is far cheaper than rebuilding a pier or a section of wall later.

## How we replace brick in New Lenox

1. **Diagnose the cause.** Salt, splash, impact, a rusting lintel or blocked weeps. We fix the cause so the new brick lasts.
2. **Find the match.** We pull a sample, compare current blends, look for leftover or salvaged brick and show you the options against the wall.
3. **Cut out damaged units** carefully, removing mortar around each brick without disturbing neighbouring brick, ties or flashing.
4. **Check behind the brick** for wall ties, flashing and weep holes, and correct what we can while the wall is open.
5. **Set the new brick** fully bedded in mortar matched in colour, then tool the joints to the existing profile.
6. **Clean and review** the area with you once the mortar has set.

## Materials we use

Matched face brick from current, leftover or salvaged stock; **Type N** mortar coloured to match the existing joints; corrosion-resistant wall ties where old ties have failed; and new flashing and weep vents where we find them missing. We do not use grey bagged mortar for coloured joints.
