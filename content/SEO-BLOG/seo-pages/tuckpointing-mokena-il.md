## The Four Places Newer Veneer Fails First

### 1. The chimney
Exposed on four sides above the roofline, with no heated interior drying it. **It ages faster than every wall on the house**, and on a great many Mokena homes it is the only masonry that genuinely needs repointing.

### 2. Under gutter seams
A leaking or overflowing gutter section delivers concentrated water down one vertical band of wall, every storm, for years. Damage shows up as a stripe rather than a spread.

### 3. The base course at grade
Splash-back, snow banking against the wall for weeks, and winter road salt keep the lowest courses saturated far longer than anything above them.

### 4. The shaded elevation
North and east faces dry slowly, so they enter every freeze-thaw cycle wetter than the sunny side. One elevation fails while the other three look new.

**Sound joints on the front of the house tell you nothing about those four locations.**

## What Tuckpointing Does

Mortar is engineered as the **sacrificial** part of a masonry wall — deliberately softer than the brick, so it absorbs movement and moisture stress and wears out first. Replacing mortar is cheap; replacing brick is not.

**Repointing is the structural half: grinding or raking failed mortar out to a sound depth, then packing fresh mortar in layers.** Tuckpointing, as most homeowners use the term, includes tooling that joint so the repair matches the profile the house was built with.

Once joints open, water runs behind the brick face instead of off it. In a Will County winter it freezes there, expands about nine percent, and works the wall apart from the inside.

## Veneer: What Is Behind Your Brick

This matters for how the repair is done. Brick veneer is a **single decorative layer over a framed or block backup**, with an air cavity behind it. It is designed to leak: water gets past the brick, runs down the back face, hits **through-wall flashing**, and exits through **weep holes** in the bottom course.

That system works until a detail fails — and it fails badly if a repointing crew **mortars the weep holes shut** because they look like missing joints. That converts a drained wall into a bucket. We leave weeps open and verify they are draining before finishing.

The other hidden component is the **ties** — the metal anchors holding the brick skin to the building. They are steel, they corrode when the cavity stays wet, and you cannot see them. That is the real argument for repointing early rather than waiting.

## Signs Your Mokena Home Needs Repointing

- **Mortar you can dig out with a screwdriver.**
- **Receded joints** sitting behind the brick face.
- **Sandy grit** on sills, steps and along the foundation.
- **A vertical band of damage** under one gutter section.
- **Efflorescence** — chalky white bloom — which on veneer usually means water is not draining properly.
- **Blocked or missing weep holes** at the base course.
- **Spalling brick faces**, flaking or popping.
- **Rust staining** below a window — a corroding steel lintel.
- **An open-jointed chimney**, the fastest-failing masonry on the property.
- **Step cracks** through joints at corners, often from clay soil movement.

## Our Process

1. **Free on-site assessment** — every elevation, plus the chimney, gutters, downspouts, grade, sills, lintels, flashing and weep holes.
2. **Mortar matching** — color, sand and, critically, hardness. **Type N** for most above-grade veneer, **Type S** where conditions warrant.
3. **Grinding and raking** to a sound depth, typically two to two and a half times the joint width. Never skimmed over.
4. **Cleaning the joints** so new mortar bonds to clean masonry.
5. **Repointing in layers**, packed and compacted with no voids behind the face.
6. **Tooling** to match the original joint profile.
7. **Weep hole verification** — confirmed open and draining.
8. **Proper cure**, protected as conditions require.
