## Steel That Grows

Brick cannot span an opening on its own. Every window, door and garage opening in a masonry wall has something carrying the brick above it, and in Bridgeview's post-war housing that something is almost always a **steel angle lintel** — an L-shaped steel bar bearing on the masonry at each side.

Steel is entirely reliable until it gets wet. Then it corrodes, and corroding steel does something inconvenient: **rust occupies several times the volume of the metal it replaced.**

Inside a rigid brick wall there is nowhere for that expansion to go. So it goes upward and outward, pushing the brick courses above the opening apart. That is **rust jacking**, and it is why a lintel failure presents as a horizontal crack along the joint line above a window rather than as anything obviously metallic.

**The staining you see is rust escaping. The problem is the rust that stayed inside.**

## The Progression

1. **Water gets in** — through failed mortar joints, a failed sill, cracked caulking at the window perimeter, or a gutter overflowing above.
2. **The steel starts corroding**, hidden inside the wall.
3. **Rust staining appears** on the brick — the first visible sign, and the cheapest moment to act.
4. **The joint above the opening cracks** horizontally as expansion begins lifting brick.
5. **Brick displaces** — courses lift, mortar fails, units loosen.
6. **Section loss in the steel** means the lintel is no longer reliably carrying its load.

The whole sequence is driven by water, which is why lintel repair that does not also fix the water source has a countdown on it.

## Signs Your Bridgeview Home Needs Lintel Work

## Why Bridgeview Specifically

Bridgeview's housing went up largely between the 1950s and 1970s — **brick ranches, Cape Cods, split-levels, bi-levels and Georgians**, with a high proportion of attached brick garages on compact Cook County lots. That era used steel angle lintels essentially everywhere, and **that steel is now sixty to seventy years old.**

Two features of the local stock concentrate the problem:

**Wide mid-century openings.** Post-war design favored the **picture window** and the two-car garage. Both are long spans, both load the steel heavily, and both are more sensitive to corrosion-driven deflection than a narrow bedroom window.

**Low openings.** Garage-door lintels and basement-level openings sit close to grade, where splash-back and **road-salt spray** keep them wetter than anything higher on the wall. Salt accelerates corrosion considerably.

Add Cook County freeze-thaw — dozens of crossings of the freezing point each winter — and the water reaching the steel does its damage on an annual cycle.

## Our Process

1. **Free on-site assessment** of every opening, identifying which lintels are corroding and where water is entering.
2. **Fix the water source** — joints, sill, caulking, gutters — because otherwise the new steel starts the same clock.
3. **Temporary support** of the masonry above the opening.
4. **Careful removal** of the brick courses over the lintel, salvaging sound units for reuse.
5. **Remove or treat the steel.** Corroded steel is replaced; sound steel is cleaned, treated and primed.
6. **Install new lintel** with proper bearing at each end and appropriate protective coating.
7. **Rebuild the brickwork** with salvaged and matched units, repointed with mortar matched for hardness, color and sand.
8. **Seal and finish** — new backer rod and sealant at perimeters, joints tooled to the original profile.

## The Cost of Waiting

Lintel corrosion does not pause. Every winter the steel expands a little more, the crack opens a little wider, and more water reaches the steel through the crack the steel just made. What starts as staining on one window becomes cracked brick on three, and eventually becomes loose masonry above a doorway people walk through daily.

There is also a compounding effect worth knowing: **the same water rusting your lintels is working on your mortar joints and brick faces at the same time.** Lintel staining is frequently the most visible symptom of a wall-wide water problem.
