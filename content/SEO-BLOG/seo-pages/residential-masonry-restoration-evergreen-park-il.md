For a mason, that is enormously useful information. It means **the masonry problem on your house is almost certainly the same problem four doors down**, arriving at the same time for the same reason. Diagnosis gets faster, pricing gets more honest, and a homeowner can sanity-check what they are being told simply by walking the block.

> If three houses on your street have a cracked limestone sill under the same window, you are not looking at three accidents. You are looking at one builder's detail reaching the end of its service life across an entire subdivision.

## The five details that fail on this stock

### 1. Limestone and cast sills

Post-war builders used relatively thin limestone or cast sills under windows, often set with minimal slope and no drip. Water sits on them, enters the joint at each end, freezes, and cracks the sill through — usually in the same place on every house on the block. Left long enough, the water goes past the sill and into the wall below the window.

### 2. Steel lintels over openings

Above windows, doors and garage openings sits an exposed steel angle. Unprotected steel corrodes, and corroding steel **expands** — lifting the courses above it and opening a horizontal crack that looks exactly like tired mortar. The tell is an **orange rust stain** streaking down from the opening. That is a repair to make early, because the alternative is rebuilding the masonry the steel has displaced.

### 3. The bottom three courses

The base of the wall is the last part to dry and the part exposed to splashing rain, piled snow and de-icing salt. It is where spalling shows first on almost every Evergreen Park house. It is also the condition most often blamed on the brick when the cause is water management — grade sloping toward the house, a downspout discharging at the foundation, a walk that has settled back toward the wall. [Why the bottom three courses of brick fail first](/blog/why-the-bottom-three-courses-of-brick-fail-first) and [gutters, downspouts and grading](/blog/gutters-downspouts-grading-brick-damage-chicagoland) cover it.

### 4. Front porches, stoops and steps

A post-war brick porch is a separate small structure with its own slab, its own cap and its own joints, and it takes more water and more salt than any wall on the house. Porch and stoop masonry is frequently the worst masonry on an otherwise sound home.

### 5. The chimney

Highest, wettest, most freeze-thaw-exposed masonry on the house, and on a 1950s home usually topped with a thin mortar wash rather than a proper sloped crown. [Why the top of your chimney fails first](/blog/why-the-top-of-your-chimney-fails-first) covers what to look for.

## Restoration means the right order, not everything

Most Evergreen Park homeowners are quoted a whole-house number and reasonably decide to do nothing. The better approach is to separate **causes** from **symptoms**:

| Priority | Work | Why |
|---|---|---|
| First | Anything letting water in — failed joints on weather elevations, cracked sills, chimney crown | Everything else is downstream of this |
| First | Corroding lintels with active rust staining | The damage compounds and gets structural |
| Second | Spalled brick replacement where faces are gone through | Cannot be repointed; needs replacing |
| Second | Porch, stoop and step repair where it is a trip or water hazard | Safety and water together |
| Later | Sound-but-tired joints on sheltered elevations | Real work, not urgent work |
| Last | Cosmetic matching and cleaning | Do it once the wall is sound |
| Only after the above | Breathable sealing | Sealing a leaking wall traps the water in |

## Mortar, matching and the thing to insist on

On 1950s Evergreen Park brick the governing rule still applies: **the mortar has to be softer than the brick it holds**, so movement and moisture relieve through the replaceable joint rather than spalling the brick face. On this stock that generally means a moderate mix rather than the hardest one available — and it means the mix gets specified to the wall, not chosen out of habit.

Matching matters on a street of near-identical houses, because a mismatched repointing is visible from the sidewalk. We identify the existing mortar's colour and sand texture, tool the joint to the existing profile, and on a prominent elevation cure a small sample before committing to the wall. Replacement brick gets matched by unit, colour and texture — [brick matching and replacement material](/blog/brick-matching-replacement-material) covers how.
