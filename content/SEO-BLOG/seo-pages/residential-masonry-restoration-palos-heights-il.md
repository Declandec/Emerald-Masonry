## Restoration vs. a Repair

**A repair fixes one thing.** Restoration scopes the entire masonry envelope at once, identifies everything failing or about to, and sequences the work so water sources get fixed before the damage they caused.

Done properly, restoration usually costs **less across five years** than four separate repairs — setup and access get paid for once instead of four times, and repairs stop being undone by problems nobody looked at.

## The Order That Protects the House

If you take one thing from this page, take the sequence.

**1. Anything actively letting water in.** Chimney crown and flashing, failed sills, open mortar joints on the weather-beaten elevation, failed caulking at window perimeters.

**2. Corroding steel.** Rust staining below a window means a **steel lintel** is corroding and expanding inside the wall. It worsens every winter and eventually lifts the brick above the opening.

**3. Damage the water already caused.** Spalled brick, deteriorated joints, failed sills — real repairs, but pointless before steps 1 and 2.

**4. Protection.** Breathable sealing where warranted, after joints are sound.

**5. Cosmetic.** Matching, cleaning, appearance.

**Phasing by what is most visible is the expensive way.** The chimney crown you cannot see from the driveway is often what is causing the spalled brick you can.

## Signs You Need a Full Assessment

- **Rust staining** running down from a window, door or garage opening.
- **A horizontal crack** in the joint directly above an opening.
- **Spalling brick faces** — flaking, popping or crumbling.
- **Mortar you can rake out** with a screwdriver, or joints receded behind the brick.
- **Green or black film** on a shaded elevation — a moisture signal worth following.
- **White efflorescence** on the brick.
- **A cracked chimney crown**, rusted or tar-patched flashing, staining on the stack.
- **Water stains inside** near exterior walls or the chimney chase.
- **A vertical band of damage** under one gutter section.
- **More than one of the above** — the actual signal you need a scope, not a single repair.

## Mortar Matching

**The governing rule is that mortar must be softer than the brick it holds**, so joints take the stress and the brick does not. Palos Heights's post-war brick is denser and machine-made, laid with harder **Portland-based mortar**, which usually means **Type N** for above-grade repointing and **Type S** where conditions warrant. A mix too soft erodes early; a mix too hard spalls the faces. We match hardness first, then color, sand and joint profile.
