## What Foundation Masonry Repair Covers

Foundation masonry repair is the repair and restoration of the **masonry foundation itself** — the brick, concrete block (CMU), or stone that carries the house, and the mortar that holds it together. On a typical Berwyn home that work includes:

- **Repointing** deteriorated mortar joints in the exposed foundation course, grinding out the failed mortar and packing in fresh, matched mortar.
- **Replacing spalled or crumbling foundation brick and block** that has lost its face or gone soft.
- **Parging** — troweling a protective mortar coat over the exposed foundation face to smooth it, seal it, and help it shed water.
- **Rebuilding** a section of foundation wall that has deteriorated past the point of spot repair.
- **Sealing the transition** where the foundation meets the brick veneer above, a common entry point for water.

This is masonry work, not poured-concrete crack injection or piering. If your problem is a cracked poured-concrete wall or active sinking, that's a different trade — and we'll tell you so rather than sell you the wrong fix.

## Signs Your Berwyn Foundation Needs Masonry Repair

Most foundation masonry problems show themselves at or just above ground level. Walk the perimeter of your Berwyn home and look for:

- **Crumbling or missing mortar** at the base of the walls — joints you can rake out with a screwdriver or a finger.
- **Spalling, flaking, or powdering foundation brick or block**, often worst on the shaded or downspout side.
- **White, chalky efflorescence** on the foundation or the inside of the basement wall — a sign water is moving through the masonry.
- **A damp, musty basement**, peeling paint on the inside of the foundation wall, or water stains after a hard rain.
- **Step cracks** running through the mortar joints, or gaps opening where the foundation meets grade.
- **Bowing or bulging** in a basement wall — this one is more serious and belongs in the honest-distinction section below.

Quotable rule of thumb: **when the lowest mortar joints on your house turn to sand, water has already found its way in — the repair only gets bigger the longer it waits.**

## Why Berwyn Foundations Fail Faster

Berwyn's housing stock is largely a century old, and that history is written into its foundations. The original mortar on many of these homes is **soft lime mortar**, which is far less water-resistant than modern mixes and washes out quickly once it's exposed at grade. Add the specific conditions this part of Cook County deals with:

- **Freeze-thaw at grade** — water soaks the lowest masonry, freezes, expands, and pops the mortar and brick face apart, cycle after cycle.
- **Downspouts and grading** dumping roof water right against the foundation on narrow bungalow lots where there's little room to move it away.
- **Clay soil** that swells when wet and shrinks when dry, working against the base of the wall.
- **Road salt and de-icer** splashing onto foundations near driveways and sidewalks over the winter.

Because Berwyn's bungalows and two-flats sit shoulder-to-shoulder, water that isn't managed has nowhere to go but down and into the masonry.

## Masonry Repair vs. Structural Settlement — An Honest Distinction

Not every foundation problem is a masonry problem, and we won't pretend otherwise. **Deteriorated mortar, spalling brick, efflorescence, and a damp basement are masonry and moisture issues — that's our work, and we repair them directly.** But if a wall is actively **bowing, leaning, sliding inward, or opening cracks that keep getting wider**, that points to structural movement — soil pressure or settlement — which should be evaluated by a **structural engineer** before any masonry goes back. We're glad to look, tell you plainly what we see, and coordinate the masonry restoration once the structural side is addressed. That honesty is part of why homeowners and property managers keep calling us back.

## Our Foundation Masonry Repair Process

1. **Free on-site inspection.** We look at the foundation inside and out, identify what's failing and why, and separate masonry repair from any structural concern.
2. **Expose and prep.** Where the foundation is below grade, we dig only as much as the repair needs to reach sound masonry.
3. **Grind and rake** the failed mortar joints back to solid material without damaging the surrounding units.
4. **Replace failed units** — cut out spalled brick or block and set in matched replacements.
5. **Repoint** with color- and strength-matched mortar, tooled to match the existing joints.
6. **Parge and protect** the exposed face where appropriate, and address grading, downspouts, or the veneer transition driving the water.
7. **Clean up and backfill**, leaving the foundation sound and shedding water.

## Materials We Use

We match mortar to the age and hardness of your foundation — using softer, lime-rich mixes on older bungalows so the mortar stays sacrificial and doesn't destroy the surrounding brick, and appropriate Type N or Type S mortar where the wall calls for it. Replacement brick and block are sourced to match size, color, and texture, and we use breathable parge coats and sealers that let a masonry foundation dry rather than trapping moisture inside it.
