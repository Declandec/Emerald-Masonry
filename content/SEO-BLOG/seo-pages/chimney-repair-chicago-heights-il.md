## What chimney repair actually covers

"Chimney repair" is a catch-all, so here's what we mean by it on a real Chicago Heights home:

- **Chimney tuckpointing** — grinding out failed mortar joints and repacking them with fresh, color-matched mortar. This is the most common fix and stops water from getting behind the brick.
- **Above-roofline rebuilds** — when the brick at the top has crumbled, leaned, or lost its structure, we dismantle down to the last sound course and rebuild it with new brick and mortar.
- **Crown repair** — the concrete cap on top of the chimney is the first thing to fail. We patch or recast a properly sloped crown so water sheds away instead of pooling.
- **Flashing repair** — the metal-and-seal joint where the chimney meets the roof is a frequent leak source. We re-seal or replace it.
- **Spalled and falling brick** — we replace individual faces that have popped, flaked, or fallen.
- **Chimney caps** — installing a cap keeps rain, animals, and debris out of the flue.

## Signs your Chicago Heights chimney needs repair

You don't need to climb on the roof to spot trouble. From the ground or your attic, watch for:

- **White staining (efflorescence)** streaking down the brick — that's mineral salt left behind by water moving through the masonry.
- **Crumbling, sandy, or missing mortar** between the bricks.
- **Flaking or spalled brick** — faces that have popped off, leaving rough, pitted surfaces.
- **A leaning or bowing chimney**, or daylight visible between the chimney and the house.
- **Pieces of brick or mortar** found in the yard, on the roof, or in the gutters.
- **Water stains** on ceilings or walls near the chimney chase, or a damp smell in the firebox.

> If you can see white staining and crumbling mortar at the same time, water is already moving through your chimney — that's the point to call before freeze-thaw turns a repoint into a rebuild.

## The risks of waiting

A chimney problem rarely fixes itself. Water that gets behind failing mortar freezes, expands, and pries the brick apart a little more with every cold snap — which is exactly why south-suburb chimneys decline fastest in winter. Left alone, a small repointing job becomes a full above-roofline rebuild, and a cracked crown lets water into the flue and the wall framing below it. A leaning chimney is a safety issue: bricks can fall onto the roof, the driveway, or a walkway. Catching it early is almost always the cheaper and safer path.

We never quote an exact price sight-unseen, because honest pricing depends on the chimney. The main factors:
