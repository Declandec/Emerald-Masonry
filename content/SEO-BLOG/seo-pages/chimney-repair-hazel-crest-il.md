## What chimney repair covers

"Chimney repair" is really a handful of related repairs, and most chimneys need two or three of them at once:

### Chimney tuckpointing
Over the decades, mortar joints erode faster than the brick around them. [Chimney tuckpointing](/services/tuckpointing) grinds out the failed mortar and packs in fresh, color-matched mortar so the chimney sheds water again. It is the single most common — and most cost-effective — chimney repair we do in Hazel Crest.

### Above-roofline rebuilds
When the brick above the roofline is leaning, loose, or the mortar has washed out over a large area, tuckpointing alone won't hold. We take the chimney down to a sound course and rebuild it brick by brick. A proper [chimney repair](/services/chimney-repair) rebuild restores both the structure and the draft.

### Crown repair
The crown is the concrete or mortar cap at the very top. Cracks here let water run straight down inside the chimney. We rebuild or reseal the crown with a proper wash and overhang so rain drips clear of the brick.

### Flashing repair
Flashing is the metal seal where the chimney meets the roof. Failed or improperly installed flashing is one of the most common causes of "chimney leaks" that are actually roof-line leaks. We reseal or replace it so the joint is watertight.

### Spalled brick replacement
When water freezes inside a brick, the face pops off — that's spalling. We cut out spalled units and swap in matching [brick repair](/services/brick-repair) so the chimney is solid and uniform again.

### Chimney caps
A cap keeps rain, animals, and debris out of the flue. We install stainless or galvanized caps sized to your flue.

## Warning signs your Hazel Crest chimney needs attention

Catching these early is the difference between a day of tuckpointing and a full rebuild:

- **Efflorescence (white staining)** — powdery white residue means water is moving through the masonry.
- **Crumbling or missing mortar** — open joints let water in and weaken the stack.
- **A leaning or bowing chimney** — a structural red flag; do not wait on this one.
- **Falling brick or brick pieces in the yard** — spalling and mortar failure in progress.
- **Water stains inside** — near the fireplace, ceiling, or attic around the chimney chase.
- **A rusted or cracked crown/cap** — the first line of defense has failed.

> If you can pull a chunk of mortar out with your fingers or you're finding brick on the ground, your chimney has already crossed from "monitor" into "repair now."

## The risk of waiting

Chimney problems compound. A hairline crack in the crown lets in water; that water freezes and thaws through a Chicago-area winter; the freeze-thaw cycle pops brick faces and washes out mortar; the weakened stack starts to lean. What began as a $-modest crown reseal becomes an above-roofline rebuild — and, in the worst cases, a safety hazard over a walkway or driveway. Water that gets past a failed chimney also travels into ceilings, framing, and the firebox. Repairing early is almost always the cheaper path.
