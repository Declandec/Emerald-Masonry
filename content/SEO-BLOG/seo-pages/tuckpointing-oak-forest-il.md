> On a brick veneer house, the mortar is only half of the weatherproofing. The other half is the drainage behind the brick: flashing and weep holes. Tuckpointing that ignores the drainage can make a leak worse.

## Why tuckpointing in Oak Forest is different: most homes are brick veneer

Oak Forest grew mostly after World War II, with blocks of brick ranches, then raised ranches and split-levels through the 1960s and 70s, and newer homes after that. Almost all of them are built the same way: a wood-frame wall with a single layer of face brick in front of it and a small air gap between.

That design expects some water to get through the brick. It runs down the back of the brick, hits a strip of **flashing** at the bottom of the wall, and drains out through **weep holes**, the small open slots you can see in the bottom mortar joint just above the foundation, and above windows and the garage door.

Solid masonry homes, like the older bungalows closer to Chicago, work differently. There the mortar is the main defence. On an Oak Forest veneer home, it is one part of a system.

| | Brick veneer (most Oak Forest homes) | Solid masonry (older Chicago-area homes) |
|---|---|---|
| **Wall build** | One brick layer, air gap, wood frame | Two or more brick layers bonded together |
| **How water leaves** | Flashing and weep holes at the base and over openings | Through the mortar and brick faces as they dry |
| **Common leak cause** | Blocked weeps, failed flashing, open joints | Failed mortar joints, cracked brick |
| **Tuckpointing risk** | Filling weep holes shut | Using mortar harder than the brick |

## Signs your Oak Forest home needs tuckpointing

- **Recessed mortar** a quarter-inch or more behind the brick face, often on the west and south walls first.
- **Sandy joints** that scrape out with a key.
- **Stair-step cracks** at the corners of picture windows and garage openings.
- **Loose brick** on the brick piers beside the garage door and on rowlock window sills (the row of bricks set on edge under a window).
- **Open or loose chimney joints** above the roofline.
- **Weep holes packed with mortar**, caulk or mulch. That is not a tuckpointing sign, but it needs fixing at the same visit.

## Signs it's a drainage problem, not a mortar problem

- Water stains on drywall at the **bottom** of a wall or below a window, while the mortar outside looks sound.
- **White efflorescence** in a band along the lowest courses.
- Landscape beds or raised soil **covering the weep holes**.
- Rust staining at the steel lintel over the garage door.

If those are what you see, repointing the whole house will not stop the leak. Opening the weeps, correcting the grade or repairing flashing will.

## How we tuckpoint brick veneer homes in Oak Forest

1. **Find the water first.** We check weep holes, grade, gutters and flashing at the base, windows and garage before recommending repointing.
2. **Match the mortar.** We record the colour, sand and joint profile and make cured sample patches for your approval.
3. **Cut joints to depth** without chipping brick edges, at least twice the joint width.
4. **Repoint with Type N mortar** matched to the brick. Most brick from this era is hard and dense, so Type N suits it.
5. **Keep every weep hole open.** We clear any filled by earlier work and never fill the bottom joint solid.
6. **Tool joints** to the original profile so repaired walls blend in.
7. **Clean and cure**, protecting new mortar from fast drying and frost.
