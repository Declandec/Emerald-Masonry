## The Three Foundation Types We Repair in Joliet

| Foundation type | Typical Joliet era | How it fails | Usual repair |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Limestone / rubble stone** | Pre-1940 | Mortar joints erode and wash out; stones shift | Repointing, resetting displaced stone, parging |
| **Brick** | Late 1800s–1930s | Soft brick spalls at grade; joints deteriorate | Repointing, brick replacement, drainage correction |
| **Concrete block (CMU)** | Post-war to present | Step cracks, bowing, joint failure, face spalling | Repointing, block replacement, parging, crack repair |

Each of these fails differently and needs a different repair, and the most common mistake we see is a one-size-fits-all patch applied over a wall whose actual problem was never diagnosed.

## Signs Your Joliet Foundation Needs Masonry Repair

- **Mortar washing or crumbling out from between foundation stones or blocks** — often visible as gaps you can see daylight or soil through.
- **Stair-step cracking** climbing through the mortar joints of a block or brick foundation.
- **Horizontal cracking or bowing** in a block wall, which indicates lateral soil pressure and always warrants evaluation.
- **Spalling and flaking brick** in the first few courses above grade, where splash-back keeps the masonry wet.
- **Parging falling off in sheets**, exposing the wall behind it.
- **Water entering the basement** at the wall–floor joint or through visible joints.
- **Efflorescence** — white powdery deposits on interior basement walls.
- **Damp or musty basement air** with no obvious plumbing source.
- **Displaced or dropped stones** in a rubble foundation, or a visible bulge in the wall line.

## Why Joliet Foundations Deteriorate

Three forces do most of the damage here.

**Water at grade.** The base of a foundation is the wettest masonry on any property. Rain splashes back off hard surfaces, downspouts discharge beside the wall, grade slopes toward the house instead of away, and the soil holds moisture against the masonry for days. Mortar in constant contact with wet soil erodes.

**Freeze-thaw.** Will County runs through dozens of freeze-thaw cycles each winter with no lake buffer. Water inside a saturated joint expands as it freezes and works the mortar apart from within — the same mechanism that destroys chimneys, concentrated at the wettest part of the building.

**Soil movement.** Joliet's clay-heavy soils swell when wet and shrink when dry. That seasonal movement puts lateral pressure on foundation walls, which is why block walls in this area develop horizontal cracks and bowing, and why older stone foundations shift and open at joints.

Add a century of service and it becomes clear why the mortar goes long before the stone does.

## Our Foundation Masonry Repair Process

1. **Free on-site inspection.** We evaluate the foundation inside and out, read the crack patterns, check grading, downspouts and drainage, and determine whether the wall is deteriorating or actually moving.
2. **Diagnosis before scope.** Water management problems get identified up front — repairing masonry while leaving the water source in place guarantees a repeat.
3. **Joint preparation.** Failed mortar is raked and cleaned out to sound material.
4. **Repointing.** Joints are packed with mortar matched to the wall — a softer, more forgiving mix on old limestone and soft brick, an appropriate structural mix on block.
5. **Resetting and rebuilding.** Displaced stones are reset; failed brick or block is replaced with matched units; deteriorated sections are rebuilt.
6. **Parging and protection.** The exposed portion of the wall is parged where appropriate to shed water and protect the masonry.
7. **Drainage recommendations.** Grade, downspout extensions and splash control — the low-cost measures that protect the repair for decades.

## Materials We Use

Mortars matched to the wall type and era — **lime-modified mixes for old limestone and soft brick, Type N and Type S** where structurally appropriate; matched **replacement brick and concrete block**; parging and cementitious coatings; and salvaged or matched stone for rebuilding rubble sections. On limestone foundations we deliberately avoid hard, brittle mixes: mortar that is stronger than the surrounding stone concentrates stress at the stone faces instead of at the joint, which is exactly backwards.

## What Happens If You Wait

Foundation masonry is the one place where deferral compounds fastest, because the damage is driven by water that keeps arriving whether you address it or not. Open joints let water into the wall and into the basement. Saturated masonry spalls and loses section. Loss of section in a bearing wall becomes a structural problem rather than a maintenance one — and structural repairs on a foundation are an order of magnitude more disruptive and more expensive than repointing was.
