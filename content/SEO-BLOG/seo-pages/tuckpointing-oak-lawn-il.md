## What Tuckpointing and Repointing Actually Do

Mortar is designed to be the sacrificial part of a brick wall. It is meant to be softer than the brick so that as the wall moves, absorbs moisture and freezes, the mortar takes the damage and the brick does not. That is by design — mortar is far cheaper and far easier to replace than brick.

**Repointing is the structural half of the job: grinding or raking the deteriorated mortar out to a sound depth, then packing fresh mortar into the joint in layers.** Tuckpointing, in the way most Oak Lawn homeowners use the word, includes finishing and tooling that joint so the repair matches the profile the house was built with. Emerald Masonry does both as a single operation — a sound repair with a finish that disappears into the wall.

When those joints are left open, water runs behind the brick face. In a Chicagoland winter it freezes there, expands roughly nine percent, and pushes the wall apart from the inside. That is how a hundred-dollar joint problem becomes a brick replacement problem.

## Signs Your Oak Lawn Home Needs Tuckpointing

- **Mortar you can dig out with a screwdriver.** The screwdriver test is the fastest honest diagnostic there is. Sound mortar resists a firm push; failed mortar gives way like packed sand.
- **Receded joints.** If the mortar sits noticeably behind the face of the brick, it has already lost depth — and depth is what sheds water.
- **Sandy grit on sills, steps and the foundation line.** That grit is your mortar, and it is leaving the wall.
- **Step cracks** running diagonally through the joints, often near a corner, a window, or the garage.
- **Efflorescence** — that chalky white bloom on the brick — which means water is moving through the wall and depositing salts on the way out.
- **Spalling or flaking brick faces**, which usually means water has already been getting in for years.
- **A chimney with open joints or a cracked crown**, common on Oak Lawn's mid-century chimneys, which are exposed on all four sides and weather faster than any wall on the house.

## Why Oak Lawn's Post-War Brick Needs a Different Mortar

This is the point almost every competing page skips, and it is the difference between a repair that lasts and a repair that damages your house.

Pre-1920 Chicago-area masonry — the bungalows and two-flats of Berwyn, Cicero and the city's Bungalow Belt — was laid with soft, lime-rich mortar and softer, often locally made brick. Repointing those walls with a hard modern Portland mix traps stress in the brick, and the brick faces pop off within a few freeze-thaw seasons.

Oak Lawn's stock is mostly the opposite problem. Homes built here in the 1950s and 60s were laid with **harder, Portland-based mortar and denser, machine-made face brick**. A mix that is too soft on those walls will erode out of the joints early; a mix that is correctly matched will wear in step with the brick around it for decades. Matching means matching three things:

| What we match | Why it matters on an Oak Lawn wall |
|---|---|
| **Hardness / mix type** (Type N vs. Type S) | Mortar must be no harder than the brick, or the brick takes the stress and spalls |
| **Color and sand** | Post-war mortar color varies house to house; the sand is half the color |
| **Joint profile** | Concave, weathered or raked — the tooling is what makes a repair invisible |

A wall repointed with the wrong mix does not fail politely. It fails as spalled brick faces, which is a far more expensive repair than the joints were.

## Our Tuckpointing Process in Oak Lawn

1. **Free on-site assessment.** We walk every elevation, test joints, check the chimney, sills, lintels and grade line, and identify where water is actually entering.
2. **Mortar matching.** We match color, sand and hardness to your existing mortar — critical on Oak Lawn's post-war mixes.
3. **Grinding and raking.** Failed mortar is cut out to a sound depth, typically two to two and a half times the joint width, not skimmed over.
4. **Cleaning the joints.** Dust and loose material are removed so the new mortar actually bonds.
5. **Repointing in layers.** Mortar is packed in and compacted so there are no voids behind the face.
6. **Tooling.** Joints are struck to match the original profile while the mortar is at the right firmness.
7. **Cleaning and curing.** The wall is brushed down and the mortar is protected and allowed to cure properly — rushing this stage is a common cause of early failure.

## What Drives the Cost of Tuckpointing in Oak Lawn

We do not quote tuckpointing by the square foot sight unseen, and you should be skeptical of anyone who does. The real drivers are:

- **Extent** — spot repointing one elevation versus a full wrap of the house and garage.
- **Height and access** — ground-level work versus a two-story elevation, a chimney above the roofline, or anything needing scaffolding or a lift.
- **Existing mortar hardness** — post-war Portland mortar takes longer to grind out than soft lime mortar.
- **Matching difficulty** — an unusual brick color or sand blend takes more work to match.
- **Additional damage found** — spalled brick, a rusting lintel, a failed chimney crown or bad sills discovered during the walk.
- **Season and cure conditions** — cold-weather work requires protection and adds time.
