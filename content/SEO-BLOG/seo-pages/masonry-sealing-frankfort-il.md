## What Masonry Sealing Actually Is

Brick and mortar are porous by design. They absorb some water, then dry out — that cycle is normal and healthy. **Masonry sealing** applies a penetrating, breathable water-repellent that soaks into the surface and dramatically reduces how much liquid water the masonry drinks in, while still letting water vapor escape from inside the wall. The wall sheds rain better but can still dry.

That "still dries" part is everything. The right sealer is a **silane/siloxane-type penetrating repellent** — it does not form a skin on the surface. What you must never put on brick in a freeze-thaw climate is a **film-forming coating or waterproof paint**: those seal moisture *inside* the wall, and when that trapped water freezes and expands, it pops the face off the brick. In Frankfort's winters, film-forming products don't protect brick — they destroy it.

## Sealing Is Not a Substitute for Repair

Here is the point Emerald leads with on every estimate, because it saves homeowners money: **a water-repellent cannot waterproof failing masonry.** If your mortar joints are open, your brick is cracked, or your flashing has failed, water walks straight past any sealer through those paths. Seal over the top and you've done nothing but trap moisture and hide the problem while it gets worse.

So the correct order is always: **repair first, seal second.** If a wall needs [tuckpointing](/services/tuckpointing) to refill deteriorated mortar joints or [brick repair](/services/brick-repair) to address spalled or cracked units, that comes first. Only once the masonry is sound does sealing add value. When we look at your home, we'll tell you honestly whether it needs repairs, sealing, both, or neither — we won't sell you a coat of sealer over a wall that needs repointing.

## When Sealing Genuinely Helps in Frankfort

Frankfort is full of large 1990s–2010s custom and subdivision homes with big brick-veneer and stone-veneer facades, tall chimneys, and exposed elevations — plus the older, historic buildings around the Frankfort Historic District downtown. On the newer homes especially, the brick is usually still sound, which makes them ideal candidates for protective sealing. Masonry sealing earns its keep when:

- **Sound brick is soaking up water** — you see the wall darken noticeably and stay wet long after rain, especially on south- and west-facing elevations that take the weather.
- **Efflorescence keeps blooming** — the chalky white residue means water is moving through the masonry; on sound walls, a breathable repellent reduces the absorption feeding it.
- **A chimney takes a beating** — chimneys are exposed on every side above the roof and benefit from repellent once the crown, cap, and joints are sound. Pair sealing with [chimney work](/services/chimney-repair) where needed.
- **You've just had the wall repointed** — sealing is a smart finishing step *after* tuckpointing, not a replacement for it.

## Signs a Frankfort Home May Benefit

Walk your elevations after a rain. If a wall drinks in water and darkens deeply while a sheltered wall stays light, that thirsty wall is a sealing candidate — assuming the mortar and brick are sound. Persistent efflorescence, minor damp patches on otherwise intact walls, and heavy weather exposure on a prominent front elevation all point the same way. What sealing does **not** address — and what we'll flag instead — is active leaking through cracks, open joints, failed sills, or bad flashing. Those need repair, and [caulking](/services/caulking) at the right joints, not a repellent.
